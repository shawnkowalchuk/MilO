## Truck pairing and trip detection

**Status:** In progress (phase 1): built, and not yet run against a real truck. On the phone only a trip started with the button has been recorded (2026-10-05); no truck is paired there. The pairing screen has been drawn only on an emulator with no Bluetooth device to list (2026-10-06) · **Platforms:** Android · **Last updated:** 2026-10-06

**What it does**
Starts a trip automatically when the phone connects to the truck over Bluetooth and ends it after the truck disconnects. This is the most important capability in the app: the mileage app Shawn uses today often fails to start trips even when the phone is clearly connected, so **reliability is the number one requirement**.

**Required behaviour**
- Onboarding: Shawn picks the truck from the phone's paired Bluetooth devices. The app associates it with `CompanionDeviceManager` and observes device presence, so the system wakes the app on connect and disconnect even if it has been closed.
- Backup trigger: a manifest-registered receiver for `BluetoothDevice.ACTION_ACL_CONNECTED` and `ACTION_ACL_DISCONNECTED`, filtered to the truck's address.
- On connect: start a foreground service (type `location`) with an ongoing "Trip in progress" notification and begin GPS recording.
- On disconnect: wait a grace period (default 2 minutes, configurable) before ending the trip. Reconnecting inside the grace period continues the same trip.
- If Android Auto is still connected (`androidx.car.app` `CarConnection`), do not end the trip. Shawn sometimes uses Android Auto over a USB cable.
- **A trip runs from Bluetooth connect to disconnect plus grace, and nothing else splits it.** Decided at kickoff: a long stop with the engine running stays inside one trip. Stops are not cut into separate sessions.

**How it works today**
Designed in `docs/adr/ADR-002-trip-detection.md`. First pairing, then what tells MilO about the truck, then the path a trigger takes, then the rules.

*Pairing*
- **The pairing screen** (`feature/pairing/`) is opened from the Setup screen's truck row, and from the Settings screen's "Change truck" (or "Pair truck") button. Its Back arrow leads back to whichever of the two it was opened from. It shows three cards. "Your truck": the stored truck and, in plain words, whether Android is watching for it, followed by how the attempt just made went. "Before you can pick the truck", only when something is in the way. "Paired with this phone": the phone's paired Bluetooth devices, named, each with its own button (Pair; Pair again on the stored truck; Use this one on another device once a truck is stored).
- **What can be in the way,** one at a time and in this order: Nearby devices is not allowed (the card's button asks for it with Android's dialog); the phone has no Bluetooth; Bluetooth is switched off (a button to the Bluetooth settings); location is switched off (a button to the Location settings; the devices are listed but their buttons are greyed out, because Android needs location on to make the association); nothing is paired with the phone (the Bluetooth settings again). The screen reads the phone again every time it comes to the front, which includes the quick settings panel closing over it (see [Design system](#design-system), `CameToFrontEffect`). It reads once more when Android reports that Bluetooth has finished switching on or off (`bluetoothSwitchChanges`): switching on takes the phone a second or two, and Shawn is back on the screen sooner than that.
- **Picking a device** calls `TruckPairing.associate` with the screen's Activity. Android hands back its consent dialog, which the screen shows once (the ViewModel remembers which one it showed, so a rotation does not show it twice), and the dialog's result goes to `TruckPairing.onConsentResult`. The screen then says one of: "Paired with (name)" and, above it, that Android is watching for the truck; "Not paired. Android's dialog was closed without allowing. Nothing was changed."; or "Pairing failed", with `TruckPairing`'s own reason word for word and what to try. "Not paired" is shown only when Shawn himself closed the dialog (result 0) or pressed "Don't allow" (result 1). From Android 13 the dialog can also end because Android gave up looking for the device or failed inside; every such result is "Pairing failed" (`consentWasDeclined` in `PairingUiState.kt`).
- **Changing the truck** is picking another device. `TruckPairing` stores the new truck and removes every other association, the old truck's among them.
- What the screen shows is decided by one pure function, `pairingUiState`, from the phone's device list, whether location is on, the stored truck, `TruckPairing.status` and `TruckPairing.progress`. `progress` belongs to the whole app and outlives the screen, so the result of an attempt made on an earlier visit is not shown.
- `TruckPairing` is what the screen calls: `pairedDevices()` lists the phone's paired Bluetooth devices; `associate(device, activity)` asks Android for a companion device association with the chosen one (an address filter, single-device mode, no device profile: the one combination that finds an already-paired device without a scan); Android answers with a consent dialog for the screen to launch, delivered through `progress`; `onConsentResult` takes the dialog's result.
- When the association exists, the truck's address (in capitals), name and association id are stored in the settings, every other association MilO holds is removed, and Android is asked to observe the truck: from then on it binds MilO's companion service whenever the truck connects. The trip controller is then asked to read the truck, because Android does not always report a truck that is already connected.
- `check()` runs at every process start and every time MilO comes to the front. It compares the stored truck with the associations Android lists and asks Android again to observe (asking twice changes nothing). The result is one of: armed, no truck, association missing (it was removed in the phone's settings), not supported (a truck is stored, but the phone has no companion device support), failed. It is logged as a `PAIRING` line each time. While no truck is armed the line also lists the phone's paired devices, and says so if the phone has no companion device support.
- An association with no truck stored for it is adopted as the truck, if it is the only one and its device is paired with the phone. That is how a truck can still be paired over adb if the screen fails: the association is made over adb (`docs/DEVICE_TEST_CHECKLIST.md`). An association for an address that is not among the phone's paired devices is turned down, and the `PAIRING` line says so.
- The same adoption replaces a stored truck whose own association is gone. Over adb it is the way back from a wrong truck, and the way to pair another one if the screen fails: remove the association, make the right one, open MilO. A stored truck that still has its association is never replaced by adoption.

*What tells MilO about the truck*
- **The Bluetooth receiver** (`TruckBluetoothReceiver`, in the manifest and exported) hears four broadcasts for every Bluetooth device: the link going up, the link going down, and the hands-free and the audio profile changing state. It looks the truck up in the settings and compares addresses. For another device it writes one "Ignored" line to the event log and does nothing else. For the truck: the classic link going up is a "link connected" trigger and going down a "disconnected" trigger, both trusted as they stand; a profile reaching "connected" or "disconnected", and a low-energy link to the truck's address, are prompts to read the truck's connection.
- **The same receiver, registered by the trip service** for as long as a trip is recorded, so that a disconnect is heard even if the manifest receiver is not served. During a trip each broadcast therefore arrives twice, and the event log shows both.
- **The companion service** (`TruckCompanionService`). Android binds it when the truck connects, starting MilO's process if it has to. "Appeared" is a trigger that starts a trip which must be confirmed (rule 12); "disappeared" is a "disconnected" trigger. Android has had three shapes of this callback (Android 12; 13 to 15; 16 and later) and newer versions also send the older ones, so each shape acts only on the versions for which it is the newest. Every time the service is created it also prompts a reading, because Android rebinds it after MilO's process died without repeating "appeared".
- **The reconcile receiver** (`TruckReconcileReceiver`): the phone has booted, or MilO was updated. Both prompt a reading.
- **Process start and MilO coming to the front** prompt a reading too (`MiloApplication`, `MainActivity.onStart`).
- A manifest receiver keeps its broadcast open until the controller has dealt with the trigger (8 seconds at most), so the process is not frozen between `onReceive` returning and the truck being read.

*Reading the truck* (`TruckConnectionSource`, implemented by `BluetoothTruckConnection`)
- The answer is connected, not connected or unknown, with a few words on how it was reached. Those words are part of the event-log line of the trigger that asked.
- On this phone (and up to Android 16) there is no way to ask whether a device is connected. MilO asks the hands-free profile and the audio profile which devices they are connected to, each through a proxy fetched for that one reading and handed back after it. One profile listing the truck is enough. "Not connected" needs both to say so.
- From Android 16 QPR2 it asks the link itself (`BluetoothDevice.isConnected`).
- Unknown: the Bluetooth permission is missing, the settings cannot be read, or a profile gave no answer within 3 seconds while the phone says some device is connected on it. Unknown is never taken for connected. A reconcile or a once-a-minute reading that comes back unknown changes nothing; a timer or a button goes by what was already believed; a process start counts it as "not connected".
- No truck paired, no Bluetooth on the phone, or Bluetooth switched off: not connected.

*The path of a trigger*
- Everything that can prompt a trip calls one function, `TripController.onTrigger(trigger, source)`. The callers are the Bluetooth receiver, the companion service, the reconcile receiver, `MiloApplication` (a reconcile at every process start), `MainActivity` (a reconcile every time MilO comes to the front), `HomeViewModel` (the Start and End buttons), the Android Auto screen (its one button, `platform/car/TripStatusScreen`), `TruckPairing` (a truck was just stored) and the trip service (its timers).
- The controller puts the trigger in an inbox. One coroutine (`TripWorker`) works the inbox off in order, so triggers on different threads cannot interleave.
- For each trigger the worker reads the truck's connection if the trigger calls for it (`TripEvidence`), asks the trip rules what should happen, and then: writes a line to the event log with the source and the state before and after; writes each effect to storage (`TripLedger`) and logs it; and only then tells the service and the screens.
- **The service comes first.** If the outcome is an open trip and the trip service is not in the foreground, nothing is stored. The controller runs the preflight (precise location, "Allow all the time", location switched on, battery not "Restricted", Nearby devices allowed) and asks Android for the service, with the trigger in the start intent. The service's first statement is `startForeground`; once that has succeeded it hands the trigger back, and the trip is opened. A connect or a press of Start asks for the service straight from the thread the trigger fired on, without waiting for the worker, because Android's allowance for such a start lasts seconds.
- **If the start fails** at the preflight or in Android, no trip row is opened. The reason goes to the event log, the home screen shows it, and a high-importance notification says "MilO could not start this trip. Tap to start". The tap starts the service directly. Which failures this warning covers, and which it cannot, is set out under [Diagnostics and reminders](#diagnostics-and-reminders), "If the truck connects and a trip fails to start".
- **If the service is lost in mid-trip** (Android destroyed it while the process lives), the worker asks for it again. If Android refuses, the worker drops the trip it holds in memory: the home screen stops showing a trip in progress and shows why. The trip row stays open in storage, and the next trigger picks it up through the restart rules below, which close it if it has gone stale.
- When the last trip closes, the worker tells the service to stop. A service that was started for nothing (the hold-off was set, or the event was a duplicate) is stopped the same way.
- After the process was killed, the first trigger makes the worker read the open trip and its stored fixes and apply the restart rules below. `START_STICKY` has Android create the service again by itself, which counts as a reconcile. The service has `stopWithTask="false"`, so swiping MilO out of the recent apps does not end a trip.

*The rules*
1. `TripStateMachine.step(state, event, rules)` takes the current state and one event and returns the new state plus a list of effects (start a trip, mark the truck seen, start or cancel the grace period, end the trip, set or release the hold-off). It touches nothing itself: the controller carries the effects out.
2. Events state what is true, not what just happened: "the truck is connected", "Android Auto is not connected", "Start was pressed and the truck is not connected", "End was pressed", "the truck moved". The same event twice changes nothing, so dropped, repeated and reversed Bluetooth events cannot corrupt a trip.
3. After every event the rules settle the trip against what is known:
   - idle and the truck connected: a trip starts, unless the hold-off is set;
   - a trip open and the truck or Android Auto connected: it carries on, and a running grace period is cancelled;
   - neither connected: the grace period starts (once; a repeated disconnect does not move the deadline), and when it runs out the trip ends;
   - a manual trip the truck never joined: it ends after 30 minutes without movement.
4. Every event carries the time, but a trip whose time has run out is closed only by an event that brings a fresh reading of the truck: a connect or disconnect, the timer's own reading, or a press of Start or End. A GPS fix or an Android Auto change closes nothing, because what the rules believe about the truck may be out of date. `TripStateMachine.nextCheckAtMs` says when a timer is needed. When it fires, or when that time is already past, the caller reads the truck's connection and sends it as an event, which is how "check again before closing" is done.
5. A reconnect continues the same trip only inside the grace period, plus 30 seconds to allow for a timer that fires late. After that the trip is over, whatever connects: it is closed where the truck was found gone, and a connected truck starts a new trip in the same step.
6. After a process restart, `TripStateMachine.restore` rebuilds the state from the open trip row, the stored hold-off, the time of the trip's newest stored point and a fresh reading of both connections, then applies the same rules. A trip that was recording, and whose newest point is more than 30 minutes old, is closed at that point first: nobody watched the truck in between.
7. Start pressed while a trip is waiting out its grace period, with the truck still gone, ends that trip where the truck was found gone and starts a manual trip.
8. An ended trip is cut at the moment the truck was found gone (or the last movement, for the no-movement rule). `TripClosing.close` drops the stored points recorded after that moment and works out the end time, the distance and the start and end positions from the rest, and marks a trip under the minimum distance `DISCARDED`.
9. `TripRepository` stores the trip. Each write matches one effect and is safe to repeat. Starting a trip while one is open returns the open one.
10. **The hold-off ends** on whichever of three things comes first: a reading that shows the truck disconnected; a link-level connect event (the ACL connect broadcast or the companion "appeared" callback) more than 60 seconds after End was pressed; or 12 hours, applied at the next reading of the truck. The time of the End press is stored with the hold-off.
11. **A lost disconnect is caught by a reading once a minute.** While a trip is open the service prompts the controller every 60 seconds to read the truck's connection. "Connected" is always believed. "Not connected", when the rules believe the truck connected, is believed only the second time in a row; the first is logged and otherwise ignored. Any other trigger in between starts the count again. If the minute timer is late (the phone slept), a GPS fix that arrives 70 seconds or more after the last reading prompts it, and a timer that then fires within 30 seconds of that reading is skipped (`PollPacer`), so the readings stay about a minute apart.

    **A reading of "not connected" counts only once a reading has seen the truck.** A trip that rests on a link-level connect event is not doubted by a reading until some reading has shown the truck connected on that link. This holds for the minute reading and for a reconcile alike (MilO opened, the companion service created, a profile broadcast). Such a reading is logged with "No reading has shown the truck connected since its link was made, so this proves nothing" and changes nothing. A new link event starts this again. A disconnect event is always trusted.

12. **The companion "appeared" callback starts a trip that must be confirmed.** When idle it opens a trip at once, with the truck not yet marked as seen and a deadline 15 seconds on. A link-level connect event or a reading of "connected" confirms it, and it is an ordinary truck trip from then on. At the deadline the controller reads the truck; if it is still not connected (or cannot be read) the trip ends as a false start: no grace period, and it is discarded whatever distance the phone covered. When a trip is already open the callback changes nothing about the trip; all it can do there is release a hold-off that is more than 60 seconds old. The trip-start sound waits for the confirmation.
13. **Android Auto holds a trip open, but not for ever.** The service watches `CarConnection` from the moment recording begins and reports every change. While Android Auto is believed connected, the service makes the library ask again once a minute. Once Android Auto alone has held a trip open for 12 hours it is taken for a stuck value and no longer believed, until it has been seen to report "not connected".

- Tests: `app/src/test/.../core/trip/TripStateMachine*Test.kt` (the rules in ADR-002's order, how a grace period ends, the buttons, the hold-off, the companion start, awkward orderings, random sequences, restarts), `TripClosingTest.kt`, `LostDisconnectDetectorTest.kt`, `PollPacerTest.kt`, `AndroidAutoHoldGuardTest.kt`; `app/src/test/.../platform/trip/TripController*Test.kt`, which run the controller against stand-ins for storage, the truck and the service (`TripControllerReadingTest.kt` for the triggers and for a truck that cannot be read, `TripControllerPollTest.kt` for which readings of "not connected" count, `TripControllerRestartTest.kt` for a lost process, a lost service and failing storage); `app/src/test/.../platform/bluetooth/`: `TruckSignalsTest.kt`, `TruckTest.kt`, `BluetoothTruckConnectionTest.kt` (what is made of the two profiles' answers), `TruckPairingTest.kt` and `TruckPairingCheckTest.kt` (against a stand-in for Android's companion device manager, in `TruckPairingFakes.kt`) and `BluetoothSwitchTest.kt`; and `app/src/test/.../feature/pairing/PairingUiStateTest.kt` (what the pairing screen shows for each thing the phone and `TruckPairing` can report, and which results of the consent dialog count as declined). **Depends on**

**Edge cases & gotchas**
- Phone reboots or the app is updated while the truck is already connected: no connect broadcast fires, so the existing connection has to be detected another way.
- With HyperOS Autostart off and MilO not running, decompiled system code shows the connect broadcast is dropped and the system's attempts to start MilO's services are rejected. Autostart is off by default for a sideloaded app. This is unconfirmed on Shawn's phone until tested, but Autostart is treated as mandatory. See `docs/research/2026-10-03-miui-background-limits.md`.
- After a reboot, Android holds Bluetooth events until the phone has been unlocked once, so a trip begun before the first unlock starts recording at unlock.
- Swiping MilO away in recents, the clear-all button and Xiaomi's cleaners can kill it while it waits for the truck. Force stop leaves it unable to start until Shawn opens it again.
- Ending a trip by hand while the truck is still connected holds automatic start off. Pressing End with no trip open and the truck connected does the same, and pressing End again restarts the hold-off from that press. It ends as rule 10 says. What is left of the old risk: if the disconnect and the next connect event are both missed, the trip after that starts only when something reads the truck as connected 12 hours or more after the End press.
- A connect event within 60 seconds of the End press does not release the hold-off, even if the truck really did drop and reconnect in that minute. The disconnect itself releases it if it is seen.
- A lost disconnect is noticed between one and two minutes late (rule 11), and the grace period starts then. The trip is cut at that later moment, so up to two minutes of walking away from the truck can be counted. With the phone asleep and no GPS fixes arriving, the readings come later still, and so does the cut.
- **The once-a-minute reading is only as good as the check behind it, and on this phone the check has never run.** It looks at the hands-free and the audio profile. A truck that is connected on neither reads as "not connected" for the whole drive. The second half of rule 11 keeps that from ending the trip: the readings are logged every minute and change nothing. The price on such a truck: a disconnect that is never reported is not caught by a reading, so the trip then stays open until End is pressed or the process dies (the restart rules close it). And a process that restarts in mid-drive finds the truck "not connected" and ends the trip after the grace period. Device check 32 is the test. The reading through the two profiles is the one piece of the automatic part that ran nowhere, not even on the emulator, whose Android version asks the link instead.
- In the first moments of any trip a link event started, before a reading has shown the truck, a lost disconnect is not caught either (rule 11). On an ordinary truck that is the few seconds until a profile connects.
- A truck's connection that cannot be read ("unknown") never ends a trip that is recording, and never starts one. The price: while every reading is unknown, a disconnect that was never reported is not noticed either. Each unknown reading is a line in the event log.
- At a process start an unknown reading counts as "not connected". A trip that was recording then starts its grace period (a later reading can still cancel it), and a hold-off is released as if the truck had been seen gone.
- A broadcast for the truck is recognised by its address alone. If the settings cannot be read within a second, the receiver cannot tell whose broadcast it is: it then asks for a reading of the truck in place of trusting the event, so a link connect does not start the trip at once.
- The receiver writes a line for every Bluetooth device that connects or disconnects (earbuds, a watch), with its address, marked "Ignored". They prove that the broadcast arrived, which is what the first weeks on the phone are about; they also add to a log that nothing trims yet.
- The companion "disappeared" callback is trusted as a disconnect. Should Android send one while the truck is still connected, the trip goes into its grace period and the next reading (within a minute) cancels it. With no trip open it releases a hold-off, and the truck then starts a trip at the next reading.
- A hands-free or audio profile that connects while the hold-off is set does not release it and does not start a trip: it is not a new link. A profile that drops is another matter. Its broadcast prompts a reading, and if it was the only profile connected (the usual case with Android Auto on a cable, where only hands-free is up), the reading says "not connected". That releases the hold-off, and the profile coming back starts a trip, although the link never dropped and Shawn never left the truck. Left that way on purpose: any reading of "not connected" releases the hold-off, because a missed trip is worse than an unwanted restart (ADR-002, amendment 24). Device check 47 shows whether this truck does it.
- On Android 15 and 16, asking Android to observe a truck that is already connected binds the companion service but sends no "appeared". Seen on the emulator. The reading taken each time the companion service is created covers it; this phone runs Android 14, which does send it.
- Over adb, another truck is paired the way the first is: remove the stored truck's association, make one for the new truck, and open MilO; the lone association is adopted in place of the stored truck. While the stored truck still has its association, adoption replaces nothing. The pairing screen has no such limit.
- On a phone that does not report companion device support there is no association to adopt, and the `PAIRING` line says so. Only the pairing screen can store a truck there: it stores it without an association, so that the Bluetooth receiver knows which device to listen for. Whether HyperOS reports the feature is not known until tried.
- **Android's consent dialog has never been shown by any build of MilO.** The pairing screen has been drawn only on an emulator that has no Bluetooth device to list (2026-10-06, opened from Settings), so no device has ever been picked on it. Device checks 67 to 72 are the first time.
- The pairing screen cannot remove the truck without pairing another. Nothing asked for that.
- Changing the truck while a trip is being recorded is not prevented. The trip controller is told that the truck changed and reads the new truck's connection, and the trip rules go by the new truck from then on. What that does to a trip the old truck was holding open has not been tried.
- If Android never answers a request to pair, the screen stays on "Waiting for Android" and no device can be picked. Leaving the pairing screen and opening it again lets Shawn pick again.
- The pairing screen's Back arrow closes that screen and nothing else, however often it is tapped while the screen slides away (`closeIfOnTop`). Before, a second tap closed Setup too and a third crashed MilO.
- The receiver that hears Bluetooth being switched on or off is registered only while the pairing screen is on the back stack. It has never received a broadcast: device check 70.
- A failed pairing is shown with `TruckPairing`'s reason as it is written for the event log, in English and with the exception's name where there is one. It is exact, not pretty.
- Pairing a truck again makes a second association on some Android versions. MilO keeps the one with the highest id and removes the others.
- On Android 12 the association is not reported through a callback, only through the result of the consent dialog. If Android has not listed it by the time MilO looks, pairing fails with "lists no association" and has to be repeated. Never seen; this phone runs Android 14.
- The timers of a trip (grace period, confirmation, the minute) are coroutines in the service and can fire late while the phone sleeps. A GPS fix stands in for a late timer: for a deadline once it is 10 seconds overdue, for the minute reading 70 seconds after the last one. The overdue deadline is dealt with before that fix is counted, so the first fix after a long silence closes a forgotten manual trip where it last moved; it does not count as new movement. With no fixes arriving (indoors) a trip can stay open, and the service running, past its grace period until the phone next wakes. A trip that runs out of grace is still cut at the right moment; only its closing is late. A disconnect that was never reported is different: the trip is cut when the second reading is taken, so late readings mean a late cut.
- The process killed (or frozen) while a trip is open: the next morning's connect does not join yesterday's trip. A trip in grace is closed by its stored deadline, restart or not. After a restart, a trip that was recording is closed at its newest point if that is more than 30 minutes old; up to 30 minutes it carries on, and the gap is counted as a straight line. A restart also closes a trip that stored no point for 30 minutes for any other reason, such as location switched off. A process that stays alive through a lost disconnect relies on the once-a-minute reading (rule 11).
- The 30 seconds and the 30 minutes are constants in `TripState.kt` (`LATE_CHECK_TOLERANCE_MS`, `RESTART_GAP_LIMIT_MS`). They were chosen in review without Shawn and are his to change (ADR-002, Amendments).
- Start pressed during the grace period: what was driven between the truck being found gone and the press belongs to neither trip.
- The grace deadline, the 30-minute limit and the moment a trip is cut at are times of day. If the phone corrects its clock by more than the time left, a grace period lasts longer or shorter than set, and a clock set back during the grace period lets the walk away from the truck be counted. Rare; logged as `[DEBT]` in FINDINGS_LOG.
- Android Auto holds a trip open in every case: it stops the grace period from starting, cancels one that is running, and pauses the no-movement rule for a manual trip. It never starts a trip.
- What the phone records during the grace period (Shawn walking away from the truck) is stored but is not part of the trip if the truck stays away. If the truck comes back, it is.
- The no-movement rule ends a manual trip where it last moved, not 30 minutes later.
- A companion start is not confirmed by Android Auto being connected, only by the truck's own connection (ADR-002, amendment 7, taken literally).
- If the process dies in the 15 seconds a companion start waits to be confirmed, the deadline is worked out again from the stored trip (started by the truck, truck never seen) and the same rule applies at the restart.
- If Android refuses the service, the trigger is not retried. The notification is the way back: a tap starts a manual trip, or picks up the trip a killed process left open.
- The tap starts the trip service directly. On stock Android that is allowed even from a dead process. On HyperOS with Autostart off, the research expects a service start in a dead app to be rejected; the tap would then do nothing and leave no line in the log. Untested: device check 19a. If it fails there, the tap has to open MilO and let the visible screen start the trip.
- The preflight asks for "Allow all the time" and for Nearby devices even for a press of Start with the app open. Until both are granted, no trip can be recorded at all.
- **A pairing does not travel.** After a restore by Android, and after an import of an export file, the truck is stored as paired only if Android on this phone holds its association; otherwise its address and name are kept and the pairing check reports "paired before, pair it again" ([Backup, export and import](#backup-export-and-import)). Nothing in the pairing code was changed for that: the check has always asked Android.
- An unreadable settings file does not stop trips: the controller logs it once and runs with the defaults (2-minute grace, 300 m minimum, no hold-off).
- The grace period and the minimum distance are Shawn's to set on the [Settings](#settings) screen. Nothing tells the trip controller about a change: it reads both at every trigger, so the stored value is the one in force from the next event on. A grace period that is already running keeps the deadline it was started with.
- If storage fails in the middle of an event, the controller logs the failure, forgets what it held in memory, and reads the state from storage again at once: a service that came up for a trip which could not be stored is stopped, and a trip that is still open in storage carries on. The fix or the event that failed is lost. If the hold-off could not be stored after End, the truck starts a new trip straight away. If storage fails that second time too, nothing more is tried until the next trigger.

## GPS recording and distance

**Status:** In progress (phase 1): fixes are recorded during a trip, and the trip's distance and its start and end coordinates are stored when it closes. The start and end addresses are looked up afterwards and stored with the trip (run on an emulator, never on the phone) · **Platforms:** Android · **Last updated:** 2026-10-06

**What it does**
Records the truck's position during a trip and turns it into a distance in km, with start and finish addresses.

**Required behaviour**
- Record a GPS fix every 5 seconds using `FusedLocationProviderClient`, and count distance only after 10 m of real movement. Shawn's brief said "every 5 seconds or 10 m"; the location API cannot express "or" (its interval and distance settings combine as "and"), so the 10 m rule is applied in MilO's own distance calculation.
- Discard points with accuracy worse than 25 m, and impossible jumps (implied speed over 180 km/h).
- Store the raw points so distance can be recalculated later.
- Discard trips under 0.3 km (configurable), for example moving the truck around the yard.
- Reverse-geocode the start and end points to street addresses and store them with the trip. Start and finish locations and mileage are tracked automatically; there is no odometer entry anywhere in the app.

**How it works today**
*Recording.* When the controller tells the trip service that a trip is open, the service starts `LocationRecorder`, which asks the fused location provider for a fix every 5 seconds at high accuracy: no minimum distance, never faster than 5 seconds, no batching, and no cached position (the first fix must be a new one). Each fix arrives in a `LocationCallback` on the main thread and is handed to the controller, which stores it in `raw_points` under the open trip and adds it to the running distance (`TripProgress`). The home screen and the notification show that running distance. The recorder also writes to the event log when fixes are requested and stopped, how long the first fix took and how accurate it was, and when location has been unavailable for 10 seconds and when it comes back. The fixes stop when the trip closes.

*Closing.* When the trip rules end a trip, the controller reads the trip's stored fixes back, cuts off those recorded after the truck was found gone, and works out the distance, the end time and the start and end positions from the rest (`TripClosing`). It stores them on the trip and marks the trip `FINISHED`, or `DISCARDED` if it is under the minimum distance or was a false start. The event log gets the outcome, the distance and how many fixes were stored, used and rejected.

*The calculation,* `DistanceCalculator` in `core/trip/`, is the same for the running distance and for the final one. It takes the fixes in the order recorded and applies four rules to each:
1. **Accuracy.** A fix with no accuracy, or an accuracy radius over 25 m, is not used.
2. **Impossible jumps.** A fix that would need more than 180 km/h to reach from the last good fix is not used. The time between them comes from the phone's elapsed-realtime clock, which never jumps. A long gap (a tunnel) is not a jump, so the distance across it is counted as a straight line. If three rejected fixes in a row agree with each other, they are believed instead and counting restarts from them: this is what stops one bad fix, such as a stale first position, from poisoning the rest of the trip. The leap itself is never counted.
3. **Only real movement counts.** Distance is added only once the truck is at least 10 m from where distance was last counted, and at least twice the two fixes' own stated error: 20 m with 5 m fixes, 40 m with 10 m fixes, 100 m with 25 m fixes. What is added is the straight line from where distance was last counted. On a straight road that loses nothing; through a turn it cuts the corner.
4. **Out and back is not a drive.** If the fix after a counted step is nearer to where counting stood before the step than to where the step went, the step was a bad fix and is taken back. This is what keeps one bad fix 200 m away, while the truck stands at a light or sits parked, from adding 400 m.

Distance between two fixes is the haversine great-circle distance, in pure Kotlin so it runs in unit tests. It differs from Android's own ellipsoid calculation by at most about 0.5 %, far less than GPS error over such short steps.

Every fix is stored in `raw_points` whether or not these rules use it, with both clocks, its accuracy and its reported speed, so a trip can be recalculated later with better rules. The thresholds are named constants gathered in `DistanceLimits`.

*Addresses.* `TripAddresses` in `platform/address/` turns the stored start and end positions of a trip into two addresses.
1. **It only watches.** It reads the trip controller's published state and the stored trips, and writes nothing but the four address columns of a finished trip. Ending a trip never waits for it, and a failure in it leaves the trip as it was.
2. **When it looks.** One pass over the trips that are due is made when a trip stops being in progress, at every process start, and each time the Trips screen comes to the front. The pass at process start is asked for through the trip controller (`TripController.whenCaughtUp`), so it runs after the controller has dealt with what the last process left behind: a trip the restart rules close there was never seen in progress by the lookup, and is looked up by that pass. Passes run one at a time; a request made during a pass leads to one more pass after it. There is no scheduled job (WorkManager is not in the build yet): a trip whose lookup failed waits for the next of those occasions.
3. **Which trips are due** (`isDueForLookup` in `AddressRetry.kt`): a `FINISHED` trip that lacks an address for an end whose position is stored, has not used up its attempts, and whose last attempt is long enough ago. **An address that is Shawn's own is never lacking:** one he typed, changed or emptied on the edit screen, and both addresses of a trip he added by hand (see [Trip log](#trip-log-home-day-and-month-views)). The lookup neither asks about such an address nor writes to it, and the update in storage refuses it too, so an answer that was on its way while he saved cannot undo what he typed. A `DISCARDED` trip is never looked up, and neither is a `DELETED` one. A deleted trip that is restored, or a discarded one that Shawn counts after all, is a finished trip again: it is due like any other, and the Trips screen asks for a pass at once. Trips recorded before addresses existed are due like any other, and are caught up by the first pass.
4. **The lookup itself** is Android's `Geocoder` (`GeocoderAddressLookup`, behind the interface `AddressLookup`): no key, a network connection, up to five candidates asked for, 20 seconds waited at most. On Android 13 and later the answer arrives on a listener; on Android 12 the blocking call runs on a background thread, which is interrupted when the 20 seconds are up (`onBlockingThread`; that form has never been run, the phone is Android 14). This is the one place where a position leaves the phone.
5. **One line per address** (`addressLine` in `AddressLine.kt`), in this order of preference: the street and the town, with the house number in front when the geocoder gives one ("10103 104 Avenue Northwest, Edmonton"); the street alone; the town alone (the district, county or province stands in where the geocoder names no town); and last the geocoder's own line with the country and the postal code taken out. A country or a postal code is never shown. Of several candidates the one that says the most is used, and the geocoder's first when two say as much. A house number does not count as saying more: the geocoder lists its best match first, and a numbered candidate further down can be the building next door or one on another street.
6. **No network is not an attempt.** Before a pass asks anything, `NetworkStatus` asks Android whether the phone has a working internet connection. Without one nothing is asked and nothing is counted. The log gets one line, and another only when what is waiting has changed: a trip that starts and ends without a network gets one while its start is waiting and one, "Waiting: 1 finished trip.", once it has ended.
7. **Giving up.** A lookup that leaves an address missing (the geocoder knows none there, or it failed) is counted on the trip. After the first the trip waits at least 2 minutes, after the second 1 hour, after the third 1 day; after the fourth (`MAX_ADDRESS_ATTEMPTS`) it is not asked about again. An address that was found is kept, so a trip can end up with one of the two. A lookup that fails outright also ends the pass: the trips waiting behind it would fail the same way and each lose an attempt.
8. **A trip in progress.** Its row has no position until it closes, so the lookup reads the position of the trip's first usable fix from the trip controller (`CurrentTrip.startLatitude`), asks for its address once, and keeps the answer in memory for the Trips screen (`TripAddresses.openTripStart`). When the trip closes with that same start position, the remembered address is stored without asking again.
9. **The event log,** category `ADDRESS`: one line per lookup of a trip ("Trip 3: start address found; end address none (the geocoder knows no address there). Failed attempt 1 of 4; the next one is at least 2 min away."), one per lookup for a trip in progress, and one when a pass was put off for lack of a network. The lines name no address and no position.

**Where the code lives**
- `platform/address/`: `TripAddresses.kt` (the passes), `AddressRetry.kt` (what is due, when to give up, what a row says about each end), `AddressLine.kt` (the one line), `AddressLookup.kt` and `GeocoderAddressLookup.kt` (the geocoder), `NetworkStatus.kt`, `AddressLogText.kt` (the log lines). `app/MiloApplication.kt` asks for the pass at process start, through `TripController.whenCaughtUp`.
- `data/trip/`: the four address columns on `Trip`, `findTripsLackingAddress` and `recordAddressLookup` on `TripRepository`. The migration that added the columns is `data/MiloMigrations.kt`.
- `platform/trip/LocationRecorder.kt` (the request and the callback); `platform/trip/TripLedger.kt` (stores each fix, closes the trip).
- `core/trip/`: `DistanceCalculator.kt` (the four rules and the thresholds), `Haversine.kt`, `TrackPoint.kt`, `TripProgress.kt` (the running distance and the time of the last movement), `TripClosing.kt` (minimum distance, end time and positions).
- `data/point/`: `RawPoint.kt` (the table), `RawPointDao.kt`, `RawPointRepository.kt`; the database is `data/PointsDatabase.kt`.
- Tests: `app/src/test/.../core/trip/DistanceCalculatorTest.kt`, `DistanceCalculatorParkedTest.kt`, `DistanceCalculatorOutlierTest.kt`, `DistanceCalculatorTurnsTest.kt`, `HaversineTest.kt`, `TripProgressTest.kt`, `TripClosingTest.kt`; for the addresses `app/src/test/.../platform/address/AddressLineTest.kt`, `AddressRetryTest.kt`, `TripAddressesTest.kt`, `TripAddressesWithControllerTest.kt` (the lookup beside a real trip controller), `GeocoderAddressLookupTest.kt` (the time limit around Android 12's blocking call) and `app/src/test/.../data/MiloMigrationsTest.kt`.

**Edge cases & gotchas**
- The distance shown during a trip can be a little higher than the stored one: what is recorded during the grace period counts while the trip is open, and is cut off when it closes.
- A fix's time of day is worked out from the phone's clock and the fix's age, not taken from the fix. The trip rules compare fix times with times read from the phone's clock, and the satellite time inside a fix can differ from it.
- A fix that arrives after the trip has closed is not stored.
- If the location permission is taken away during a trip, Android ends the app; the trip is picked up or closed at the next start.
- A stationary truck with Bluetooth connected still produces GPS jitter, which must not add phantom km.
- The 10 m rule alone does not achieve that. In a simulated hour of parked fixes scattered as a stated accuracy of 5 m implies, 10 m alone added about 360 m, enough to be kept as a trip; with the stated-error rule the same hour adds nothing. So the brief's "10 m" is a floor, not the whole rule. The thresholds are to be tuned from the raw points of the first test drives.
- **The stated-error rule costs distance on turns.** All numbers are simulations, not this phone. On a town route with a right-angle turn every 300 m at about 25 km/h, a fix every 5 seconds alone misses about 2 %. The calculation is short by the same 2 % with 5 m fixes, about 4 % with 10 m fixes and about 11 % with 25 m fixes. A truck circling inside a space smaller than the threshold (a yard, a small roundabout) with fixes of 8 m or worse measures nothing.
- **The end of a trip is short by up to the threshold:** up to 20 m with 5 m fixes, 40 m with 10 m, 100 m with 25 m, because the last stretch never crosses it. A real 350 m trip recorded with poor fixes can therefore come out under the 300 m minimum and be discarded (it is kept as `DISCARDED`, see below).
- **Bad fixes that are still believed** (simulated in review, not seen on this phone). A wrong first fix less than about 700 m from the truth stays as the trip's start position and adds up to its own distance from the truth. One wrong fix straight after a GPS gap is believed if the truck could have got there in the time of the gap, and the way back to the road is counted as well: after a 60-second tunnel, a fix 600 m to the side added 500 m to a 5.3 km drive, and one 2 km to the side added about 1 km. Two bad fixes in a row that agree with each other look like a real there-and-back. Not fixed, because the right cure depends on what this phone's bad fixes look like; the likely one is to hold a fix that follows a gap until the next fix agrees with it.
- Rule 4 has a price: pulling forward and reversing within one fix (shunting in the yard) is not counted.
- A trip under the minimum distance is marked `DISCARDED` and kept with its points, not deleted. If the distance was wrong, the trip is recovered on the Trips screen with "Count this trip" (see [Trip log](#trip-log-home-day-and-month-views)). It is then counted with the distance that was measured; nothing recalculates it, and the edit screen is where a wrong distance is put right by hand.
- After a reboot in the middle of a trip the elapsed-realtime clock restarts, so the distance covered across the reboot cannot be checked and is not counted.
- The phone's own speed reading is stored but not used yet. It is the likely next refinement: for the parked-truck rule in place of stated accuracy (which would end the corner cutting), and to reject a leap from a fix that says it is standing still.
- If the phone's clock was wrong at the start of a trip and corrected during it, the distance is still right (points are cut by stored order, not by time), but the trip's stored start time is the wrong one and can even be later than its end.
- **An address can arrive late.** A trip that ends with no signal, or whose lookup is cut short because the phone puts MilO to sleep once the trip service has stopped, gets its address at the next occasion: the next trip ending, MilO being started, or the Trips screen being opened. Until then the Trips screen says it is still being looked up. The same goes for a trip the restart rules close later than at process start (after the trip service could not be started, or together with the start of a new trip): the lookup never saw it in progress, so it waits for the next occasion, which for a new trip is its first GPS fix.
- **A trip MilO has given up on stays without that address** unless Shawn types one in on the edit screen. Nothing asks again by itself; "Restore recorded values" on an edited trip does start the attempts afresh for an address he had typed.
- **An address is what the geocoder says, not a measurement.** It names the nearest address it knows, which at a large yard or a rural site can be a neighbour's, an "Unnamed Road", or nothing. The stored coordinates remain the record. An address once stored is never replaced by a later lookup; Shawn can replace it on the edit screen, and what he types is kept as he typed it.
- The house number is written before the street, as in Canada, whatever the phone's language.
- The same place can be worded differently on two trips if the first fixes fell on different sides of a lot.
- The start address shown for a trip in progress is held in memory. If the process is restarted in mid-trip it is looked up again.
- A trip discarded as a false start may still have had its start looked up in its first seconds, for the screen. Nothing is stored for it.

## Trip-start sound

**Status:** In progress (phase 1): the sound plays at every trip start, and the Settings screen switches it on and off, plays it, and lets Shawn choose an audio file of his own. Choosing a file ran on an emulator, never on the phone · **Platforms:** Android · **Last updated:** 2026-10-06

**What it does**
Plays a short R2-D2 style droid chirp once, when the app has connected to the truck and is ready to track the trip. It is Shawn's audible confirmation that detection worked.

**Required behaviour**
- Plays once per trip start, at the moment recording has really begun. No sound on trip end.
- Comes from the phone speaker and behaves like a notification sound: silent when the phone is on vibrate or Do Not Disturb.
- Played by the trip service itself, not attached to a notification channel, because MIUI is reported to switch channel sounds off by default.
- The sound committed to the repository is an original synthesized droid-style chirp. The film recording is copyrighted, so it is never committed.
- **Shawn's own clip replaces it in builds made on his Mac.** A file placed at `app/src/debug/res/raw/trip_start_chirp.<ext>` overrides the bundled chirp in debug builds, which are the builds installed on the phone. That folder is git-ignored, so the clip stays on his Mac and his phone and never reaches GitHub. Since 2026-10-05 it holds his R2-D2 clip (4.5 seconds). CI and any other machine build with the synthesized chirp. The event log says "playing the bundled chirp" either way, because it is the sound built into that build.
- A setting to choose any audio file on the phone, without rebuilding. Built on 2026-10-06: see "Choosing a sound" below.

**How it works today**
1. When the controller has opened a trip row, with the service already in the foreground, it tells the service that a trip has just started. That happens once per trip: not when a trip is picked up after a restart, and not when the grace period is cancelled. One exception: a trip opened by the companion "appeared" callback alone is not yet known to be a trip. Its sound plays when the truck's connection is confirmed, and a false start makes no sound at all (`TripTransition.tripReallyBegan`).
2. The service takes down any "could not start this trip" notification, reads the settings, and if the sound is switched on calls `TripStartSound.play`.
3. The sound is played with `MediaPlayer` and notification-type audio attributes, so the phone plays it at the notification volume and mutes it on silent, on vibrate and in Do Not Disturb. The player asks for the phone's built-in speaker as its preferred output.
4. If the settings hold a custom sound, that is played. It is always MilO's own copy of the file Shawn chose, never the file he picked. If it cannot be opened, or fails while preparing or playing, the bundled chirp is played instead and the event log says why.
5. The bundled chirp is `res/raw/trip_start_chirp.wav`: 1.4 seconds of sine sweeps, warbles and short beeps, generated by `tools/make_trip_start_chirp.py`. The script is committed and produces the same file every time; the build does not run it.
6. One sound at a time: a sound that is still playing is cut off when the next one starts. A trip start never meets that; it is there for the Play button below.

*Choosing a sound* (the Settings screen, card "Trip-start sound"; see [Settings](#settings))
1. **A switch,** "Play a sound when a trip starts". Off means no sound at any trip start. It does not change which sound is chosen.
2. **"Sound in use"** says "the built-in sound", or "your own sound" with the name of the file that was picked.
3. **Play** plays the sound a trip start would play now, through the same player, whether or not the switch is on. Pressing it again starts the sound again from the beginning.
4. **"Use my own sound"** opens Android's own file picker, set to audio files. The file Shawn picks is then, in this order:
   - copied into MilO's private storage (`no_backup/trip_sound/`), under a new name, beside the copy in use if there is one. A file over 10 MB, an empty file and a file that cannot be read are refused here. "Cannot be read" includes whatever the app that holds the file answers with: the file is opened by that app's code, and an exception it throws (it no longer knows the file, MilO may no longer read it) is a refusal like any other, never a crash;
   - handed to Android's player to prepare. A file the player cannot prepare (not audio, or damaged) is refused, and its copy is thrown away;
   - written into the settings, as the place of the copy and the name of the picked file;
   - and only then is the copy that was in use before removed. There is one copy at most, however often a sound is chosen.
5. **A refused file changes nothing.** The settings and the copy in use are exactly as before, so the sound that was playing until now still plays. The card says why in one sentence ("MilO could not copy that file", "That file is too large for a trip-start sound (more than 10 MB)", "That file cannot be played as a sound", each ending "The sound was not changed."), and the event log gets an `ERROR` line with what Android or the file system said.
6. **"Use the built-in sound"** is offered while a sound of Shawn's own is in use. It clears the two settings and removes the copy.
7. Closing the file picker without choosing changes nothing and says nothing.
8. A change that has begun is finished even if Shawn leaves the Settings screen in the middle of it.

**Where the code lives**
- `platform/trip/TripStartSound.kt` (the player, and `playbackProblem`, the check that a file can be played); the call at a trip start is in `platform/trip/TripService.kt`.
- `platform/trip/OwnTripSound.kt` (choosing a file and going back, step by step) and `PickedAudio.kt` (reading the picked file through Android's content resolver).
- `data/sound/OwnSoundStore.kt` (MilO's copy: where it is kept, the size limit, one copy at most).
- `feature/settings/` (the card, the file picker, the words for a refusal). `app/AppContainer.kt` builds `ownTripSound` and `soundPreview`, the player behind the Play button.
- The script is `tools/make_trip_start_chirp.py`. The three settings (`sound_enabled`, `custom_sound_uri`, `custom_sound_name`) are in `data/settings/SettingsStore.kt`.
- Tests: `app/src/test/.../platform/trip/OwnTripSoundTest.kt` (a file that cannot be read, cannot be played or is too large changes nothing, and neither does one whose app answers with an exception of its own; a new choice replaces the copy before it; going back) and `app/src/test/.../data/sound/OwnSoundStoreTest.kt` (the copies, against a real folder). The player itself and the file picker have no unit tests: they need Android.

**Edge cases & gotchas**
- **Choosing a sound has run on an emulator only** (FINDINGS_LOG, 2026-10-06): a WAV file was picked, copied, played by the Play button and at the next trip start, and replaced by a second pick; a text file with an audio name was refused. On the phone it is device checks 117 to 123. What HyperOS's own file picker looks like, and whether it offers audio files from the places Shawn keeps them, is not known.
- **The copy is not in any backup,** on purpose: it can be megabytes, and Android's backup takes nothing at all from an app that is over its 25 MB limit. So after a restore by Android the settings name a copy that is not there. Since phase 4, part B, the first start after a restore removes that setting ([Backup, export and import](#backup-export-and-import)): the built-in sound plays, the Settings screen says so, and the sound has to be chosen again on that phone. An export file does not carry the sound either, and an import leaves the phone's own sound as it is.
- "Can be played" means that Android's player can prepare the file. A file with a video track and no sound in it would pass and play nothing.
- A long file is played to its end at every trip start. Nothing limits the length, only the size (10 MB).
- **Play is silent when a trip start would be:** on silent, on vibrate, in Do Not Disturb and in Bedtime mode. The card says so. The event log still gets its line ("Settings screen, Play pressed. Trip-start sound: playing …").
- Play uses a player of its own, not the trip service's. If a trip starts while a sound from Play is still playing, the two overlap.
- In a build made on Shawn's Mac "the built-in sound" is his R2-D2 clip, because that build has it in place of the chirp (above).
- The phone's speaker is a preference, not a guarantee: Android may route the sound elsewhere. Where it comes out with the truck connected is a check on the phone.
- The sound does not lower music that is playing and does not check for a phone call. The research suggests both; neither was asked for.
- **Heard on the POCO X5 once, at a trip started with the button** (2026-10-05, FINDINGS_LOG, "First results from the phone"): Shawn's R2-D2 clip played. Before that he heard nothing, although the log showed the sound being played without error: the phone was in Bedtime mode. Whether HyperOS mutes notification-type audio on vibrate the way stock Android does is unconfirmed (device check 16). A sound chosen on the Settings screen has never been heard on the phone.
- **On Android 17 the chirp of an automatic start would be silent.** MilO targets API 37, and Android 17 lets such an app play audio in the background only from a foreground service that was started while the app was visible or by the user. A service started by a Bluetooth broadcast, the companion callback or boot is not one, and playback then fails without an error: the log would still say "playing the bundled chirp". The POCO X5 runs Android 14 and is not affected. If MilO ever runs on Android 17, the chirp has to become the sound of a notification, which the system plays (FINDINGS_LOG, 2026-10-05).
- If the process dies in the 15 seconds a companion start waits to be confirmed, and the truck is confirmed after the restart, that trip makes no sound.

## Safety net: manual button and driving alert

**Status:** In progress: the button works; the driving alert is built (2026-10-06) and **has never notified Shawn**: its rules run in unit tests, its registration, Setup row and Settings card ran on an emulator, and the build as it stands has had no report of driving, on an emulator or on the phone. Device checks DA-1 to DA-14 · **Platforms:** Android (phone) · **Last updated:** 2026-10-06

**What it does**
Catches the case where Bluetooth never connects at all.

**Required behaviour**
- A Start/Stop button on the home screen starts or ends a trip by hand.
- During scheduled hours, if the phone's driving detection says Shawn is in a moving vehicle and the truck is not connected, notify him with a tap-to-start action. Needs the Physical activity permission.
- Driving detection never starts a trip by itself, because it would also log rides in other vehicles.
- A manually started trip still respects the schedule: it is classified Business or Personal by its start time, the same as an automatic one. (Built on 2026-10-06: the rule that sorts a trip is not told what started it. See [Schedule and Business/Personal](#schedule-and-businesspersonal).)

**How it works today**

*The button.* The home screen has one button. With no trip open it says "Start trip" and sends the `MANUAL_START` trigger; with a trip open it says "End trip" and sends `MANUAL_END`. Both go through `HomeViewModel` to `TripController.onTrigger`, the same function every other trigger calls, so a manual trip is recorded, closed and logged exactly like an automatic one. Each press reads the truck's connection first; if it cannot be read, the press goes by what was last believed. A manual trip the truck never joins ends on End trip, or after 30 minutes without movement.

The [Android Auto screen](#android-auto-screen) has the same button, under the same words, and sends the same two triggers. A trip started there is the same kind of manual trip. In the event log the two are told apart by the source: "Start button" and "End button" on the phone, "Android Auto Start button" and "Android Auto End button" on the car.

*The driving alert* (since 2026-10-06; phone only).
1. **What it is.** A notification, "You seem to be driving" / "No trip is being recorded. Tap to start one.", on a channel of its own ("Driving alert", high importance). **It never starts a trip.** A trip starts only when the notification is tapped. The tap starts the trip service with the `MANUAL_START` trigger, through the same intent the "could not start this trip" notification uses, so the trip is an ordinary manual trip; its `TRIGGER` line has the source "tap on the driving alert". The code that decides about the alert is given neither the trip controller nor the trip storage, so it has no way to start one.
2. **Where the report comes from.** Google Play services' Activity Recognition (the Transition API, part of the location library MilO already uses). MilO asks to be told when the phone enters and leaves a vehicle, and nothing else. The phone says "a vehicle", never which one, and Google gives no figure for how long it takes to notice: other apps report about a minute, sometimes several. The report is delivered to a receiver in the manifest (`DrivingReceiver`), so it can start MilO's process when MilO is not running.
3. **When the alert is shown** (`judgeDriving` in `platform/driving/DrivingAlertRules.kt`, a pure function). All of these must hold:
   - the newest report says the phone has **entered** a vehicle, and it is at most 5 minutes old;
   - the alert is switched on in [Settings](#settings);
   - no trip is open in storage;
   - **a truck is paired** (a truck's address is stored). Without one there is nothing a trip could be for, and every ride in any vehicle during the work hours would alert;
   - a fresh reading of the truck does not say "connected";
   - a trip that started at this moment would be saved as **Business** by the work schedule. That is `classifyTrip`, the rule that sorts a finished trip, so "the work hours" mean the same here as on the Trips screen: on a tracked day, from the stroke of the start time to just before the end time, in the phone's time zone;
   - **no trip ended in the last 5 minutes** (`AFTER_TRIP_QUIET_MS`). The phone takes a minute or more to notice a drive and can report it again, so a report that comes just after a trip is taken to be about the drive that trip recorded. The time is the end time of the newest closed trip as the Trips screen shows it, whatever became of the trip (kept, discarded or deleted). For a trip the truck ended that is its last recorded point up to the moment the truck was found gone, not the end of the grace period, so with the grace period set to 5 minutes or more nothing of the span is left once the trip has closed;
   - **no alert was posted in the last 30 minutes** (`ALERT_QUIET_MS`). The time of the last alert is kept in the settings file, so this holds after MilO's process has been restarted.

   The edges: 4 minutes 59 seconds after a trip's end there is no alert, at 5 minutes there is; 29 minutes 59 seconds after an alert there is no second one, at 30 minutes there is. A stored time that lies ahead of the phone's clock (the clock was set back) silences nothing.
4. **A truck that cannot be read does not stop the alert.** Without the Bluetooth permission, or when Bluetooth gives no answer, the reading is "unknown". The trip rules never start anything on that; the alert is shown on it, because the worst it can cause is one notification, and it is exactly the situation in which no trip starts by itself. That is about a paired truck whose connection cannot be read: **with no truck paired at all there is no alert.**
5. **When it is taken away again:** when it is tapped; when a trip starts being recorded, however it started; when the phone reports having **left** the vehicle; when a later report of entering finds that one of the conditions no longer holds; and when the alert is switched off or the permission is gone. One condition is the exception: a later report of entering that only fails "no alert in the last 30 minutes" changes nothing, so an alert that is still showing stays, and one that has gone is not posted again. It is not taken away by the clock: an alert shown at 16:25 is still there at 16:35 if nothing else happens.
6. **One alert in 30 minutes.** A drive with stops makes the phone report "left" and "entered" again and again, and each "left" takes the alert away; the limit is what keeps each "entered" from sounding it anew. Posted again while it is showing (after the 30 minutes), it makes no second sound. The time is stored when the alert is posted, seen or not; if it cannot be stored, the alert has been posted all the same and the log line says that the next report may bring it up again. A report of entering that is more than 5 minutes old changes nothing either way: nothing promises that a report is fresh, and MilO asks to be told again at every process start. Whether this phone hands over old reports then is device check DA-12.
7. **Asking the phone** (`DrivingAlert.arm`). Reports are asked for while the switch is on and the Physical activity permission is granted, and the request is taken back when the switch goes off. The request is made at every process start, which is what renews it after a reboot and after an update of MilO: both start a new process, and Google does not say that a request outlives either. It is looked at again each time MilO comes to the front (the permission may just have been granted) and when the switch is pressed; the phone is asked only if something has changed since this process last asked.
8. **A report that starts MilO's process** is judged after the trip controller's first look at storage. A trip the last process left open long ago is closed by that look and no longer counts; one that carries on does count, although it is published only seconds later, when the trip service is up again. That is why the stored row is asked and not what the controller publishes. The end of the last trip is read from storage in the same way.
9. **A failure inside the alert stays inside it.** The alert runs in the process the trip service runs in, and a report arrives about a minute into every truck drive, while the trip is being recorded. Each piece of its work (judging a report, asking the phone, taking the notification away for a trip that began) is run so that an exception is written to the event log as one `ERROR` line, "The driving alert failed while …", with its stack trace, and goes no further; the receiver is let go whatever happened. If the event log is what failed, the failure goes to a crash file and reaches the log at a later start (`DrivingFailures`, the pattern of the address lookup).
10. **The event log** has a category of its own, `DRIVING`. One line for every delivery of reports, acted on or not: "Driving detection: entered a vehicle 4 s ago. Alert shown: no trip is being recorded and the truck is not connected", "…No alert: it is outside the work hours", "…No alert: the truck is connected", "…No alert: a trip is being recorded", "…No alert: no truck is paired", "…No alert: a trip ended less than 5 min ago", "…No new alert: the last one was less than 30 min ago", "…The alert is withdrawn if it was showing: the drive is over", "…Not acted on: the report is more than 5 min old and says nothing about now". An alert posted while notifications are off ends "…so nobody saw it". The detail of each line lists every fact the decision went by: the switch, whether a trip is open, whether a truck is paired, the reading of the truck with how it was reached, what the schedule makes of this moment, and how many whole minutes ago the last trip ended and the last alert was posted ("never" if there is none). And one line whenever what MilO asks of the phone changes: "Driving alert: watching for driving (process start)", "Driving alert: not watching for driving (MilO in front): the Physical activity permission is not granted", "…: it is switched off in Settings". A request the phone refuses is an `ERROR` line with the reason, written once and tried again the next time.

**Where the code lives**
- The button: `feature/home/HomeScreen.kt` and `HomeViewModel.kt`; on the car, `platform/car/TripStatusScreen.kt` and `CarAction` in `platform/car/CarScreenContent.kt`. The rules for a manual trip are in `core/trip/` (see [Truck pairing and trip detection](#truck-pairing-and-trip-detection)).
- The alert, in `platform/driving/`: `DrivingAlertRules.kt` (`judgeDriving`, the three spans of time, `vehicleReport`, which reads an event of the phone as "entered" or "left" with its age, `DrivingWatch`), `DrivingAlert.kt` (asks the phone, judges a report, shows and withdraws the notification, stores the time of an alert, writes the log), `DrivingFailures.kt` (keeps a failure of the alert away from the recording), `DrivingDetection.kt` (the request to Play services and its `PendingIntent`), `DrivingReceiver.kt` (the manifest receiver), `DrivingLogText.kt` (the wording of the log lines).
- The two things the alert reads and writes in storage: the end of the newest closed trip (`TripRepository.findNewestTripEndMs`, a read of the `endedAtMs` column) and the time of the last alert (`lastDrivingAlertAtMs` in `data/settings/`).
- The notification and its channel: `platform/trip/TripNotifications.kt` (`showDrivingAlert`, `cancelDrivingAlert`), beside the "could not start" notification whose tap it shares.
- The switch: `feature/settings/SettingsCards.kt` (`DrivingAlertCard`); the stored value is `drivingAlertEnabled` in `data/settings/`. The permission's row: `platform/system/SetupRules.kt` (see [Permission checklist](#permission-checklist)).
- `app/MiloApplication.kt` and `app/MainActivity.kt` call `arm`; `app/AppContainer.kt` builds the alert.
- Tests: `app/src/test/.../platform/driving/`. The rules: `DrivingAlertRulesTest` (every reason for and against, which report counts, the edges of the work hours) and `DrivingAlertLimitsTest` (no truck paired, the 5 and the 30 minutes to the second, how an event of the phone is read). The alert beside a real trip controller on stand-ins: `DrivingAlertReportsTest` (no report ever asks for the trip service or opens a trip; a report waits for the controller's first look at storage), `DrivingAlertQuietTest` (the three limits, across a restart of the process too), `DrivingAlertWatchTest` (what is asked of the phone and when) and `DrivingAlertFailureTest` (a failure is logged and gets no further). `DrivingLogTextTest` for the wording, `DrivingFixtures.kt` for what they share. The Settings switch storing first and telling the alert second is `app/src/test/.../feature/settings/SettingsViewModelTest.kt`.

**Depends on**
Google Play services (Activity Recognition), the Physical activity permission, the notification permission, the work schedule (see [Schedule and Business/Personal](#schedule-and-businesspersonal)), the paired truck and the reading of its connection, and the stored trips (see [Truck pairing and trip detection](#truck-pairing-and-trip-detection)).

**Edge cases & gotchas**
- **This build has had no report.** An emulator cannot be made to report driving, and the phone has not been driven with this build. Everything from "a report arrives" on rests on unit tests and on reading the code: the delivery to the receiver, the notification, its tap. The reviewers of 2026-10-06 did see reports judged, the notification posted and its tap start a trip on an emulator, with the build as it was before their findings were acted on; how the reports were produced there is not recorded (FINDINGS_LOG). Device checks DA-3 to DA-10, DA-13 and DA-14.
- **Nothing happens until a truck is paired.** The phone has none paired today, so the alert stays silent there, and every report is logged as "No alert: no truck is paired" (device check DA-13).
- **A tap on the alert does not run the preflight.** It starts the trip service directly, as a tap on "could not start this trip" does (ADR-002, point 10). A missing location permission is refused by Android and ends in that warning. But with **location switched off for the whole phone** the tap starts a trip that says "Trip in progress" and records nothing; Home's Start button refuses in that state and says why. Seen by a reviewer on an emulator, and left as it is: `[DEBT]` in FINDINGS_LOG, 2026-10-06, with the ways out.
- **Whether a report can start MilO on HyperOS is unknown.** The delivery names MilO's receiver directly, which ADR-002 noted might get past the Autostart gate; the research thinks it likely needs Autostart like every other start of a dead app. Nothing has tested it (ADR-002, amendment 27; device checks DA-8 and DA-9). If it cannot, the alert only works while MilO's process is alive, which is when Bluetooth is least likely to have failed.
- **The alert is late by design.** The phone needs a minute or more of driving to notice, so a trip started from the alert misses the beginning of the drive. It starts when the notification is tapped; nothing is added for the distance already driven.
- **It fires in any vehicle:** a colleague's car, a bus, a taxi during the work hours. That is why it only notifies. The switch in Settings is the way to silence it.
- **In stop-and-go traffic the phone may report leaving and entering a vehicle again.** The "left" takes the alert away, and for 30 minutes after the alert a new "entered" does not bring it back. So after the first stop of a drive the alert may be gone although the drive goes on, and it does not return within the half hour. Device check DA-10 counts what this phone does.
- **The 30 minutes also cover the next drive.** An alert at 10:00 on a ride in a colleague's car means that a drive in the truck at 10:20, on a day Bluetooth fails, brings no alert before 10:30. And the phone reports entering a vehicle once for a drive, so a drive that began inside the 30 minutes gets no alert at all unless the phone reports again. Thirty minutes is what was asked for; the figure is one constant, `ALERT_QUIET_MS`.
- **The 5 minutes after a trip can hide a real drive** that begins within 5 minutes of the last trip's end on a day Bluetooth fails for the second drive and not the first (device check DA-14 is the ordinary case).
- **The alert's sound is the phone's, not MilO's.** It is an ordinary notification on a high-importance channel. MIUI is reported to switch the sound of an app's channels off by default (`docs/research/2026-10-03-miui-dev-bluetooth-audio.md`, finding 33), so on this phone it may arrive silently until the channel is set in the phone's settings (device check DA-5).
- **A report older than 5 minutes is ignored,** which also drops a real one that reaches MilO that late. The limit is a judgement (`MAX_DRIVING_REPORT_AGE_MS`), and every ignored report is in the log with its age.
- **Settings that cannot be read do not silence it:** the alert then runs as it does out of the box (switched on, Monday to Friday 08:00 to 16:30), and the detail of each line says so.
- **A trip that is open but not being recorded counts as a trip.** If Android refused to restart the trip service for a stored trip, no alert is shown; the "could not start this trip" notification is the one that speaks then.
- **The alert does not know about the hold-off.** After End trip with the truck still connected the truck reads "connected", so there is no alert. After End trip on a manual trip without the truck there is none for 5 minutes; after that a report of entering a vehicle shows it, unless the last alert was less than 30 minutes ago.
- **An older build must not be installed over this one once a `DRIVING` line is in the log** (ARCHITECTURE section 6).

## Android Auto screen

**Status:** In progress (phase 1): built, and **never run anywhere**. Not on the truck, not in Google's desktop emulator of a car display (the Desktop Head Unit, which is not installed on the Mac), not on an emulator. It is proven by the build and by unit tests of everything it decides · **Platforms:** Android Auto (projected from the phone) · **Last updated:** 2026-10-06

**What it does**
Shows MilO on the truck's Android Auto display, built with the Car App Library (`androidx.car.app`). Shawn asked for it so he can see more easily that the app is running, and override it without touching the phone. It is in phase 1 because that visibility matters most during the first test drives.

**Required behaviour**
- Shows tracking status, the current trip's km and duration, and today's session count and total km.
- Start Trip and End Trip buttons act as a manual override. They drive the same trip logic as the phone's Start/Stop button, and a trip started here still respects the schedule (it is sorted into Business or Personal when it ends, like any other; see [Schedule and Business/Personal](#schedule-and-businesspersonal)).

**The known risk, before anything else**
Google's documentation says two things. Android Auto's "Unknown sources" developer setting covers media apps, messaging notifications and parked apps, and "doesn't apply to apps built using the Android for Cars App Library". And to test an app in a real vehicle, "you must install it from a trusted source such as Google Play". MilO is installed from Android Studio. So by Google's own words this screen is expected to show in the desktop emulator of a car display and **not on the truck**. Reports from other people conflict, and one unanswered report says sideloaded apps do not show on a POCO X5 Pro. Shawn tests it on the truck (device checks 88 to 100). If MilO is not listed there, there are three choices:
1. **A private Google Play install.** Play's "internal app sharing" or an internal test track delivers MilO to the phone with no review and no public listing, and Android Auto then treats it as trusted. It needs a Play Console account (25 US dollars once, with identity verification). Play signs its copy with its own key, so that copy and a build from Android Studio cannot update each other: changing over means uninstalling, which deletes the trips unless they are exported first. It also ends "never on the Play Store".
2. **A media-app style entry.** "Unknown sources" does allow a sideloaded media app on a real car. MilO would appear as a media app, with the status as its "now playing" text and Start and End as its buttons. A workaround, and not the screen that was asked for.
3. **Dropping the screen.** Everything else works without it: the rule "do not end the trip while Android Auto is connected" does not depend on it, and the phone's notification still shows the trip.
Unofficial tools that pretend an app came from Play exist. They are untrusted software from strangers and the reports on them are mixed, so they are not one of the choices. Source for all of this: `docs/research/2026-10-03-android-auto-screen.md`, findings 8 to 12.

**How it works today**
1. **One screen, never another.** A car allows an app five steps and then closes it. A redraw is a harmless "refresh", and not a step, only while the header's title, the number of rows and every row's title stay exactly the same. So the screen is one pane with the header "MilO" and always three rows, titled **Status**, **This trip** and **Today**. Whatever changes is written under a title, never into it, and nothing is ever opened on top of this screen.
2. **Status** is one line. The first of these that applies is shown:

   | The line | When |
   |---|---|
   | Truck disconnected. Waiting for it to reconnect | A trip is open and in its grace period |
   | Recording. Truck not connected | A trip is open and the trip rules believe the truck's Bluetooth is not connected: a trip started by hand that the truck has not joined, or one that Android Auto alone is holding open |
   | Recording | A trip is open |
   | Could not start: (why) | No trip, and the last attempt to start one was refused. The reason is the first thing the preflight found (location permission, "Allow all the time", location switched off, battery set to Restricted, Nearby devices), or "Android did not allow it" |
   | Not recording. MilO's setup is incomplete | No trip, and a required row of the setup checklist is not in order (the home screen's rule, `needsAttention`) |
   | Not recording. Truck not connected | No trip, and the trip rules believe the truck is not connected |
   | Not recording | No trip, and nothing above applies: ended by hand with the truck still connected, or MilO has only just started |

3. **This trip** shows the kilometres to one decimal and the time since the trip started in whole minutes ("12.4 km · 23 min", or "12.4 km · 1 h 5 min"), and "No trip in progress" when there is none.
4. **Today** shows the finished trips that started today and their total ("3 trips · 41.2 km"), by the one rule the Trips screen and the phone's home screen count by (`todayTrips` and `isCounted` in `data/trip/TripTotals.kt`): a discarded or a deleted trip is not counted, and the trip in progress is not counted until it ends. A trip deleted on the phone leaves the car's total at once, and a restored one comes back. **The kilometres are added up as everywhere else since 2026-10-06:** each trip is rounded to a tenth of a kilometre and the rounded figures are added (`sumOfTenths`), so the row is the sum of the figures the phone lists for the same trips. **The row counts every finished trip, Business and Personal together.** The phone's Today card has shown the two apart since 2026-10-06; the car's row was left as it was on purpose, so its figures are the phone's Business and Personal added up. "No trips yet" before the first; "Not available" until storage has answered, or if it could not be read.
5. **One button.** "Start trip" while no trip is open, "End trip" while one is, the grace period included. It sends `MANUAL_START` or `MANUAL_END` to `TripController.onTrigger`, exactly as the phone's button does. It works while the truck is moving: it is an ordinary button, not one of the library's parked-only ones. A press does what the button said when it was drawn.
6. **Where it reads from.** The screen has no ViewModel and decides nothing. It follows four things: `TripController.activity` (the trip, why the last start failed, and what the trip rules believe about the truck), the trips that started today (`TripRepository`), the shared setup checklist, and the clock. One pure function, `carScreenContent`, turns them into what is shown.
7. **When it redraws.** Only while the car is showing the screen, and only when what is printed has changed: kilometres are compared after rounding to one decimal and the time after rounding down to whole minutes. The running figures of a trip are redrawn at most every 15 seconds. Everything else (the status, the button, a trip starting or ending, today's totals) is drawn at once.
8. **A start that is refused** changes the Status line to "Could not start: (why)", which stays until a trip does start. If it happens while the screen is showing, the car also shows a short message, "MilO could not start this trip". The reason is in the event log twice: the trip controller's own `SERVICE` line ("Could not start recording for Android Auto Start button: MANUAL_START: BACKGROUND_LOCATION_MISSING"), and an `ANDROID_AUTO` line saying that the car screen said so. The phone gets its "could not start this trip" notification as for any other start.
9. **Who may connect.** Android Auto reaches the screen through a service that has to be open to other apps. What it lets in is checked by the fingerprint of the app's signing certificate against the list the Car App Library ships: Android Auto itself, and Google's host for cars that run Android themselves. Any other app is turned away. The same list is used in every build.
10. **What it writes to the event log,** all as `ANDROID_AUTO` lines: "car app service created, a host is connecting"; "session created by (the host's package name), Car API level (the level agreed)"; "shown on the car's display"; "no longer shown"; "session destroyed"; "car app service destroyed"; and "said that the trip could not start". Each press of the button is the trip controller's own `TRIGGER` line, with "Android Auto Start button" or "Android Auto End button" as its source and the state before and after in its detail.
11. **If the screen's own code fails,** the failure is written to the log as an `ERROR` line and MilO closes itself on the car's display. An exception left alone would end the whole process, and that is the process recording the trip.

**Where the code lives**
- `platform/car/MiloCarAppService.kt`: the service Android Auto binds, and the check of who may connect.
- `platform/car/MiloCarSession.kt`: one visit of Android Auto. Builds the screen and writes the session's lines to the log.
- `platform/car/TripStatusScreen.kt`: the screen. Follows the four sources, redraws, builds the pane.
- `platform/car/CarScreenContent.kt`: `carScreenContent` (what is shown, from the controller's state and today's trips), `CarStatus`, `CarAction`, and `differsOnlyInTripFigures` (which changes wait for the 15 seconds). Pure functions.
- `data/trip/TripTotals.kt`: `todayTrips` (what counts as today), shared with the phone's home screen since 2026-10-06.
- `platform/trip/TripActivity.kt`: `truckConnected`, added for this screen.
- `core/util/TimeSpan.kt`: `daySpan`, today as a span of stored time. `core/util/DurationFormat.kt`: `wholeHoursAndMinutes`, the time since a trip started.
- The manifest declares the service (category `androidx.car.app.category.IOT`), the car app descriptor `res/xml/automotive_app_desc.xml` (a "template" app) and the lowest Car API level the screen works with, 7. The words are in `strings.xml` under "The Android Auto screen".
- Tests: `app/src/test/.../platform/car/CarScreenContentTest.kt` (every state: no trip, recording, the grace period, truck not connected, setup incomplete, each kind of refused start; the figures; the button), `app/src/test/.../data/trip/TripTotalsTest.kt` (what counts as today), `CarScreenRefreshTest.kt` (what is redrawn at once and what waits), `MiloCarSessionTest.kt` (the session's log line), `app/src/test/.../platform/trip/TripActivityTest.kt` (what is reported about the truck) and `app/src/test/.../core/util/TimeSpanTest.kt` (a day). The service, the session and the screen themselves have no tests: they need a car, and there is no Robolectric (STANDARDS §11).

**Depends on**
`androidx.car.app:app` and `app-projected` 1.7.0, and `androidx.lifecycle:lifecycle-runtime`. The Android Auto app on the phone, which is what draws the screen and decides the Car API level. The trip controller, the trip repository and the setup checklist.

**Edge cases & gotchas**
- **Nothing here has been run.** Whether the pane looks right, whether the car accepts it, whether the button reacts, whether the lines appear in the log: all unknown until device checks 88 to 100.
- **"Truck not connected" is what the trip rules believe, not a fresh look at the truck.** While no trip is open nothing reads the truck's connection unless a Bluetooth event arrives or MilO is opened on the phone. If MilO missed the truck connecting, the car says "Not recording. Truck not connected" while the truck is connected. That is the moment the button is for: a press of Start trip reads the truck first. Opening MilO on the car's display does not make the trip controller read the truck, the way opening it on the phone does; whether it should is Shawn's to decide (FINDINGS_LOG, 2026-10-05).
- For up to 15 seconds after the companion service alone has started a trip, the line is "Recording. Truck not connected": the truck is not confirmed yet.
- **The lowest Car API level is 7.** The header is built with the library's current `Header` class, which needs level 7. The level belongs to the Android Auto app on the phone, not to the truck, and Google does not publish which level which version speaks. An Android Auto too old for level 7 would not open MilO at all; the log would then show "car app service created" with no "session created" after it. A host that failed the certificate check leaves the same trace; the library's own Logcat lines (their tags begin with `CarApp`) tell the two apart.
- If Google changes Android Auto's signing certificate, the list in a pinned library goes out of date and MilO is turned away until the library is updated.
- **The library is one release behind a security fix.** The release notes of 1.8.0-rc01 say it includes one, without naming it, and tell every lower version to update. It is a release candidate, so 1.7.0 stays until 1.8.0 is stable, and 1.8.0 is then to be taken at once. It matters here because the check of who may connect (9 above) is the library's code, and the service behind it is open to every app on the phone (FINDINGS_LOG, 2026-10-05, `[DEBT]`).
- **A refused start that repeats exactly** (the same reason again) shows no second message: the Status line already says it and does not change.
- A Status line that says "Could not start" stays after the cause was put right, until the next trip starts. The phone's home screen does the same.
- **Android Auto decides how long the screen lives.** Google's own reference host drops an app about three minutes after the driver leaves it; what Android Auto does on this phone is not documented, and the log lines are how it will be learned. Nothing depends on the session: the trip is recorded by the trip service whether or not the screen exists.
- **Being shown on the car does not count as being in front for location.** A trip started from the car's button is started like one started by the truck: it needs "Allow all the time", and battery use unrestricted or the companion association. Without them the start is refused, and the screen says so.
- Opening MilO on the car when its process is dead starts the process, which reads the truck as every process start does. Whether HyperOS lets Android Auto start a dead MilO with Autostart off is unknown (device check 99).
- The setup checklist is read each time the car shows the screen. A setting changed while the screen stays up shows only after one of MilO's phone screens has read it again.
- The button has no icon and there is one button, not two. Google's design guide prefers an icon on every button and frowns on a button that changes its label; one button was the brief.
- The category `IOT` is the least wrong of the library's categories (none is for logging trips). It decides which rules Google would review the app against, and nothing is reviewed for an app that is not on Play.
- `todayTrips` was in `platform/car/` while the car screen was its only user. Since the home screen's Today card exists it is in `data/trip/TripTotals.kt`, and both surfaces call that one function.
- The rule "do not end the trip while Android Auto is connected" does not depend on this screen and works either way: `platform/car/AndroidAutoWatcher.kt` watches `CarConnection` during a trip (see [Truck pairing and trip detection](#truck-pairing-and-trip-detection), rule 13).

## Permission checklist

**Status:** In progress (phase 1): built as the Setup screen. Shawn went through it on the phone on 2026-10-05, everything except pairing the truck; the device checks for it have not been run as written · **Platforms:** Android · **Last updated:** 2026-10-06

**What it does**
One screen, Setup, showing every requirement with its state, each with a button that takes Shawn to the place to fix it. The home screen warns while a required one is not in order.

**Required behaviour**
- Items: precise location, background location ("Allow all the time"), notifications, Bluetooth connect, companion background permissions, battery optimisation exemption, Physical activity (for the driving alert).
- Xiaomi items for this phone: Background autostart, Battery saver "No restrictions", "Pause app activity if unused" off, the "Other permissions" switches (Show on Lock screen, Start in background, Permanent notification), and MilO locked in recents. Each has a button that opens the right HyperOS screen.
- The screen is honest about what it can verify. Autostart can only be read through an unofficial check, so it shows as "looks on", "looks off" or "unknown". The Battery saver profile and the recents lock cannot be read at all, so they are "confirm you set this" steps.

**How it works today**
1. **The rows.** `setupRows` in `platform/system/SetupRules.kt` is a pure function: given what the phone reports (`SetupFacts`), the state of the truck's pairing and Shawn's confirmations, it returns every row with its state (OK, problem, unknown, needs confirmation), which sentence to show and what its button does. `SetupReader` reads the facts from Android. The five facts that stop a trip from being recorded are read by `TripPreflight.facts()`, the same code that checks them before the trip service is started, so the checklist and the preflight cannot disagree.
2. **The ten Android rows,** on every phone:

   | Row | In order when | Its button |
   |---|---|---|
   | Precise location | The precise location permission is granted | Android's permission dialog |
   | Location: Allow all the time | The background location permission is granted | Asks for it, which makes Android open its own location page. No button until precise location is granted: Android ignores the request before that, and the row says so |
   | Notifications | MilO's notifications are shown | The permission dialog (Android 13 and later), or the notification settings |
   | Nearby devices (Bluetooth) | The Bluetooth permission is granted | The permission dialog |
   | Location switched on | Location is on for the whole phone | The phone's Location settings |
   | Truck paired and watched | The pairing check says "armed" | MilO's pairing screen. The button is always there: "Pair truck" while nothing is paired or Android has dropped the association (the sentence then says "pair it again"), "Change truck" otherwise |
   | Battery use: unrestricted | MilO is exempt from battery optimisation, and not "Restricted" | Android's dialog that asks for the exemption (`REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS` is declared for it); for "Restricted", MilO's page in the settings. On a Xiaomi phone that page is HyperOS's "App info", which has no "Unrestricted", so the sentence names its Battery saver choice "No restrictions" there |
   | Not paused when unused | "Pause app activity if unused" is off for MilO | The page with that switch |
   | Battery Saver off | The phone-wide Battery Saver is off | The Battery Saver settings |
   | Physical activity | The Physical activity permission is granted, or the driving alert is switched off in Settings (the row then says "Not needed: the driving alert is switched off in Settings." and asks for nothing) | The permission dialog. Since 2026-10-06; only the [driving alert](#safety-net-manual-button-and-driving-alert) uses it |

3. **The four HyperOS rows,** only on a Xiaomi, Redmi or POCO phone (`isXiaomiFamily`, from the manufacturer and the brand the phone reports):
   - **Background autostart.** Read through the unofficial check the research describes: `AppOpsManager.checkOpNoThrow` with MIUI's app-op 10008, reached by reflection. Mode 0 is shown as "Looks on", any other mode as "Looks off", and a call that fails as "MilO could not read this setting". Each sentence says that it is not a certainty. Only when there is no reading does the row offer "I have set this".
   - **Battery saver: No restrictions,** **Other permissions** (Show on Lock screen, starting or opening windows in the background, Permanent notification, as one row) and **Locked in recent apps.** MilO cannot read any of them. Each shows an empty ring and the instruction until Shawn presses "I have set this"; then it is green and says "You confirmed this on (date). MilO cannot check it." "Not set any more" takes the confirmation back. The dates are in the settings store.
   - Their buttons open the HyperOS screens the research found: the Autostart list (`com.miui.securitycenter`'s `AutoStartManagementActivity`, then the action `miui.intent.action.OP_AUTO_START`), MilO's Battery saver choices (`com.miui.powerkeeper`'s `HiddenAppsConfigActivity`), and MilO's permission editor (`miui.intent.action.APP_PERM_EDITOR`). The recents lock has no button: it is a gesture on MilO's card.
4. **Required and recommended.** A row is required if an automatic trip can fail to start, or be cut short, without it. Three rows are only recommended: "Not paused when unused", "Physical activity" and "Other permissions". Their sentences begin with "Recommended". Physical activity is recommended and not required because nothing but the driving alert needs it, and the alert is a safety net that notifies: no trip starts by itself, or fails to, because of it, so it does not belong under the home screen's "a trip may not start by itself".
5. **Opening a settings screen** (`platform/system/SystemScreens.kt`). Each screen has a list of ways to open it, best first, and the last is always Android's own page for MilO. They are tried in order inside a try/catch, so a screen this build of HyperOS does not have is never a crash. When a fallback was used, or nothing opened, an `ERROR` line in the event log says which screen and why.
6. **Asking for a permission.** The button shows Android's dialog. After two refusals Android stops showing it and answers "refused" at once; MilO then opens the settings page that holds the permission, so the button always leads somewhere (`androidDidNotAsk`).
7. **Re-reading.** Android sends no event when a permission or a setting changes. The Setup and Home screens ask the shared `SetupChecklist` to read the phone again every time they come to the front: coming back from a settings screen, one of Android's dialogs closing, and the quick settings panel closing over MilO. The first two resume the screen; the panel does not, so MilO's window getting the focus back counts as well (`CameToFrontEffect`, see [Design system](#design-system)). The truck row and the confirmations update by themselves.
8. **The home screen's warning.** While any required row is not OK, Home shows a card "Setup needs attention" with a button to Setup. The rule is `needsAttention`, and the Setup screen's count at the top uses the same one. No truck paired is one of the required rows.

**Where the code lives**
- `feature/setup/`: `SetupScreen.kt`, `SetupViewModel.kt`, `SetupTexts.kt` (which words each row shows).
- `platform/system/`: `SetupRow.kt` (the rows, their states and what a button can do), `SetupRules.kt` (`setupRows`, `needsAttention`), `SetupFacts.kt` (what is read from the phone, the Physical activity permission included, `SetupReader`, the Autostart reading, `isXiaomiFamily`), `SetupChecklist.kt` (the rows as a flow, shared by Setup and Home), `SystemScreen.kt` (the ways of opening each settings screen, as plain values), `SystemScreens.kt` (opens them), `PermissionAsk.kt` (`androidDidNotAsk`), `TripPreflight.kt` (the five facts shared with the trip service).

## Schedule and Business/Personal

**Status:** In progress (phase 2): built on 2026-10-06. Ran on an emulator, never on the phone. Device checks 128 to 152, and 158 and 161 for a trip that is edited · **Platforms:** Android (phone) · **Last updated:** 2026-10-06

**What it does**
Decides whether a trip is Business or Personal from when it started, and lets Shawn overrule it.

**Required behaviour**
- Settings: choose which days to track and a start and end time for each day. Default Monday to Friday, 08:00 to 16:30. An option applies the same hours to all days.
- A trip that starts inside the schedule is saved as Business. A trip that starts outside it is saved as Personal, with a setting to ignore such trips entirely instead.
- Shawn can switch any trip between Business and Personal.
- If a Business trip is still running at the end time, keep recording until the trip ends and flag it "ran past schedule".

**How it works today**
1. **The schedule** is seven days, each with a switch ("tracked") and a start and an end time. It is kept with the other settings. A day's end must be later than its start, both on a whole minute: there are no overnight spans, and the latest end is 23:59. Out of the box: Monday to Friday, 08:00 to 16:30; Saturday and Sunday are off and carry the same hours for the day one of them is switched on. The screen is described under [Settings](#settings).
2. **The rule** (`classifyTrip` in `core/schedule/TripClassification.kt`, a pure function). A trip is **Business** when its **start**, read as a day and a time of day in the phone's time zone, falls on a tracked day at or after that day's start time and before its end time. Otherwise it is **Personal**. On the stroke of the start time is Business; on the stroke of the end time is Personal. Only the start decides, and what started the trip (the truck, or a Start button on the phone or the car) makes no difference.
3. **Ran past schedule.** A Business trip that ends after the end time *of the day it started on* is flagged. The end time is worked out as a point in time, so a trip that runs past midnight is judged against the day it began, and a day on which the clocks change is as long as it really was. Ending exactly at the end time is not past it. A Personal trip is never flagged.
4. **When it happens: when a trip is finalised, and at no other moment.** The trip rules close the trip as they always did; in the same write that closes it, the category and the flag are stored (`TripLedger.close` in `platform/trip/`, `TripRepository.closeTrip`). The schedule that is used is the one stored at that moment, so a change on the Settings screen applies to trips that end after it. **The schedule never decides whether a trip starts, carries on or ends.** Nothing in the trip rules, the Bluetooth triggers or the trip service reads it. (Since 2026-10-06 the driving alert reads it, through the same rule, to send its notification only when a trip started at that moment would be Business. A notification is all that comes of it.) A truck that connects at 07:55 for a drive at 08:05 is one trip that started at 07:55, recorded whole, saved as Personal, and Shawn can mark it Business.
5. **"Ignore them".** With that chosen for trips outside the schedule, a trip is still started, recorded and measured exactly as usual. When it is finalised and turns out Personal, it is stored as **discarded**, with its times, distance, positions and GPS points, and marked as ignored for that reason. It is then out of the list and the totals, and can be found on the Trips screen under "Show deleted and discarded trips", where it says "Left out: it started outside your work hours, and Settings says to ignore such trips. Not counted." and has the button "Count this trip". Counted, it is an ordinary Personal trip. A trip the trip rules had already discarded (too short, or a start that was never confirmed) is not marked as ignored: it says what it always said.
6. **By hand.** On the Trips screen a finished trip can be marked as Business or as Personal (see [Trip log](#trip-log-home-day-and-month-views)). That writes the category and that Shawn set it, and nothing else. A trip set by hand is never changed by MilO again. With "Ignore them" on, marking a kept trip Personal does not discard it: the setting is applied once, when a trip is finalised, and this trip has now been dealt with by hand.
7. **Trips recorded before the schedule existed** have no category after the update (the migration leaves them "not sorted"). At every process start a catch-up (`TripCategoryCatchUp` in `data/trip/`) looks for closed trips without a category that were not set by hand, and sorts each by the schedule **as it is at that moment** and the phone's time zone then. It writes the category and the flag and nothing else: it never discards a trip, whatever "Ignore them" says, and it sorts deleted and discarded trips too, so that they have their category when they are restored or counted. After its first pass it finds nothing. The first pass after the update runs before Shawn has seen the Settings screen, so it goes by Monday to Friday, 08:00 to 16:30.
8. **A later change of the schedule changes no stored trip,** sorted by the schedule or set by hand. What a trip was saved as is a fact about the day it was driven.
9. **A change of a trip's times by hand** (the edit screen, see [Trip log](#trip-log-home-day-and-month-views)) is the one thing that sorts a stored trip again, and by one rule, `refileTrip` in `core/schedule/TripRefiling.kt`:
   - **A category Shawn picks in the form is his.** The trip is saved as that and marked as set by hand.
   - **A category that was set by hand stays,** whatever is done to the times.
   - **Otherwise a new start is sorted again,** by the schedule as it is at the moment of the save and the phone's time zone then. Only the start decides Business or Personal, so only a changed start re-sorts: a changed end, distance or address leaves the category as it was saved, even if the schedule has been changed since.
   - **"Ran past schedule" follows the times.** Whenever the start or the end changed, it is worked out again from the new times and the schedule as it is now, whoever chose the category: true only if the schedule makes the trip Business and it ends after that day's hours. If neither time changed it is kept as stored. It is still shown only while the trip is Business.
   - **"Restore recorded values" goes by the same rule** for the times that come back: a trip that is not set by hand is sorted again by its recorded start, by the schedule as it is then.
   - **A trip that is added by hand** is sorted by its start like any other, unless Shawn chooses himself. "Ignore them" is not applied to it, and neither is the minimum distance: he typed it in on purpose.
   - The form shows what a save will store before Save is pressed, and says who chose: the work hours, Shawn, or "as this trip was saved".
10. **A trip that is not sorted** (possible only between the update and the first pass, or while the settings file cannot be read) is shown as "Not marked Business or Personal yet", is counted in a line of its own, and can be marked by hand.
11. **The event log.** The `TRIP` line that records the end of a trip now ends with what the schedule made of it and what it went by: "…; saved as Business: it started on a Tue at 10:14:05; that day's hours are 08:00 to 16:30 (America/Edmonton)", "…; saved as Business, ran past schedule: …", "…; saved as Personal: it started on a Sat at 10:02:40; Sat is not a tracked day (America/Edmonton)". An ignored trip's line starts "Trip 12: discarded: it started outside the work schedule, and trips outside it are set to be ignored (12340 m). It can be counted on the Trips screen" and, like the line of a trip that was too short, ends with what the trip is if it is counted after all ("…; if it is counted, it is Personal: it started on a Sat at 10:02:40; Sat is not a tracked day (America/Edmonton)"). The catch-up writes one `TRIP` line for a pass that sorted something ("Business or Personal set for 5 trips recorded before they were sorted (process start), by the work schedule as it is now: 3 Business (1 ran past schedule), 2 Personal. No trip was discarded or otherwise changed."), with each trip and its reason in the detail. Every marking by hand is one `TRIP` line, and a refused or failed one is a line too.

**Where the code lives**
- `core/schedule/`: `WorkSchedule.kt` (the seven days, what hours a day may have, the defaults, the copy), `TripClassification.kt` (`classifyTrip`, and `fileTrip`, which adds the "ignore" decision at the moment a trip is finalised), `TripRefiling.kt` (`refileTrip`: what a change of the times by hand makes of the category and of "ran past schedule"), `TripCategory.kt` (Business, Personal). Pure Kotlin: no file in the package imports anything of Android, its string resources included.

**Depends on**
The settings store, the trips table (the four columns are there since database version 3), and the phone's time zone and clock.

**Edge cases & gotchas**
- **Never run on the phone.** What ran on an emulator on 2026-10-06 is in FINDINGS_LOG under that date.
- **The start decides, to the millisecond.** A trip that starts a minute before the day's start time is Personal however long it runs. The cure is the button: mark it Business.
- **The trip's start is when MilO opened the trip,** which for a trip the truck starts is when the phone connected to it, not when the truck began to move.
- **The time zone is the phone's at the moment the trip is sorted,** not where the trip was driven. A trip is sorted when it ends, so this only shows if the phone changes zone during a trip. For the catch-up it is the zone at the first start after the update. **What it costs:** a work drive from Alberta into the Pacific time zone is read an hour earlier than it began, so one that started in the first hour of the work day is saved as Personal, and with "Ignore them" on it is left out. In winter a drive into Saskatchewan is read an hour later, and one that started in the last hour of the day goes the same way. Nothing on the trip's row says why; its line in the Log names the zone it was read in. It is put right by hand: Mark as Business, after Count this trip if it was left out. The cure is to store the zone with the trip when it starts and to sort by that: a new column, and a change to how a trip is started, which the work on the schedule was not to touch. A reviewer reproduced the case on an emulator on 2026-10-06; it was left as it is, for Shawn to decide.
- **The first catch-up uses the default hours.** If Shawn's real hours differ, trips recorded before the update can be sorted wrongly, and changing the schedule afterwards does not sort them again. They are marked by hand.
- **"Ran past schedule" is stored when the schedule sorts the trip,** and is shown only while the trip is Business. A trip that was Personal by the schedule and is marked Business by hand never shows it, even if it ended after the day's end time: a marking does not work the flag out again, and the schedule gives such a trip no hours to run past. Only a change of the trip's times on the edit screen works it out again (step 9).
- **An edit of the start can change what a trip is saved as.** Moving a start from 07:55 to 08:05 makes a Personal trip Business, and the form shows that before Save is pressed. It is sorted by the schedule as it is on the day of the edit, not as it was on the day of the drive; if the hours have changed since, the form's choice is put right by hand, which also makes it final.
- **With "Ignore them" on, a Personal trip looks like a trip that vanished:** it is recorded, then it is not in the list. The Trips screen's line "N deleted or discarded trips are not shown" is the only sign, and the Log says why.
- **An ignored trip gets no addresses** until it is counted: only a finished trip is looked up.
- **A setting that cannot be read leaves a closing trip unsorted** (it is not sorted by hours that are not Shawn's), and the catch-up logs an `ERROR` line at each start until the settings can be read again.
- A day's hours cannot run past midnight. Night work that crosses midnight would need two days' hours, and the trip is still judged by its start alone.
- **What a trip is saved as decides whether it is on the report for the accountant:** only Business trips are ([Monthly PDF and submission](#monthly-pdf-and-submission)). A trip that the schedule made Personal by a minute is left off until it is marked Business; the Report screen says how many Personal trips its period has.

## Trip log: home, day and month views

**Status:** In progress: the home screen shows the trip in progress and today's finished trips, and the Trips screen lists one month at a time (times, from and to addresses, Business or Personal, km), where a trip can be deleted and restored, a discarded trip counted after all, and a finished trip marked Business or Personal. Each day and each month shows its Business figures first and Personal apart, added up as the report for the accountant adds up, and a month whose report was sent says when its Business trips have changed since. A finished trip can be edited and a missed trip added by hand, on an edit screen opened from Trips; both are marked in the list. All of it ran on an emulator, never on the phone · **Platforms:** Android · **Last updated:** 2026-10-06

**What it does**
Shows what was recorded and lets Shawn correct it.

**Required behaviour**
- Home screen: current trip status, plus today's sessions (count, km each, total km, total drive time).
- Day view: each session with start and end time, start and end address, km, and Business/Personal.
- Month view: sessions and business km per day, plus month totals.
- Shawn can edit any trip, delete trips, and manually add a missed trip. Manually added or edited trips are marked as such.
- There is no purpose or note field. Dropped at kickoff.

**How it works today**
*Home* (`feature/home/`). Beside the title, a cog that opens [Settings](#settings). While the setup checklist needs attention, a card "Setup needs attention" with a button to Setup (see [Permission checklist](#permission-checklist)). Then one card, "Current trip": with no trip open, "No trip in progress"; with one, the distance so far, "Trip in progress" (or that it is waiting for the truck to reconnect) and the time it started. It reads `TripController.activity` through `HomeViewModel` and keeps no copy. Below the card, if the last start failed, a second card lists why. Then the Start trip / End trip button, and under it the card "Today":
1. **What it shows.** Business first (since 2026-10-06): the word "Business", the km of today's Business trips in large figures, and under it how many Business trips and their drive time ("3 trips · 1 h 12 min driving"). Then, in a quieter line of its own and never added to the Business figure, "Personal: 5.0 km · 1 trip" (it is there with zeros on a day without a Personal trip). Then every finished trip on a line of its own with its start time, its end time, what it is saved as ("Business", "Business · ran past schedule" or "Personal") and its km, newest first. Before the first trip of the day: "No finished trips yet today."
2. **What is counted** is decided by `todayTrips` in `data/trip/TripTotals.kt`, the function the [Android Auto screen](#android-auto-screen) uses: finished trips that started today. A discarded or a deleted trip is not there, and so a trip left out by "Ignore them" is not there either. The trip in progress is not there: it has the card above, and while one is open the Today card says "The trip in progress is added when it ends." The split into Business and Personal is `TodayTrips.totals`, worked out by the same `categoryTotals` rule the Trips screen uses. **Every total is the sum of the kilometres shown for its trips,** each rounded to a tenth first (`sumOfTenths` in `core/util/DistanceFormat.kt`, the rule of the report for the accountant; "How the kilometres are added up", below).
3. **Drive time** is the time from start to end of each finished Business trip, added up and rounded down to whole minutes once. A stop with the engine running is inside a trip, so it is in the drive time. Personal trips have no drive time on the card.
4. **It follows storage.** A trip that ends, or is deleted, restored or counted on the Trips screen, changes the card by itself. Only today's trips are read (`daySpan` in `core/util/TimeSpan.kt`).
5. **Which day is today** is worked out when the screen comes to the front (`CameToFrontEffect`), in the phone's time zone. A trip belongs to the day it started on.
6. The button stands above the card so that it does not move down the screen as the day fills up.

*Trips* (`feature/trips/`), the second button of the bottom bar. Shawn asked for it by name on 2026-10-05: "a trip button to view previous and current months trips".
1. It opens on the current month, every time it is entered. (Coming back from the edit screen after a save, it shows the month the saved trip is in: "Editing a trip", step 13.) At the top, one card: the month's name and year; then Business first, the word "Business", the km of the month's Business trips in large figures and their number; then, apart and quieter, "Personal: 22.0 km · 3 trips"; then **whether the month's report has been sent to the accountant** ("Not submitted", "Submitted on Oct 6, 2026", or the same with the day it was sent again and the revision's number); for a submitted month whose Business trips are no longer what the report held, **a red line that says so** ("Changed since the report was sent. It listed 4 trips and 51.7 km; the month now has 3 trips and 50.7 km.", see [Monthly PDF and submission](#monthly-pdf-and-submission)); and the button **"Report for the accountant"**, which opens the Report screen for the month on the card ([Monthly PDF and submission](#monthly-pdf-and-submission)); and two buttons, Previous month and Next month. Next month is greyed out on the current month: the screen never goes past it. Back is one month at a time, without limit. Business and Personal are never added into one figure. (A third line, "Not marked Business or Personal yet: …", appears only while such a trip exists; see [Schedule and Business/Personal](#schedule-and-businesspersonal).)
2. Under it a switch, "Show deleted and discarded trips", off by default. (Until 2026-10-06 it was "Show discarded trips".)
3. Then the trips, one card per day, newest day first and newest trip first within a day. **Under the day's date, its heading has a second line: the day's number of sessions and its Business km** ("3 trips · 42.1 km business"). The sessions are the day's finished trips, Business and Personal together; the km are of the Business ones only. Each trip shows its start time, its end time and its km, under the times where it went, and under that **what it is saved as: "Business", "Business · ran past schedule" or "Personal"**. **A trip whose figures are Shawn's own carries an asterisk after its times and one more line, "* Added by hand" or "* Edited by hand"** (see "Editing a trip" below); the asterisk is the mark the report for the accountant uses for the same trips. Where it went: "10103 104 Avenue Northwest, Edmonton → 9321 Jasper Avenue, Edmonton". Shawn asked for this: "I just want to track from start address to end one for each trip." There is no map.
   - **A missing address is said in words,** never as coordinates and never as a gap. While neither address is known: "Looking up the addresses…". When MilO has given up on both: "No address found for this trip." With one known, the other side of the arrow says "looking up the address…" or "no address found".
   - An address that Shawn left empty himself, in a trip he added by hand or on the edit screen, is "no address entered", and with both empty "No address entered for this trip.": MilO never looked, so "no address found" would not be true.
   - The words are chosen by plain functions in `TripPlaceTexts.kt` from what the trip's row says (`startPlace` and `endPlace` in `platform/address/AddressRetry.kt`): an address, still being looked up, not found, or left empty by hand.
   - Each time the screen comes to the front it asks for the missing addresses to be looked up (see [GPS recording and distance](#gps-recording-and-distance)). An address that arrives while the screen is open appears by itself.
4. **What is counted.** The totals and the numbers of trips are of the finished trips only (`isCounted` in `data/trip/TripTotals.kt`), added up by what each is saved as (`categoryTotals`, same file), each trip as it is printed (below). A trip discarded as too short (or as a false start), a trip left out by "Ignore them" and a trip Shawn deleted are left out of the list and the totals; a line says how many are hidden ("2 deleted or discarded trips are not shown."). With the switch on they are listed in their day with their km greyed and with what they would be saved as, and neither the totals nor a day's heading change:
   - a discarded trip is marked "Discarded: too short, or a start that was never confirmed. Not counted.", with no line about addresses (a discarded trip is never looked up);
   - a trip left out by "Ignore them" is a discarded trip too, and is marked "Left out: it started outside your work hours, and Settings says to ignore such trips. Not counted." Its km are the full distance that was recorded;
   - a deleted trip is marked "Deleted by you. Not counted.", with the addresses it already had when it was deleted. An address it lacked is "no address found", never "looking up": nothing is looked up for a trip while it is deleted.
5. **A trip in progress** is shown in a card of its own above the days, "In progress", with its start time and its running distance, and is not in the total until it ends. The running distance comes from the trip controller, because the stored row holds 0 while a trip is open; if the controller is not recording that trip, no figure is shown. Under it, where the trip started: "Where it started is not known yet." until the first usable GPS fix, then "Looking up where it started…", then "From (address)", or "No address found for where it started."
6. **Which day and month a trip belongs to.** The day and the month it started in, in the phone's time zone. A trip that runs past midnight is listed once, under the day it started.
7. An empty month says "No trips in (month and year)."
8. **How the kilometres are added up** (since 2026-10-06, phase 4). One rule for every total MilO shows, the report for the accountant included: **each trip is rounded to a tenth of a kilometre, and the rounded figures are added** (`sumOfTenths` in `core/util/DistanceFormat.kt`). So the rows of a day add up to the figure in the day's heading, the days add up to the month, and the month's Business figure is the total its report prints, to the tenth. The month card, each day's heading, Home's Today card and the Android Auto screen's Today row all go through that one function, by way of `categoryTotals` and `todayTrips`. Until then the screens added up metres and rounded the sum once: on an emulator one test month read 716.3 km on Trips and 716.4 km on its report, and a day of three trips of 1.1 km read "3.4 km business". A single trip's figure was not changed; `formatKilometres` is now that same rounding written out.

*Marking a trip Business or Personal.* The brief: "I can switch any trip between Business and Personal."
1. Tapping a finished trip shows its buttons under it. The first is "Edit trip" (below). Then "Mark as Personal" on a Business trip or "Mark as Business" on a Personal one (a trip that is not sorted yet shows both); "Delete trip" is the last.
2. The press is made at once, with no question: the other button undoes it. The buttons are put away with the press. Left open, the same spot would at once hold the opposite button, and a double tap would undo the first.
3. **What it does.** The trip's category is written, with the note that Shawn set it by hand (`TripRepository.setCategoryByHand`), and nothing else on the row changes: it stays a finished, counted trip with its times, distance and addresses. Its row, its day's heading, the month's two totals and Home's Today follow at once.
4. **Set by hand is final for MilO.** The catch-up never touches such a trip, and no change of the schedule does. Only another press changes it.
5. **"Ran past schedule" follows the category.** It is shown only on a Business trip. A Business trip that ran past the schedule and is marked Personal stops showing it; marked Business again, it shows it again.
6. **With "Ignore them" on, marking a kept trip Personal does not discard it.** The marking writes the category and never the status.
7. **Only a finished trip can be marked.** A deleted or a discarded trip is restored or counted first, and the trip in progress has no category yet. The rule is in storage too: the update matches a finished trip that does not already have the category (`categoriesOffered` in `data/trip/TripCategories.kt` is the same rule for the screen, so it cannot offer what storage would refuse).
8. **The event log** gets one `TRIP` line per press: "Trip 12: marked Personal by hand on the Trips screen (12340 m, was Business). Nothing else about it changed, and the work schedule no longer decides what it is". A press that changed nothing gets one too ("Trip 12: mark as Personal refused on the Trips screen: it is Personal already. Nothing changed"), and a failure of storage an `ERROR` line; the screen then shows "That trip could not be changed. The Log screen says why."

*Editing a trip, restoring what was recorded, and adding a missed trip* (`feature/tripedit/`). The brief: "I can edit any trip, delete trips, and manually add a missed trip. Manually added or edited trips are marked as such."
1. **The way in.** "Edit trip" under a tapped finished trip opens the edit screen for that trip. "Add a missed trip", a text button under the month's card, opens the same screen empty. The screen has a Back arrow, and the bottom bar keeps marking Trips. A trip that is being recorded, discarded or deleted has no "Edit trip": it is ended, counted or restored first, and storage refuses anything else.
2. **The form.** Four cards: "When" (the date, the start and the end, each a button that shows the value and opens a calendar or the clock dial, and a switch "Ended the next day"), "Where" (two fields, From and To), "Distance" (kilometres, typed with a dot or a comma) and "Business or Personal" (one of two, and a line that says who chose: MilO from the work hours, Shawn, or "as this trip was saved"). Then the Save button ("Add this trip" on an empty form).
   - **The date is the day the trip started.** The end is on the same day unless the switch is on; then it is on the day after, and a line under the switch names that day ("The end time is on Tuesday, October 6, 2026."). A stored trip that ran past midnight opens with the switch on. Switching it off brings such a trip back onto one day; switching it on is how a missed trip across midnight is typed in. **MilO never moves an end to the next day by itself:** an end that is earlier than the start is far more often a slip of the dial, so it is refused, and the refusal names the switch.
3. **Nothing is stored until Save is pressed,** and the screen leaves when it has been. It is the one screen of MilO with a Save button: a trip's figures only make sense together, and one save is one line in the log. **Leaving with something changed and not saved asks first:** "Leave without saving?", with "Keep editing" and "Discard". Every way out asks: the phone's Back, the arrow beside the title, and each button of the bottom bar; "Discard" then leads where the press was going. A form in which nothing differs from what it opened with (also one that was changed and put back) is left at once. The question is the app's and not the screen's (`app/UnsavedWork.kt`): the screen only says whether it holds unsaved work (`holdsUnsavedWork` in `TripForm.kt`).
4. **What the form refuses** (`formProblems`). The end must be later than the start. Neither may be in the future. The trip may not run into the trip that is being recorded: that one runs from its start until some moment still to come, so the rule is that the edited or added trip ends no later than the recording began. The distance must be a number, not less than zero, not more than 2,000 km, and not further than the truck gets in the time (more than 180 km/h on average, which catches a lost decimal point). Zero is taken. **Each refusal is said in red where it is put right:** what is wrong with the times in the card "When", under the two times and the switch, one line for each thing; what is wrong with the distance directly under the Kilometres field, which is then outlined in red. The screen moves by itself to the first of them, because the Save button is a screen or more further down. Nothing is stored, and a line goes away as soon as what it names is put right. The form is checked at the moment Save is pressed, against the clock and the recording as they are then. Trips that have finished are not compared with each other. Only a failure of storage is said at the Save button itself.
5. **A save writes only what was changed.** Every field that was not touched stays exactly as stored: a recorded time to the millisecond (the form shows whole minutes; a time that is changed and put back to the minute it had is the stored time again), a distance to the metre (typing the figure the form shows, "12.3" over a stored 12,344 m, is not a change), an address as it is. A trip that is opened and saved is not changed and not marked, and the log says so.
6. **What counts as an edit.** The trip is marked as edited when its start, its end, its distance or one of its addresses is different after the save. Business or Personal alone is not an edit: it is written with the note that Shawn set it by hand, as on the Trips screen, and the trip stays unmarked.
7. **What MilO recorded is kept.** The first edit copies the trip's start, end and distance, which are then still the recorded ones, into three columns of the row that are never written again. The trip's positions and its GPS points are not touched by any edit.
8. **Restore recorded values.** The edit screen of an edited trip has one more card under Save, "What MilO recorded", with the recorded day, times and km and the button. It asks first, naming what comes back, because it drops what Shawn typed in their place. It puts back the recorded start, end and distance exactly, removes any address he typed or emptied and hands it back to the lookup (whose attempts for the trip start afresh; an address the lookup itself had found is kept), and takes the "edited" mark away. The distance is the figure that was kept, not a new calculation from the GPS points (ARCHITECTURE section 6 says why). The trip can be edited again afterwards, and restored again.
9. **A typed address is Shawn's for good.** An address he typed, changed or emptied is never looked up and never overwritten (see [GPS recording and distance](#gps-recording-and-distance)). An emptied one reads "no address entered".
10. **Adding a missed trip.** The form opens on today's date, whichever month Trips was showing, with nothing else filled in: no time, no address, no distance, and neither Business nor Personal chosen until there is a start time to sort by. The date can be any day up to today. The trip is stored as a finished trip marked as added by hand, with no GPS points and no positions, so nothing is looked up for it: its addresses are what was typed, or "no address entered". It is counted from that moment in the month's and the day's figures, in Home's Today and on the Android Auto screen, like any finished trip, and it can be marked, edited and deleted like any other. Editing it never marks it as edited (all of it is by hand already) and it has nothing to restore.
11. **Business or Personal after an edit** follows one rule, set out under [Schedule and Business/Personal](#schedule-and-businesspersonal), step 9: Shawn's own choice wins, a trip set by hand keeps its category, and otherwise a changed start is sorted again by the work hours; "ran past schedule" follows the times.
12. **The event log** gets one `TRIP` line for every save that reaches storage, and it names each value that changed, old and new: "Trip 12: changed by hand on the edit screen: start 2026-10-06 08:14:27 → 2026-10-06 08:05:00; distance 12345 m → 13000 m; from "12 Shop Rd, Edmonton" → "14 Shop Rd, Edmonton" (times in America/Edmonton). It is now marked as edited, and "Restore recorded values" puts back what MilO recorded". After the first edit the row keeps only what MilO recorded, so a value typed in between survives nowhere but in these lines; that is why they spell addresses out, which the lookup's lines never do. A save that changed nothing, a restore ("… recorded values restored on the edit screen: distance 13000 m → 12345 m; from "Home" → none …"), an added trip ("Trip 31: added by hand on the edit screen: … to …, 23400 m, from "Shop" to none, Business (sorted by the work schedule). …") and a write that storage refused each have their line; a failure of storage is an `ERROR` line, and the screen then says "That could not be saved. The Log screen says why." A form that was refused for what is typed in it stores nothing and logs nothing.
13. **Back on Trips after a save, the month the trip is in is shown,** at the top of its list, whichever month was on screen before (`monthOfSavedTrip` in `feature/trips/TripMonth.kt`; the app carries the trip's start from the one screen to the other). A trip that is added for last month, or moved there, is therefore in front of Shawn when he comes back, and so is a trip that was filed under today because the date was forgotten. Without this it was stored out of sight, looked as if it had not been saved, and a second attempt stored it twice (seen by a reviewer on an emulator, 2026-10-06). "Restore recorded values" does the same for the month the trip was recorded in. Leaving without a save changes nothing on Trips.

*Deleting, restoring and counting a trip.* Shawn asked on 2026-10-05: "can we delete trips?"
1. **Delete.** Above the first day a line says "Tap a trip to edit it, mark it Business or Personal, or delete it." Tapping a finished trip shows its buttons under it, "Delete trip" the last of them; tapping the trip again puts them away. One trip at a time shows its buttons. If a button would come up below the edge of the screen, as it does for the lowest trip in view, the list moves up until the trip and its buttons can all be seen. Only a tap moves the list: a button that comes back into view because the list was scrolled stays where it is. A deleted or a discarded trip is not something to tap, and a screen reader does not offer it as one; its own button (below) is. The button asks first: "Delete this trip?", with the trip's times and km and how to get it back, and the buttons Cancel and Delete. Cancel, Back and a tap beside the question change nothing.
2. **What Delete does.** The trip's status becomes `DELETED`, and nothing else on its row changes: its times, distance, positions and addresses stay, and so do its raw GPS points. It leaves the list, the month's total and count, the home screen's Today and the Android Auto screen's Today at once.
3. **Restore.** With the switch on, a deleted trip has a button "Restore". It makes the trip a finished trip again, exactly as it was. No question is asked: nothing is taken away, and Delete undoes it.
4. **Count this trip.** With the switch on, a discarded trip has a button "Count this trip". It makes the trip an ordinary finished trip, with the distance that was measured. It is there because the distance thresholds are untested on the phone and a real trip can have been discarded wrongly. The button is on every discarded trip, a false start included: on a truck whose connection no reading can confirm, a real drive ends up as one. A counted trip is deleted like any other, which is also how counting is undone.
5. **A trip that is still being recorded cannot be deleted.** Its card has no button and says so. The rule is also in storage: every one of the three changes is one update that matches on the status it starts from (`TripCorrection` in `data/trip/`: finished to deleted, deleted to finished, discarded to finished), so a press that arrives for a trip in any other state, an open one above all, changes nothing. Which button a row shows is taken from the same three lines (`correctionOffered`: the change that starts from the row's status), so the screen cannot offer a change that storage would refuse.
6. **Addresses.** A deleted or discarded trip is never looked up. When a trip becomes a finished one again, by Restore or by Count this trip, the screen asks for a lookup pass at once, and its missing addresses arrive like any other trip's.
7. **The event log** gets one `TRIP` line for every press that reaches storage: "Trip 12: deleted on the Trips screen (12340 m, was finished). It is no longer counted. Its row and its GPS points are kept, and Restore puts it back", "Trip 12: restored on the Trips screen (12340 m, was deleted). It is counted again", "Trip 9: counted on the Trips screen (250 m, was discarded). It is now a finished trip". A press that changed nothing gets one too ("Trip 12: delete refused on the Trips screen: it is still being recorded. Nothing changed"), and a failure of storage an `ERROR` line with the exception. After either of those the screen shows "That trip could not be changed. The Log screen says why." until the next change.
8. **Reading.** Only the month on screen is read: `TripRepository.observeTripsStartedBetween` takes a span of time, and `monthSpan` in `core/util/TimeSpan.kt` gives the span of a month in a time zone (from local midnight on the 1st up to, not including, local midnight on the next 1st, so a month in which the clocks change is an hour longer or shorter). **Edge cases & gotchas**
- **Editing and adding have never run on the phone.** They ran on an emulator on 2026-10-06 (FINDINGS_LOG), where the keyboard, the clock dial and the calendar are Google's and not HyperOS's. Device checks 153 to 176.
- **What is typed and not saved is lost only after "Discard"** (step 3), and in one other way that asks nothing: if Android ends MilO while it is in the background. MilO then opens on the edit screen again, with the trip as it is stored (seen on the emulator), and with no question left over. The form is held by the screen's ViewModel and not in the saved state. Turning the phone keeps it, and keeps the question if it was open.
- **The keyboard takes room from the form and does not push it away.** MilO draws behind the phone's status bar, so a window that Android slides up for the keyboard put the form's text under the clock (seen by a reviewer on an emulator, 2026-10-06). The activity is now declared `adjustResize` and `MiloApp` ends the screens where the keyboard begins: the field that is typed into stays whole above it and the form scrolls down to Save with the keyboard open. The bottom bar is under the keyboard meanwhile. Seen on the emulator's keyboard, upright and on its side; HyperOS's own is device check 173.
- **An edit is not checked against other finished trips.** Two trips can be made to overlap, or a missed trip added over a recorded one, and both are then counted. Only the trip that is being recorded is protected.
- **A recorded trip whose stored times make no sense** (the phone corrected its clock during it, so that it ends before it starts) cannot be saved as it is from the edit screen: the form asks for the times to be put right first, even if only an address was to be changed. Marking it Business or Personal on its row still works.
- **The switch says "the next day" and no more.** A trip can end on the day it started or on the day after. A stored trip that ran over more than one midnight (never seen; it would take a truck that stayed connected for over a day) opens as it is stored and names its real end day, but once the switch has been touched it ends on the start day or the next.
- **The hour that happens twice.** On the night the clocks go back (Sunday 1 November 2026, 1:00 to 2:00), a dial cannot say which of the two hours is meant. A time typed over a stored one is read on the side of the change the stored time is on: a trip that ended at the second 1:30 and is given the end 1:40 ends ten minutes later, not fifty minutes earlier (before the review it was saved fifty minutes short, with no warning). For a trip that is being added there is no stored time, and it is the first of the two; a trip across that hour cannot be typed in exactly. On the night the clocks go forward a time in the hour that does not exist is moved on by that hour.
- **A typed time is on the whole minute,** while a recorded one is exact to the second. A trip whose start was edited therefore starts at :00 seconds.
- **The edited mark does not go away by itself.** A trip that is edited back, by hand, to the figures it was recorded with is still marked; "Restore recorded values" is what takes the mark away.
- **A trip's positions are never edited.** A trip whose address was typed over still has the coordinates where the truck was, and they stay the record. A trip added by hand has none.
- **The Trips screen has never been drawn on the phone.** Device checks 76 to 86. It was drawn on an emulator on 2026-10-05, with addresses (FINDINGS_LOG). Deleting, restoring, counting and the home screen's Today card were used on an emulator on 2026-10-06, and are device checks 101 to 110 and 125 on the phone.
- **A deleted trip is never destroyed,** and nothing in MilO can destroy one: there is no "empty the bin". The row and its raw points stay on the phone for good, like a discarded trip's.
- "Count this trip" counts the distance as measured. If the trip was discarded because GPS was poor and the distance came out too short, the counted trip carries that too-short distance; nothing recalculates it from the raw points. Once it is counted it can be edited like any finished trip, and the distance typed in.
- Restore and Count this trip ask no question, Delete does. A trip counted by mistake is put right by deleting it; it then shows as "Deleted by you", not as "Discarded" again.
- Which finished trip shows its Delete button, and whether the switch is on, belong to the screen: leaving Trips and coming back starts with the switch off and no button showing.
- The home screen's Today is worked out when Home comes to the front. Left open across midnight, it keeps showing the day it was opened on until Home is left and opened again, or MilO is. The Android Auto screen looks at the clock every 10 seconds and does not have this.
- **A total is the sum of the figures shown, not of the metres behind them** (step 8 of Trips). Three trips of 1,149 m are 1.1 km each and 3.3 km together, although their metres come to 3.447 km. The difference from the real distance is at most 50 m a trip, either way, and evens out over a month; what was gained is that every figure Shawn can add up by hand adds up, and that the phone and the report never disagree.
- **Sending is not done on the Trips screen itself.** Its month card says whether the month was submitted and leads to the Report screen, where a month or a date range is made into a PDF, sent, and exported as CSV ([Monthly PDF and submission](#monthly-pdf-and-submission)). The line on the card follows the list of sent reports, so it changes by itself when Shawn answers "I sent it" there.
- There is no separate day view: a day is its card on the Trips screen, with its heading. (Deleting was phase 2 in the brief and was built on 2026-10-06, when Shawn asked for it; Business/Personal, the figures of each day, editing and adding followed the same day.)
- **Every finished trip is listed and counted, Business or Personal.** What keeps a Personal trip off the report is its category, not its absence. Only "Ignore them" takes a trip out of the list, and then as a discarded trip that can be counted.
- A day's heading counts sessions of both kinds and adds up Business km only, as the brief asks. A day of Personal trips only therefore reads "2 trips · 0.0 km business".
- The Android Auto screen's Today row still adds Business and Personal together (see [Android Auto screen](#android-auto-screen)).
- Marking a trip has never been tried with a screen reader, like the other buttons of a row (device check 127).
- The query by span of time is read through an index on the start time, added by the first migration (database version 2).
- "From → to" wraps onto a second line when the two addresses are long; a trip's row is then taller than its neighbours. A finished trip's row is never lower than 48 dp, Material's smallest target for a finger, which matters for one with a single line under its times ("Looking up the addresses…").
- The delete, restore and count buttons of a row have never been used with a screen reader (device check 127).
- A trip still recording at the turn of the month is shown in the month it started in, not in the new current month.
- **The month's Business figure is the report's total,** to the tenth of a kilometre, as long as both are worked out in the same time zone: the two read the same trips (finished, Business, started in the month) and add them up by the same function. Seen on an emulator on 2026-10-06: 716.4 km on both, where the build before showed 716.3 km on Trips.
- If the phone's time zone changes, the days and months are worked out again in the new zone the next time the screen comes to the front. A trip near midnight can then move to the neighbouring day.
- **A trip's times have 24 hours or AM and PM as the phone itself is set** ("Use 24-hour format" in Android's date and time settings; until that switch has been touched, Android goes by the language). "08:14 – 08:39" on a phone set to 24 hours, "8:14 a.m. – 8:39 a.m." on one that is not, on Home, on Trips and in the question before a delete alike. Until the review of 2026-10-06 the language alone decided. The switch is read when a screen is drawn and again each time it comes back, so a change shows without a restart (seen on an emulator, device check 152). The Log's times are always 24 hours, to the second.
- A trip whose stored start is later than its end (the clock was corrected during it, see [GPS recording and distance](#gps-recording-and-distance)) is listed as stored.

## Monthly PDF and submission

**Status:** In progress (phase 3): built on 2026-10-06, with removing a sent report and the two warnings added the same day (phase 4, part A). Ran on an emulator, the migration to database version 5 included, never on the phone. **No email draft has been seen anywhere:** Gmail on the emulator had no account. Device checks 177 to 207, and 208 to 231 for part A · **Platforms:** Android (phone) · **Last updated:** 2026-10-06

**What it does**
Makes the report for the accountant, for a month or for a date range, as a PDF and as a CSV file, hands the PDF to the email app, and remembers which reports Shawn says he sent.

**Required behaviour**
- Shawn picks a specific month or a custom date range, not only the current month, and generates a PDF of the Business trips in it. The date range was added on 2026-10-05 ("be able to pick specific month or date range"). Built with Android's own `PdfDocument`, no third-party PDF library.
- Header: name, company, vehicle, month, generated date.
- One table per day with columns **Start, End, From, To, km**, then a daily subtotal. A month total km at the end.
- **No rate and no amount owed.** Accounts works the money out, so the report shows km only.
- No purpose column and no odometer readings.
- A blank signature line.
- Manual or edited trips carry an asterisk, with a legend.
- Send button opens Gmail with the accounts address, the subject "Mileage - [Month Year] - [Name]" (for a date range, the range in words), and the PDF attached. Shawn taps send himself.
- After sending, the month is marked Submitted with the date. Each month shows submitted or not submitted. Resubmitting a month warns first, is allowed, and is labelled a revision.
- CSV export for any month or date range.
- **Settled at phase 3 kickoff:** a date-range report never marks a month as Submitted, whatever days it covers; only a whole-month report does. Every report he confirms sending, of either kind, is kept in a list he can see. And because Android cannot tell an app whether an email was sent, MilO asks "Did you send it?" when he comes back, and marks the month only on yes.
- **Settled for phase 4, part A:** a report that was recorded as sent can be removed from the list again, after a question that says what removing it does; sending a month that has not ended warns first; and the Trips screen says when a submitted month's Business trips have changed since its report was sent.

**How it works today**
*The way in.* The Trips screen's month card has a line that says whether the month's report has been sent ("Not submitted", "Submitted on Oct 6, 2026", or "Submitted on Oct 6, 2026. Sent again on Oct 9, 2026 (revision 1).") and under it the button "Report for the accountant". It opens the Report screen for the month the card is showing, with a Back arrow; the bottom bar keeps marking Trips.

*The Report screen* (`feature/report/`). Four cards.
1. **Period.** One of two: "A whole month" or "A date range".
   - A month is stepped with Previous month and Next month, never past the current one, like the Trips screen. Under its name stands the same line as on the Trips card.
   - A date range has two buttons, "From: (day)" and "To: (day)", each opening the calendar. No day after today can be chosen. Both days are inside the range, and a range can be one day. It starts as the days of the month in view (in the current month, up to today). **A first day that is later than the last takes the last day along,** and the other way round, so a range never runs backwards and nothing is refused.
2. **In this report.** What a report made now would hold: "193 Business trips · 2219.1 km"; how many of them carry an asterisk; how many lack a From or a To address, with what the report says in their place and where to type one in; and what is left off: how many Personal trips, how many trips not marked Business or Personal yet, and the trip in progress if it started in the period. A period without Business trips says so. The card follows storage: a trip that ends or is edited while the screen is open changes it.
3. **Create and send.**
   - **Create PDF** makes the PDF and says "PDF created: 13 pages." Then **Open PDF** shows it in whatever app the phone shows a PDF with, so that it can be looked at before it is sent. The line and the button are there only while the PDF is of the report as it is now.
   - **Send to accountant**, the screen's main button, makes a fresh PDF and opens the email app with it (below). Under it: "Opens your email app with the PDF attached, addressed to (address). Nothing is sent until you press send there." Before it sends it asks, where there is something to ask: about a month that has not ended, and about a period that was sent before ("Sending", steps 5 and 6).
   - **Export CSV** makes the CSV and offers it to Android's share sheet (Drive, Files, an email app): a CSV has no one place to go.
   - **What a report needs and does not have** is said in this card from the start, in red, with the button "Open Settings": "Your name is not set, and it is printed on the report. Set it in Settings, under "Report for the accountant"." and "The accountant's email address is not set, so there is nowhere to send the report. …". Create PDF and Open PDF need the name. Send needs the name and the address. Export CSV needs neither. A press that lacks what it needs makes nothing, and the screen moves to those lines.
   - A press that did not work says so in the card until the next press: the file could not be made, or the phone has no email app, no PDF viewer or nothing to share with.
4. **Sent reports.** Every report he has said he sent, newest first: the period, "Sent on Oct 6, 2026 · 193 trips", "Revision 1" where it is one, and the km. The figures are the ones the report had when it was sent. Each line has a button, **Remove**, for a report that was recorded by mistake ("Removing a sent report", below).

*Which trips are on a report* (`selectForReport` in `data/report/ReportSelection.kt`, a pure function).
1. A trip is on the report if it **counts** (a finished trip: not one that is being recorded, discarded or deleted; `isCounted`), is **saved as Business**, and **started** in the period, in the phone's time zone at that moment.
2. **Only the start decides,** as it decides the day and the month a trip is listed under on the Trips screen. A trip that began at 23:50 on the last day is on the report whole, under that day; one that began before the first day and ran into it is not.
3. A month runs from local midnight on the 1st up to, not including, local midnight on the next 1st. A date range runs from local midnight of its first day to the local midnight that ends its last day. Both are worked out from calendar days, so a period in which the clocks change is as long as it really was.
4. The trips are listed oldest first, grouped under the day they started on.

*The figures.* **The report adds up the kilometres it prints.** Each trip is rounded to a tenth of a kilometre once, and a day's subtotal and the period's total are sums of those rounded figures, so that whoever adds a column up gets the figure printed under it. **Since 2026-10-06 (phase 4) the screens add up the same way,** through the same function (`sumOfTenths` in `core/util/DistanceFormat.kt`): the month's Business figure on the Trips screen is the report's total, each day's heading its subtotal, and Home's Today and the Android Auto row follow the same rule. Before that the screens rounded a sum of metres once, and a month could read a kilometre more or less on Trips than on its report (1.0 km over 193 trips in the test data, which was made to round badly). The Report screen says in one line how its total is made.

*The PDF* (`core/report/` decides everything; `platform/report/ReportPdf.kt` draws it).
1. **Paper:** US Letter, upright, with margins of three quarters of an inch.
2. **The first page starts with** the title "Mileage report", then Name, Company, Vehicle, Period and Generated, each on a line. A company or a vehicle that is not set is left off, label and all. Under them: "Business trips only. Distances are in kilometres." A report for a period that was sent before adds "Revision 1. It replaces the report sent on October 6, 2026."
3. **One table for each day that has a trip:** the day ("Tuesday, September 1, 2026"), the column titles Start, End, From, To and km over a rule, one row for each trip, and under a thin rule the day's Subtotal. The kilometres end at one edge so that the decimal points stand under each other.
4. **Long addresses wrap inside their column,** and the row grows to hold them. A single word that is wider than the column is cut where the line is full. The two time columns and the km column are as wide as their widest entry, so "10:45 p.m." fits as surely as "22:45"; the two addresses share what is left.
5. **A trip without an address says "No address recorded"** in its place. A position is never printed.
6. **A trip that was added or edited by hand has an asterisk** after its kilometres, and the report then ends with the legend "* This trip was added by hand, or changed by hand after it was recorded." There is one legend for both, so it says nothing that is untrue of either: a recorded trip whose distance was corrected was recorded. A trip that was only marked Business by hand has none.
7. **At the end:** "Total kilometres for September 2026" with the total, the number of business trips, the legend if there is one, and a blank line to sign on with a shorter one for the date beside it. No rate and no amount.
8. **Every page ends with** the name and the period on the left and "Page 3 of 13" on the right.
9. **Where the pages break** (`layoutReport`):
   - A day that fits on a page is not split. If it does not fit in what is left of the page, it starts the next one.
   - A day that is longer than a page is split between two of its trips and goes on at the top of the next page under its heading, repeated with "(continued)", and the column titles.
   - A heading never stands alone at the foot of a page, a trip is never cut, and a day's subtotal is on the same page as the day's last trip.
   - The total, the legend and the signature line stay together.
10. Times of day are written as the phone writes them ("Use 24-hour format"), like on the Trips screen; dates and decimal separators follow the phone's language.
11. The file is `Mileage-2026-09-(name).pdf`, for a date range `Mileage-2026-09-03-to-2026-09-04-(name).pdf`, and for a revision it ends in `-rev1`. Only the letters and digits of the name are used.

*The CSV* (`reportCsv` in `core/report/ReportCsv.kt`). A row of titles (Date, Start, End, From, To, km, By hand), then one row for each trip of the same report, oldest first. Made for a spreadsheet: the date is written year first (2026-09-03), the times on a 24-hour clock, the kilometres with a dot, whatever the phone's language; a missing address is an empty cell; the last column says "added" or "edited" for a trip with an asterisk; there are no subtotals. An address with a comma or a quotation mark is quoted as the CSV standard asks, and a cell that would be run as a formula is made plain text. The file starts with the mark that tells Excel it is UTF-8. **A CSV is never a revision:** it is the period's trips as they are now, it replaces nothing that was sent, and exporting it sends nothing. So its file is `Mileage-2026-09-(name).csv` and the share sheet's title "Mileage - September 2026 - (name)" also for a period that was sent before, where the PDF would be `…-rev1.pdf` (`ReportSources.reportFor`).

*Sending.*
1. **Send to accountant** makes the PDF, remembers what is being handed over (the period, the number of trips, the total, the time), and opens the email app with the accountant's address, the subject and a first few lines filled in and the PDF attached. **MilO sends nothing itself** and has no INTERNET permission: Shawn presses send in the email app.
2. **The subject** is "Mileage - September 2026 - (name)"; for a date range "Mileage - September 3, 2026 to September 4, 2026 - (name)"; and a report that replaces one already sent ends in " - Revision 1".
3. **Which app.** Gmail, if the phone has it; otherwise whatever app handles email. If there is none the screen says so and nothing is remembered. The email app opens on top of MilO, so Back leads back to the Report screen.
4. **"Did you send it?"** Android tells an app nothing about what became of an email. So when Shawn is back on the Report screen MilO asks, naming the period and the day the email app was opened: "I sent it" or "Not sent".
   - **I sent it** records the report in the list of sent reports, as sent on the day the email app was opened. For a whole month that is what makes the month Submitted.
   - **The question says what "I sent it" will do,** in one of three ways (`sentEffect` in `data/report/Submission.kt`). For a month that was never sent: "If you sent it, the month is marked as submitted on that day." For a month that was sent before: "If you sent it, it is added to the list of sent reports as Revision 1. The month stays marked as submitted on Oct 6, 2026, the day its first report was sent." A revision moves no date, so none is promised. For a date range: that it is added to the list, and marks no month.
   - **Not sent** records nothing, and ends the question.
   - **A tap beside the question, or Back, is not an answer.** The question is put off and comes again the next time he is back on the Report screen, or when he presses Send again: one question at a time.
   - **The question outlives MilO.** What was handed over is kept in the settings file until it is answered. If Android ends MilO while the email app is open, or the email app closes MilO's screen with its own, the question is asked the next time a Report screen is opened, for whichever month.
5. **Sending a period again** asks first: "Send this report again?", with the day it was last sent, its trips and km, and the number the new report will carry. Cancel sends nothing. The new report is labelled a revision on the PDF, in the email's subject, in the file's name and in the list. The first report stays in the list as it was.
6. **Sending a month that has not ended** asks first (since 2026-10-06, phase 4): "Send before the month has ended?", naming the month and its last day, and saying what it costs: "A report sent now lists the Business trips up to this moment. Trips you make later this month are then on no report until you send the month again, as a revision." "Send now" sends; Cancel sends nothing. A month has not ended while its last day is today or still to come: on the 31st the month's last trips may not have been driven yet. The Report screen opens on the month the Trips card shows, which is the current one unless he stepped back, so this is the month he is one press away from sending. A date range is not asked about: its days were picked one by one, none later than today, and it marks no month. A month that is both unfinished and sent before asks the two questions one after the other, this one first; "no" to either sends nothing. Which questions are asked, and in which order, is one pure function (`sendQuestions` in `feature/report/SendQuestion.kt`).

*Submitted.* Nothing stores "this month is submitted". The list of sent reports is the record (`sent_reports`, ARCHITECTURE section 6), and one function reads it for both screens (`monthSubmission`): a month is submitted once a report for the **whole month** has been sent, on the day of the first one; later ones are its revisions. **A date range never marks a month,** not even one from the 1st to the last day. The same rule gives a date range that is sent twice a revision number of its own. A month whose only report is removed from the list is not submitted again by that alone, and a month whose first report was removed while a revision is left reads "Submitted on Oct 9, 2026 (revision 1)": the revision keeps its number.

*Removing a sent report* (since 2026-10-06, phase 4). "I sent it" used to be for good; a test email, or a draft that was thrown away after the answer, left a month Submitted with nothing in the accountant's inbox.
1. Each line of "Sent reports" has a text button, **Remove**. It asks first, "Remove this sent report?", and the question says what removing this one does, which depends on what else the list holds (`removalEffect` in `data/report/Submission.kt`):
   - **the month's only report:** "…is deleted from this list, and September 2026 goes back to "Not submitted"."
   - **one of several reports for a month:** "…is deleted from this list. September 2026 stays submitted, on (the day the earliest report still listed was sent), because another report for it is in the list. The reports that stay keep their revision numbers."
   - **a date range:** "…is deleted from this list. A date range marked no month, so nothing else changes."
   Every one of the three ends: "Use this if you did not send it after all. No trip is changed, and MilO takes back no email: one that was sent stays sent. There is no undo here." Cancel, Back and a tap beside the question remove nothing.
2. **What Remove does.** One row of `sent_reports` is deleted (`SentReportRepository.remove`), and nothing else is written anywhere: no trip, no other report, no setting. Whether the month is submitted follows from the list as before, so the Report screen and the Trips card change by themselves.
3. **Revision numbers are not handed out again to the reports that stay.** Revision 1 stays revision 1 when the original is removed. The next report for the period takes the number after the highest one still in the list, so with the original removed the next is revision 2, and with the newest revision removed its number is free again: right, when that revision was never sent, which is what Remove is for.
4. **The event log** gets one `REPORT` line: "Sent report removed from the list by hand, on the Report screen: 2026-09, the first report for this period, 193 trips, 2219.1 km. The month is no longer marked as submitted. No trip and no email was touched" (or "…The month stays marked as submitted: 1 other report(s) for it are still in the list (revision 1), and keep their numbers…", or "…A date range marked no month as submitted…"). A removal that storage refused is an `ERROR` line, and the card then says "That report could not be removed. The Log screen says why."
5. **It is not an undo for the email,** and there is no "put it back": a report removed by mistake is recorded again by sending it again.

*A sent month that has changed* (since 2026-10-06, phase 4). The list keeps the figures a report had when it was sent. When a submitted month's Business trips are no longer those, the Trips screen's month card says so under "Submitted on …", in red: "Changed since the report was sent. It listed 4 trips and 51.7 km; the month now has 3 trips and 50.7 km." and under it "If your accountant needs the new figures, send the report again. It goes out as a revision."
1. **What it goes by** is what is already stored (`changedSinceSent` in `data/report/Submission.kt`): the number of Business trips and the total that the month's **newest** report listed (`tripCount` and `distanceMetres` of its row), held against the month's Business trips as they are now, added up the report's way. No column and no table was added for it.
2. **What it notices:** a Business trip added, deleted, restored, counted after all or marked Personal, a Personal trip marked Business, and a distance changed enough to print differently, since the newest report.
3. **What it cannot notice:** a change that leaves both figures as they were. A start or end time or an address that was edited (they are on the report, and are not in the row of the sent report); a distance changed by less than shows in a tenth of a kilometre; two changes that cancel out, such as one trip marked Personal and another of the same printed length marked Business. Nothing stores when a trip was last changed. The line also goes away when the month is sent again, and when its report is removed from the list.
4. It is on the Trips card only. The Report screen shows the same two sets of figures in "In this report" and "Sent reports", without the sentence.

*The event log* gets a `REPORT` line for each step: a PDF or a CSV made ("PDF for 2026-09 created: 193 trips, 2219.1 km"), the report handed to the email app, and the answer ("Report for 2026-09 recorded as sent, on Shawn's word: the first report for this period, 193 trips, 2219.1 km. The month is now marked as submitted", or "…: answered "not sent" after the email app. Nothing was recorded"). Removing a report from the list leaves one more (above). A failure is an `ERROR` line. The lines name the period and the figures, and never a name, an address or the accountant.

**Edge cases & gotchas**
- **Never run on the phone, and never with an email account.** On an emulator (2026-10-06, FINDINGS_LOG) the PDF was made, pulled to the Mac and read page by page; Gmail's compose screen was opened with a read permission for the one file; the question was asked and answered each way. What Gmail does with the address, the subject, the text and the attachment has not been seen: Google documents none of it. Device checks 177 to 207.
- **MilO only knows what Shawn tells it.** A month marked Submitted means he pressed "I sent it", nothing more. A draft he left unsent, or an email that bounced, looks the same.
- **A report recorded by mistake is removed from the list** ("Removing a sent report"). **One answered "Not sent" by mistake can still only be recorded by sending it again:** there is no "mark as sent" without an email (`[DEBT]`, FINDINGS_LOG 2026-10-06, phase 4). A tap beside the question is safe: it only puts the question off. The device checks for sending were written while "I sent it" was for good, and still press it only for a spare month without trips (checks 190 to 199); they are left as they are until Remove has been seen to work on the phone (check 228).
- **The list keeps what was sent, not what the trips are now.** A trip edited after its month was sent changes the Trips screen and the next report, and not the row of the report that was sent. That is what a revision is for.
- **A month that has not ended can still be sent,** after the question ("Sending", step 6). A report sent on 28 October leaves the month "Submitted on Oct 28"; the trips of the 29th to the 31st are then on no report until he sends the month again as a revision. What reminds him now: the month card on Trips turns red once such a trip has made the month differ from what was sent ("A sent month that has changed"). What does not: the monthly reminder, which only looks at whether last month has a report at all.
- **The report's total and the Trips screen's month figure are the same figure** since 2026-10-06 (phase 4): one function adds both up. They can still differ if the phone's time zone changes between looking at one and at the other, because the month is cut at local midnight.
- **Personal trips and trips not marked yet are never on a report.** The Report screen counts them so that a trip that should have been Business is noticed before the report is sent: it is marked on the Trips screen.
- **An address still being looked up is "No address recorded" on a report made now.** The Report screen says how many trips lack one. Waiting, or typing the address in on the edit screen, puts it on the next report.
- **The trip in progress is not on a report,** and the screen says so if it started in the period.
- **A trip that ran past midnight** is listed under the day it started, with the time it ended and no note that the end is on the next day.
- **The time zone is the phone's at the moment the report is made,** for which trips are in the period, for the day a trip is under and for the times printed. A report made in another time zone than the trips were driven in can move a trip near midnight to the neighbouring day, or out of the period.
- **The files are in the cache,** which Android empties by itself when the phone is short of room, and MilO removes them after seven days. A file is made again when it is needed. A draft that waits in the email app while its file is removed may lose its attachment.
- **A day's table is kept whole, which leaves white space** at the foot of a page when the next day does not fit: 13 pages for 193 trips with long addresses. Only a day longer than a page is split.
- **The email app's first few lines** ("Attached is my mileage report for September 2026: 2219.1 km in 193 business trips.") were not in the brief. He can change or delete them before sending; they are one string in `strings.xml`.
- **If Gmail is not the phone's only email app,** the report still goes to Gmail first. A phone without Gmail gets whatever takes email, and Android may then ask which app.
- **Open PDF and Export CSV never ask "Did you send it?".** Only the email app does. A PDF shared from the viewer, or a CSV emailed from the share sheet, is not recorded anywhere.
- **The PDF's words are English,** from `strings.xml`, and its dates and decimal separator follow the phone's language. With the phone set to another language the two would mix.
- **The footer is one line.** A name and a period too long to stand beside the page number are cut short there with an ellipsis; the heading has the name whole, and a long vehicle description wraps in the heading.
- A period of several years makes a long PDF in one go, on one background thread. Not measured.

## Diagnostics and reminders

**Status:** In progress: the event log is stored, shown, filtered by category, shared as a text file and trimmed; crashes and process kills are captured into it at every start; and the monthly reminder is built (phase 4, part A, 2026-10-06). The reminder, the share, the filter and the trimming ran on an emulator, **never on the phone**, where nothing records the Log screen being drawn at all. Device checks 1 to 7, 73 to 75 and 208 to 226 · **Platforms:** Android · **Last updated:** 2026-10-06

**Required behaviour**
- A bare event log (the stored events and a plain list screen) ships in phase 1, because without it a missed trip start during the test drives cannot be diagnosed. Phase 4 finishes it.
- Event log screen: every Bluetooth connect and disconnect, Android Auto change, and service start and stop, with a timestamp. Crashes are written here too, because the app has no crash-reporting service.
- If the truck connects during scheduled hours and a trip fails to start, notify Shawn.
- A reminder notification on the 1st of each month (day configurable) to submit the previous month.

**How it works today**
1. **A crash.** `MiloApplication` installs `CrashHandler` as the process's uncaught-exception handler. When any thread throws, the handler writes one small text file (time, thread, exception, stack trace) to `no_backup/crashes/`, forces it to disk, and then passes the exception on to Android's own handler, which ends the process as usual.
2. **The next start, however it happens.** `MiloApplication.onCreate` launches three things on a background thread, one after the other. First `BackupAftermath.settle()` (since phase 4, part B): it writes what Android's backup left to tell into the log and, after a restore, puts the restored settings right for this phone ([Backup, export and import](#backup-export-and-import)); on a start with nothing to tell it does nothing. Last, after the diagnostics, `DataTransfer.finishCutOffImport()` (since the review of part B): it finishes an import that the process before this one was ended in the middle of, and on any other start does nothing. In between, `StartupDiagnostics.record()`. It:
   - copies every waiting crash file into the event log as a `CRASH` entry, dated when the crash happened, and deletes the file;
   - asks Android for its record of why earlier MilO processes ended (`ActivityManager.getHistoricalProcessExitReasons`), keeps the ones newer than the last one already imported, and writes each as a `PROCESS` entry with the reason and the system's description text. On this phone the description is where a Xiaomi cleaner names itself;
   - writes a `PROCESS` entry for the new process.
   - **trims the log** (since 2026-10-06, phase 4): removes the lines that are too old to keep ("Trimming", below), and writes a `PROCESS` line saying how many, on the starts that removed any.
3. Each record is written to the log and then marked done, one record at a time. A process that dies half-way imports at most that one record again. A duplicate line is possible; a lost one is not.
4. **Gathering evidence never stops the app.** Each of the four steps runs on its own. A step that throws is written to the log as an `ERROR` entry with its stack trace, and the next step still runs. If the event log itself cannot be written, the failure goes to a crash file (so it reaches the log at a later start) and the remaining steps are skipped, since all of them need the log.
5. `EventLogRepository` is the only way to write or read the log. Entries carry a time, a category, a one-line message and an optional detail.
6. **Trip recording writes the evidence ADR-002 asks for.** Everything goes through the trip controller's inbox, so the lines are in the order things happened:
   - `TRIGGER`: every trigger, with its source in the message and the state before and after in the detail. The source says which receiver or service heard it and names the device, for example "Bluetooth receiver: ACL connected for the truck (AA:BB:…)". A trigger that read the truck's connection adds how the answer was reached, in brackets. Also: a Bluetooth broadcast or companion callback for a device that is not the truck, marked "Ignored"; a trigger that had to start the service first ("asked Android for the trip service"); a reading that came back unknown; a once-a-minute reading that was not believed; and a reading of "not connected" taken before any reading had shown the truck connected. The lines are dated when they are written, so they read in the order things happened. Two things are left out because they arrive all day and usually change nothing: GPS fixes, and a once-a-minute reading that only confirms what was believed.
   - `TRIP`: a trip starting; the truck being seen in it; a trip finishing or being discarded, with the distance, the reason and the fix counts; the hold-off being set and released, with the reason. Since 2026-10-06 the line for a trip that closes also says what the work schedule made of it and what it went by ("…; saved as Business: it started on a Tue at 10:14:05; that day's hours are 08:00 to 16:30 (America/Edmonton)"), and a trip left out by "Ignore them" says so in place of "finished" (see [Schedule and Business/Personal](#schedule-and-businesspersonal)). Also every trip deleted, restored or counted by hand on the Trips screen, every trip marked Business or Personal there, and every such press that was refused (see [Trip log](#trip-log-home-day-and-month-views)). Those lines are written by the screen, not through the trip controller's inbox. And one line for a pass of the catch-up that sorted trips recorded earlier, with each trip in its detail; that one is written by the catch-up itself.
   - `GRACE`: the grace period starting and being cancelled. Its running out is the `TRIP` line "ended by GRACE_EXPIRED".
   - `SERVICE`: the service reaching the foreground and for which trigger; a start that failed and why, with the exception; the service being destroyed; a service lost in mid-trip, and the trip being dropped from memory when Android will not start it again; MilO being removed from the recent apps; the trip-start sound playing or failing. Also, from the Settings screen: the sound being changed to a file of Shawn's or back to the bundled chirp, and each press of Play ("Settings screen, Play pressed. Trip-start sound: playing the chosen sound").
   - `ANDROID_AUTO`: every change `CarConnection` reports, with the raw value, and the state before and after. Also the life of the Android Auto screen: its service created and destroyed, each session created (with the host's package name and the Car API level agreed) and destroyed, the screen shown and no longer shown, and each time it told the driver that a trip could not start (see [Android Auto screen](#android-auto-screen)). A press of the car's button is a `TRIGGER` line, like the phone's.
   - `LOCATION`: fixes being requested and stopped, the time to the first fix and its accuracy, and location being lost for 10 seconds or more and coming back. A shorter loss is not logged: the emulator reports one around every fix.
   - `ERROR`: a failure inside the controller, with its stack trace; an unreadable settings file; a failure of the Android Auto screen (its own code, today's trips unreadable, or a call the car answered with an error); an audio file that was refused as the trip-start sound, with what Android said about it; a change on the Settings or the Trips screen that storage refused; a catch-up of Business and Personal that could not be made ("Sorting earlier trips into Business and Personal failed. They stay unsorted"); a failure inside the driving alert ("The driving alert failed while …"), and a request to report driving that the phone refused; a report that could not be made, or an answer to "Did you send it?" that could not be stored.
   - `PAIRING`: the truck being paired or adopted, a pairing that failed and why, and every check of the pairing with its result. While no truck is armed, the line also lists the phone's paired devices with their addresses, names an association that was not adopted, and says if the phone has no companion device support.
   - `DRIVING` (since 2026-10-06): one line for every delivery of reports from the phone's driving detection, with what was done about it and why, and the facts it went by in the detail; and one line whenever what MilO asks of the phone changes ("watching for driving", or why not). Written by the driving alert itself, not through the trip controller's inbox. A request the phone refused, and a failure inside the alert, are `ERROR` lines. See [Safety net](#safety-net-manual-button-and-driving-alert).
   - `REPORT` (since 2026-10-06): a PDF or a CSV of a report made, a report handed to the email app, and Shawn's answer to "Did you send it?", with the period, the number of trips and the km (see [Monthly PDF and submission](#monthly-pdf-and-submission)). Written by the Report screen, not through the trip controller's inbox. They name no person and no address. Since phase 4 also a report removed from the list of sent reports, and the monthly reminder: the daily alarm being asked for, each reminder shown, and a look that showed nothing, with the reason ("The monthly reminder", below). The reminder writes its lines itself.
   - **Since phase 4, part B, with no new category** ([Backup, export and import](#backup-export-and-import)): `REPORT` also has the lines of an export written, of a picked file that was refused for an import (with what is wrong with it), and of an import: one line written in the very transaction that replaces the trips ("An import has replaced the 54 trips and 2 sent reports this phone held with …"), one when it is done (what replaced what, and the name of the safety copy), and, if MilO was ended in between, one from the next start that says what became of it. `PROCESS` has "Android collected MilO's data for a cloud backup …" and, after a restore, "MilO's data was put back by Android …". `PAIRING` has what became of the truck after a restore or an import. `ERROR` has a backup that Android refused for its size, and any failure of an export or an import ("MilO's backup, export or import failed while …"). They name no person, no address and no position; the truck is named by its Bluetooth address, as in every pairing line.
   - `ADDRESS`: every lookup of a trip's start and end address with what happened to each end and, if it failed, which attempt of four it was; a lookup for a trip in progress; a pass put off because the phone had no network. These are not written through the trip controller's inbox, so they are dated when written and can sit minutes or days after the trip they name. They name no address and no position.

7. **The Log screen** (`feature/eventlog/`), the last button of the bottom bar: the stored log, newest first.
   - Each line shows its time as `2026-10-05 08:14:03` (to the second, 24-hour, in the phone's own time zone, the same form in every language), its category by its stored name (`CRASH` and `ERROR` in red) and its message.
   - A line that has a detail says "Tap for details". Pressing it opens the detail under the line: the state before and after a trigger, a stack trace. Pressing again closes it. One line is open at a time.
   - **It is never read whole.** The newest 200 entries are read, through the index on the time. "Show older entries" at the bottom adds 200 more each time. The list is drawn lazily, so only the lines on screen are laid out. It opens as fast with ten thousand entries as with ten.
   - It follows the log: a line written while the screen is open appears at the top.
   - A setup button that could not open its HyperOS screen writes an `ERROR` line here (see [Permission checklist](#permission-checklist)).
   - **A filter by category** (since 2026-10-06, phase 4). Under the title stand the categories as chips, "All" first and then each by its stored name (`PROCESS`, `CRASH`, `ERROR`, `TRIGGER`, …), wrapped onto as many lines as they need. Tapping one shows that category's lines only; "All" shows every line again. The filter is part of the query, not a sieve over the 200 lines in hand, so a category's lines are found however far back they are. "Show older entries" works inside a category. A category without a line says "The log has no CRASH line."
   - **Share the whole log** (since 2026-10-06, phase 4), a text button above the chips: see "Sharing the log", below.

*Trimming* (since 2026-10-06, phase 4; `EventLogRepository.trim`, called by the start-up import as its last step).
1. **A line is removed when it is older than 90 days and is not among the newest 1,000 lines.** Both must hold (`trimBeforeMs`, a pure function). Ninety days is longer than any question about a trip waits; the log shares `milo.db` with the trips, and that file has to stay small for the backup of part B.
2. **The newest 1,000 lines stay whatever their age.** A line's age is judged by the phone's clock, and one start with the clock set far ahead must not empty the log; and a MilO that was not used for a few months still holds its last days. On the phone as it is used, 1,000 lines are about a week of driving, so this only matters in those two cases.
3. It runs at every process start, after that start's own lines are written. A start that removed something says so: "Event log trimmed: 841 lines older than 90 days removed. The newest 1000 lines are kept whatever their age". Most starts remove nothing and write nothing.
4. **What goes with an old line:** the trigger lines of a trip older than 90 days, and the before-and-after of a trip edited that long ago, which survive nowhere else ([Trip log](#trip-log-home-day-and-month-views), "Editing a trip", step 12). A trip and what was recorded of it are not in the log and are not trimmed.

*Sharing the log* (since 2026-10-06, phase 4), so that a log can be sent to whoever is helping without plugging the phone into the Mac.
1. "Share the whole log" writes the log to a text file and opens Android's share sheet with it. Where it goes is Shawn's choice: an email to himself, Drive, a chat. MilO sends nothing itself.
2. **The file** is `MilO-log-2026-10-06.txt`, named by the day it was written; a second share on the same day replaces it. It starts with three lines that say what it is, when it was written, which time zone its times are in and how many lines it holds, and then has **every line of the log, oldest first, whatever the filter is set to**: the time as the Log screen writes it, the category, the message, and a detail on lines of its own, set in. Plain text, UTF-8.
3. **Where it is written, and who can read it.** Into `cache/reports/`, beside the report files, complete or not at all, and it is handed out by the file provider of the report files (`ReportHandOff.toShareText`): the app that is picked gets a permission to read that one file, and nothing else of MilO's. Like a report file it is removed after seven days, the next time the Report screen is opened, and Android may empty the cache sooner.
4. **What the file gives away** is said under the button before it is pressed: the log names the truck and the phone's other paired devices (with their Bluetooth addresses), and spells out every address that was typed or replaced on the edit screen, the one before and the one after. It holds no GPS position, and the lines of the address lookup name no address.
5. The log is read from storage a few hundred lines at a time while the file is written, so its size does not matter. A file that could not be written says so under the button ("The log could not be written to a file. Its newest line says why.") and leaves an `ERROR` line; a phone with nothing to share with says that.

*The monthly reminder* (`platform/reminder/`, since 2026-10-06, phase 4). The brief: "Reminder notification on the 1st of each month (configurable) to submit the previous month."
1. **What it is.** A notification, "Mileage report for September 2026" / "It has not been sent to your accountant yet. Tap to open it.", on a channel of its own ("Monthly reminder", default importance: the phone's notification sound, and no pop-up over what Shawn is doing). **A tap opens MilO on the Report screen for that month,** as if it had been opened from Trips: Back leads to Trips and then to Home. The tap sends nothing.
2. **When it is shown** is one pure function, `judgeReminder` (`ReminderRules.kt`). All of this must hold: the reminder is switched on in Settings; today is this month's reminder day or later; no report for the **whole** of last month is recorded as sent (`monthSubmission`: a date range does not count, whatever days it covers); last month has at least one trip its report would list (`selectForReport`: finished, Business, started in that month); and the reminder for last month has not been shown yet today. So it comes **once a day, from the reminder day to the end of the month, until the month is submitted or the reminder is switched off.** At the turn of the month it moves on: on 1 November "last month" is October, and September is not reminded of again.
3. **The reminder day** is the 1st out of the box and can be any day from 1 to 31 ([Settings](#settings)). In a month that is shorter than the day chosen, the month's last day stands in: a reminder set to the 31st starts on 30 April, on 28 February, and on 29 February in a leap year (`reminderStartsOn`).
4. **It is decided by what is true when MilO looks, not by an alarm for a date.** MilO looks once a day, when its daily alarm arrives; at every process start (the phone booted, MilO was updated, or anything else woke it); every time MilO comes to the front; when the reminder's Settings card is changed; and when a report is recorded as sent. The answer is the same however often it looks, so a phone that was off on the 1st, a changed day and a changed time zone sort themselves out at the next look. "Today" and "last month" are worked out in the phone's time zone at that moment.
5. **The daily alarm** is one inexact alarm from Android's own alarm service (`AlarmManager.set`, type `RTC`), asked for at 09:00 of the next morning. Android may deliver it up to about an hour late, and does not wake a sleeping phone for it: it then arrives when the phone is next picked up. It needs no permission; MilO declares no exact-alarm permission. There is no WorkManager in the build (ADR-001; the research had proposed a daily WorkManager job with the same state-based decision). Android forgets every alarm at a reboot and when an app is force-stopped, so the alarm is asked for again at every process start, and each alarm that arrives asks for the next. It is delivered to a manifest receiver (`ReminderReceiver`), so it can start MilO's process.
6. **When it is taken away.** A tap removes it. Otherwise it is withdrawn at the next look that finds a reason not to show it other than "already shown today": the month's report recorded as sent (at once, as "I sent it" is pressed), the reminder switched off, the day moved past today, or last month left without a Business trip.
7. **What it stores** in the settings file: the two settings Shawn chooses (on or off, and the day), and one thing he does not choose, the month the reminder was last shown for and the day it was shown on, which is what keeps it to once a day across restarts of the process.
8. **It never touches a trip.** It reads the settings, the list of sent reports and last month's trips, and is handed neither the trip controller nor a way to write a trip. A failure inside it is written to the log as an `ERROR` line ("The monthly reminder failed while …") and goes no further, like the driving alert's. With a settings file that cannot be read it shows nothing, and says so once.
9. **The event log** gets `REPORT` lines: the alarm being asked for ("Monthly reminder: the next daily look is asked for at 2026-10-07 09:00:00, or within about an hour after it (process start). MilO also looks at every start and every time it is opened."); each reminder shown, with what it went by ("Monthly reminder shown for 2026-09 (the daily alarm): today, 2026-10-07, is on or after 2026-10-01, 2026-09 has 61 Business trips, and no report for it is recorded as sent."); and a look that showed nothing, with the first reason that applied ("Monthly reminder: nothing shown for 2026-09 (app opened): a report for 2026-09 is recorded as sent."). A look that finds what the last line already said writes nothing, so opening MilO ten times is not ten lines. A reminder nobody could see, because notifications are switched off for MilO or for this channel, says so in its line and still counts as that day's.

*If the truck connects and a trip fails to start.* The brief: "If the truck connects during scheduled hours and a trip fails to start, notify me." Nothing was built or changed for this in phase 4. This is what the "MilO could not start this trip. Tap to start" notification of phase 1 ([Truck pairing and trip detection](#truck-pairing-and-trip-detection)) covers and what it cannot, checked against the code on 2026-10-06.

**It is posted whenever MilO has decided that recording must begin and could not make it begin,** whatever prompted the start (the truck, the companion service, a reconcile, a button) and at any hour: it does not ask the work schedule, so it speaks outside the scheduled hours as well. The failures it covers:
- **the preflight found a reason** (`TripPreflight`): precise location not granted, location not set to "Allow all the time", location switched off for the whole phone, battery use set to Restricted, or Nearby devices not allowed;
- **Android refused to start the trip service** from the background (`TripServiceStarter`);
- **the service started and Android refused to let it enter the foreground** (`TripService`);
- **the service was lost in mid-trip and Android would not start it again.**

For each of them no trip is opened (or the open one is dropped from memory), a `SERVICE` line names the reason, Home and the Android Auto screen show it, and a tap on the notification starts the trip.

**It cannot cover a start that MilO never attempted, or a notification that is not seen:**
- **MilO was never woken.** If HyperOS does not start MilO's process when the truck connects (Autostart off, MilO force-stopped, or the phone not unlocked since a reboot), no code of MilO's runs, and a MilO that is not running cannot notify anyone. This is the failure the brief's sentence is most about, and no notification of MilO's own can reach it. What stands beside it: the Setup screen's rows; the driving alert, which itself needs the phone's driving detection to wake MilO; and the reconcile the next time MilO is opened, which starts the trip late if the truck is still connected.
- **The Bluetooth permission is missing.** Android then delivers no Bluetooth broadcast to MilO at all. Only the companion service's callback can still arrive, and that start does end in the preflight and the notification.
- **The truck connected and MilO decided not to start.** The hold-off after End trip is set; the reading of the truck came back "unknown" or "not connected" (a truck connected on neither profile MilO can ask); or the device that connected is not the paired truck. These are decisions with a `TRIGGER` line each, not failures, and bring no notification.
- **The trip started and came to nothing:** location was lost after the start, a companion start was never confirmed and was discarded as a false start, or the trip stayed under the minimum distance.
- **The notification was posted and not seen:** notifications switched off for MilO (the `SERVICE` line then says "the warning was not shown"), the "Trip problems" channel silenced, or Do Not Disturb.
- **MilO crashed while starting.** The crash is in the log at the next start; nothing was posted.


Checked on an emulator (Android 16) on 2026-10-03: an induced crash, a force stop and a background kill each appeared in the log at the next start, once. On 2026-10-05 the same emulator showed the trigger lines of the companion service, the boot and update reconcile and the pairing check (FINDINGS_LOG). On the POCO X5 the log has been written and read back over adb once (2026-10-05: a start refused with its reasons, the trip and sound lines of a trip started with the button, no crash and no error line); an induced crash and a kill have not been tried there (device checks 1 to 7). Nothing records the Log screen being drawn anywhere.

**Edge cases & gotchas**
- At most 20 crash files wait at once, so an app that crashes at every start cannot fill the phone. The first 20 are kept.
- Android keeps only a small number of exit records per app. If MilO is not started for a long time while being killed repeatedly, the oldest records are gone before they are imported.
- A crash produces two entries: the `CRASH` entry with the stack trace, and the system's `PROCESS` entry saying the process ended by crashing.
- **Lines older than 90 days are gone,** except the newest 1,000 ("Trimming"). A question about a trip from four months ago can no longer be answered from the log; the trip itself is untouched. The two numbers are constants in `EventLogRepository.kt`.
- The lines are in English and are not translated, on the Log screen too: the log is evidence.
- The Log screen has no search within a line. It has a filter by category, and the whole log can be shared as a text file and searched there. The filter shows one category or all of them, never two.
- **A shared log is out of MilO's hands.** It is a file in whatever app Shawn picked. It names the truck, the phone's other paired devices and every address typed or replaced on the edit screen; until 2026-10-06 the log never left the phone at all (ENGINEERING_STANDARDS section 12 says what leaves and how).
- **The reminder has never been seen on the phone.** On an emulator (2026-10-06) the alarm woke a MilO whose process had been killed, the notification was posted and its tap opened the Report screen. Whether HyperOS lets an alarm start MilO with Autostart off, whether it delivers the alarm at all to an app it has put to sleep, and whether the notification makes a sound there (MIUI is reported to switch channel sounds off) are device checks 215 to 219. If the alarm never arrives, the reminder still comes whenever MilO is opened or woken for anything else.
- **The daily alarm is another thing that starts MilO once a day.** A process started by it does what every process start does: the reconcile, the pairing check, the catch-ups, the driving alert's request.
- **The reminder is about last month only.** A month that was never sent stops being reminded of when the next month ends; nothing lists the months that are still open, except the Trips screen, month by month.
- **The reminder does not know that a sent month has changed.** A month with a report is "submitted" for it, however its trips have changed since; the Trips card is what says that ([Monthly PDF and submission](#monthly-pdf-and-submission)).
- **A reminder set to a day late in the month is short-lived:** set to the 28th it is shown for three or four days and then moves on to the next month.
- **The reminder comes while MilO is open, too.** Opening MilO on the reminder day posts the notification a moment later, while the screen is being looked at. It is the same look as every other.
- **A tap on the reminder while the edit screen holds something unsaved** asks "Leave without saving?" first, like every way out of that screen. "Discard" then opens the report; "Keep editing" stays, and the tap is forgotten.
- **The reminder's tap is a request to MilO's one activity** that names a month. Any app on the phone could send the same request; all it can do is open the Report screen on a month.
- Entries are ordered by the time they describe. A crash or a kill is written at the next start but dated when it happened, so it appears further down than the lines around its writing.
- During the hour that is repeated when the clocks go back, two lines an hour apart show the same time. Their order is still right.
- If the event log cannot be written while the trip controller is handling a failure, the process crashes on purpose, which leaves a crash file. A failure must not vanish.
- An unreadable settings file is never reset: a silent reset would lose the truck pairing without a trace. It also no longer crashes the start (that was a crash at every start, with no way out but clearing the app's data, trips included). The start-up import logs an `ERROR` entry and carries on, and the exit records are not imported while the file stays unreadable. Whatever reads the settings next has to deal with the failure itself, and each one that exists does. The trip controller logs it once and runs with the defaults. The pairing check reports `FAILED` with the reason, in its `PAIRING` line. The setup checklist logs an `ERROR` line and shows its rows without the confirmations. The Bluetooth receiver cannot tell whose broadcast it is, so it asks for a reading of the truck in place of trusting the event; that reading comes back unknown for the same reason. The companion service acts on its callback all the same, because Android only calls it for MilO's own association.
- If neither the event log nor the crash folder can be written, start-up does crash: there is nowhere left to record the failure.

## Backup, export and import

**Status:** In progress: built (phase 4, part B, 2026-10-06). Android's backup is switched on with rules of MilO's own, and the Settings screen has "Export all data" and "Import data". All of it ran on an emulator (Android 16), **never on the phone**: whether HyperOS ever backs MilO up, what its file picker looks like, and a move to a real second phone are open. Device checks 232 to 259 · **Platforms:** Android · **Last updated:** 2026-10-06

**Required behaviour**
- The main database (trips, submission status, settings, event log) is included in Android Auto Backup. Raw GPS points live in a second database file that stays out of cloud backup, because Auto Backup is capped at 25 MB and backs up nothing at all once that is exceeded.
- Manual export and import of all data to a file.

**The three copies, and what each depends on**

| Copy | Made by | Holds | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Android's cloud backup | Android, by itself, about once a day when the phone is idle, charging and on Wi-Fi | The trips, the sent reports, the event log, the settings. **Not the raw GPS points** | Backup being switched on in the phone's settings, Shawn's Google account, MilO staying under 25 MB, and **the signing key**: Android restores a backup only into a build signed with the same key |
| A transfer to another phone | Android, when a new phone is set up from the old one | The same, **and the raw GPS points** | Both phones in hand, the signing key |
| An export file | Shawn, with "Export all data" | Every closed trip, every sent report, the settings that mean the same on another phone, and the raw GPS points unless he switches them off. Not the event log | Nothing but the file. It is the one copy that is still good when the key, the account or the phone is gone |

*Android's backup* (`res/xml/data_extraction_rules.xml`, `platform/transfer/MiloBackupAgent`).
1. **What is taken** is decided by the rules file, with exclude lines only: Android takes everything of MilO's that is not excluded. So `milo.db` is never named there, and is taken together with the `-wal` file Room keeps beside it. A rule that named `milo.db` alone would leave that second file behind, and on an emulator every trip of a fresh install was in it (the main file was 4,096 bytes, the `-wal` file 716,912).
2. **Taken, in both cases:** `databases/milo.db` with `milo.db-wal`, and `files/datastore/settings.preferences_pb`, the settings.
3. **The raw GPS points** (`databases/points.db` with its three side files) are excluded from the cloud backup and included in a transfer to another phone. On the emulator MilO held a 28.7 MB points database and its cloud backup was 113,664 bytes.
4. **Never taken:** everything under `no_backup/` (crash files, MilO's copy of a chosen trip-start sound, the backup agent's notes, the safety copies, a file that is being imported) and the cache (report files, the log as a text file), both of which Android leaves out by itself; the `-shm` and `.lck` files beside each database; `files/profileInstalled`.
5. **Before the files are collected, each database is brought into one file.** The backup agent opens each database with SQLite itself (not through Room, so no migration runs and no table is touched) and moves the write-ahead log into the main file ("checkpoint, truncate"; `data/transfer/DatabaseSettling.kt`). What is copied is then one file that is whole by itself. On the emulator the backup of a MilO whose `milo.db` was 86,016 bytes with 1,660,392 bytes beside it in the log copied a `milo.db` of 110,592 bytes and an empty log.
6. **Android does not always end MilO before a backup.** The documentation says it does. On the emulator, `bmgr backupnow` with MilO alive ran the agent inside MilO's own process (the same process id), with Room's connections open beside it, and ended that process afterwards; with MilO not running it started a process for the backup. Step 5 is what makes the copy whole in the first case: once the log is empty nothing moves into the main file again before a thousand pages have been written, so the main file does not change in the moment the copy takes.
7. **A backup that is over 25 MB is written down.** Android then backs up nothing and tells nobody. The agent's `onQuotaExceeded` leaves a note, and the next start of MilO writes an `ERROR` line: "Android did not back MilO up: MilO's files are 30.1 MB and the limit is 25.0 MB. Nothing at all is backed up while MilO is over the limit, and the last backup that worked stays as it was. "Export all data" in Settings writes a copy that has no limit". Seen on the emulator with 30 MB of filler in `files/`.
8. **A backup that was collected is one line too:** a `PROCESS` line "Android collected MilO's data for a cloud backup: … Whether the copy arrived is Android's to say (Settings, Google, Backup)". It is the only sign inside MilO that Android backs it up at all. A backup that was refused for its size does not get this line.
9. **A backup Android makes by itself does not end a recording:** `backupInForeground` is left off, so Android's own schedule passes over an app with a foreground service. **A backup that is asked for does not look.** `adb shell bmgr backupnow` during a recording ended MilO's process on the emulator ("has died: fg FGS"); Android started the trip service again ten seconds later, and the trip's points have a gap of 16 seconds. Never run it during a trip. Whether "Back up now" in the phone's own Google settings takes the same path is not known, and HyperOS may not start the service again: do not press it during a trip either (device check 259 finds out, on a trip that does not matter).

*After a restore* (`platform/transfer/BackupAftermath`, at every process start).
1. Android restores when MilO is installed, before it first runs, in a process where MilO's own code is not set up. The agent's `onRestoreFinished` leaves a note; nothing else happens then.
2. **The first ordinary start deals with it.** The restored settings are those of another installation, and three things in them are not true here:
   - **The truck's companion association.** Its number is Android's, from the other phone. MilO asks Android which associations it holds here (`truckOnArrival`, a pure function). With none for the truck, **the truck's address and name are kept and the association's number is removed**, and the pairing check that follows reports "paired before, pair it again": Setup's row says "Work truck was paired, but Android no longer watches for it. Pair it again.", Home says "Setup needs attention", and the pairing screen offers the truck. MilO never believes it is paired because a file says so. If Android does hold an association for that truck here (a restore over an installation that was not removed), the truck stays paired.
   - **The confirmations of the setup checklist** (Autostart, Battery saver, Other permissions, the recents lock). They were Shawn's word about switches of the other phone, and an uninstall resets the same switches on this one. All four are removed, so Setup asks again.
   - **The chosen trip-start sound,** if its file is not here. It never is: the file is under `no_backup/`. The setting is removed, and the built-in sound plays.
3. Everything else is left as restored: the trips, the sent reports, the event log, the report's four settings, the schedule, the two numbers, the reminder, the date of the last export, a report that waits for "Did you send it?".
4. **Two lines in the log,** dated when the restore happened: `PROCESS` "MilO's data was put back by Android, from a backup or from another phone: … Taken out of the settings, because it was only true of the installation they came from: the confirmations of the setup checklist, and the chosen trip-start sound, whose file is in no backup. Go through Setup again on this phone", and `PAIRING` "After the restore: the truck (AA:BB:…) is known by address and name only. Android on this phone holds no association for it, so it has to be paired again. The association from the other installation was not kept".
5. **After a cloud restore there are no raw GPS points.** Every trip has its times, distance, positions and addresses on its own row, and "Restore recorded values" works from the row too (ARCHITECTURE, section 6), so nothing on a screen or a report changes.
6. **A restored database is opened like any other.** One from an older build is taken through the migrations, step by step; there is no destructive fallback. Seen on the emulator: a version 4 file restored by Android became version 5 at the first start with all 251 trips equal. One from a newer build is not opened: MilO dies at its start ("A migration from 6 to 5 was required but not found"), the file is left with every row, and the newer build reads it.

*Export all data* (the Settings screen's last card; `platform/transfer/DataTransfer`, `data/transfer/`).
1. "Export all data" opens Android's file picker with the name `MilO-export-2026-10-06.json`. Shawn chooses where it goes: Downloads, Drive. MilO needs no permission for that one file.
2. **One JSON document** (`data/transfer/ExportFormat.kt` describes it): what it is and which version of the form (`"format": "milo-export"`, `"formatVersion": 1`), when and by which MilO it was written, a sentence for whoever opens it, **how many of each thing it holds**, the settings, every sent report, every closed trip, and the raw points as rows of seven numbers. One thing on a line, so it can be read and compared.
3. **Every closed trip:** finished, discarded and deleted, with every column of the table, so a trip that is edited keeps what MilO recorded, a deleted one can still be restored on the Trips screen, and a trip sorted by hand stays so. A unit test holds the file against the tables' own column lists (`app/schemas`): a column that is added to a table and not to the file fails the build.
4. **The settings that mean the same on another phone:** the truck's address and name, the two numbers, the sound switch, the schedule and the choice for trips outside it, the driving alert's switch, the report's four, the reminder's two. Not the association, the sound's file, the confirmations, or any of the small pieces of state.
5. **"Include the GPS points"** is a switch on the card, on each time the screen is opened. Off, the file says `"points": null`. On the emulator 250 trips with 415,860 points were a file of 32.9 MB, written in about two seconds; without the points, 177 KB.
6. **Not while a trip is being recorded:** the buttons wait and the card says why. A trip in progress has no end and no distance yet.
7. **The file is whole or it is not there.** If the writing fails, or a trip starts while it is written, the file is removed again (or, if the app that holds it will not remove it, the card says that it is not a complete export). The same if a trip has started by the time Save is pressed: the file picker has made the file by then, empty, and MilO removes it.
8. **"Last export: Oct 6, 2026 at 1:29 PM"** stands on the card, with "without the GPS points" where that applies.
9. **A raw point that is stored for no closed trip is passed over, counted and said.** There should be none: they are what is left when the two databases have come apart (the trips put back by Android over an installation that kept its points; an import that could not be put right). The file holds closed trips only and an import refuses a point of a trip the file does not hold, so such a point cannot be in it; and an export that stopped at one could never be made, nor the safety copy every import begins with. The card says in red "Left out of the file: 2,700 GPS points that are stored for no trip this phone holds. The Log screen says which.", and the log's line names the trip ids, the first ten of them. What the file says it holds is what it holds. Seen on the emulator.

*Import data.*
1. "Import data" opens the file picker. **The picked file is first copied into MilO** (`no_backup/import/`), so that what is checked is what is imported.
2. **The whole file is read and checked before anything is touched** (`readExport`, `ExportChecks.kt`): that it says it is an export, in a form this MilO reads; every trip, sent report, setting and point, part by part; and the file against itself: as many of each thing as it says, no id twice, no point of a trip it does not hold. The file is walked through one value at a time, so its size does not matter, and its layout does not either: a file another program saved again is read the same (tried on the emulator with one written by Python, keys in another order).
3. **What is refused, and what the card says:**
   - not an export at all: "That file is not an export of MilO's. Nothing was changed."
   - cut short, or something in it that cannot be (a trip that is still being recorded, a position off the earth, an id twice): "That file is damaged or incomplete, so it was not imported. Nothing was changed. The Log screen says what is wrong with it.", and the log names it: "… Trip 7: its status is "OPEN", and only a closed trip can be in an export".
   - **from a newer MilO:** "That file was written by a newer MilO (export format 2). This MilO reads export formats up to 1. Update MilO on this phone, then import the file again. Nothing was changed."
4. **Then the question,** "Replace everything on this phone?": what the file holds and when it was exported, that importing REPLACES this phone's trips and sent reports (by number) with all their GPS points and merges nothing, what becomes of the settings and the truck, and that a copy of what the phone holds is kept first. "Replace everything" or "Cancel"; a press beside it is Cancel.
5. **A safety copy first.** Everything the phone holds is written to `no_backup/safety_copies/MilO-before-import-<time>.json`, a whole export with every point. If that fails, nothing is replaced. After an import of a picked file the newest three are kept.
6. **Then, in this order** (`platform/transfer/ImportRun`): a note that the import begins (`no_backup/import/begun.txt`); the trips and the sent reports in one transaction of the main database, which also writes a line into the event log; the settings in one write; the raw points in one transaction of the points database; the note taken away. A failure in the transaction of the trips leaves the phone exactly as it was. A failure in a later step does not undo the ones before: the card and the log say what could not be done, and the same file can be imported again. If the points cannot be stored, the points of the trips that are gone are removed, so that no imported trip is ever left with another trip's track.
7. **Not while a trip is being recorded.** Asked when the file is picked, when the question is answered, and once more inside the transaction that replaces the trips. A trip the truck starts after that is a new row with a new id, and its points are not touched.
8. **The truck after an import** (`truckOnArrival`): a phone that is paired with its truck stays paired with it, whatever the file names. Otherwise the file's truck is stored by address and name, **without an association**, and the card says "The truck has to be paired again on this phone before a trip can start by itself."
9. **What this phone knows about itself stays:** the association, the sound, the confirmations, the event log. The report that waited for "Did you send it?" is forgotten: its trips were replaced.
10. **"Put that data back"** stands on the card under each safety copy there is, newest first, each with the day and time it was kept ("Before an import MilO kept a copy of what this phone held then, on Oct 6, 2026 at 3:54 PM."). It offers that copy like a picked file, with the same question, which begins "This is the copy MilO kept before an import, on …" and says what the copy holds. So after two wrong imports the copy from before the first can be put back at once.
11. **Putting a copy back never costs another copy.** MilO keeps a copy of what the phone holds first, as before any import. Then, once the phone holds all of the copy that was put back, that copy is removed (the question says so), and no other copy is: the rule of the newest three is not applied. Pressed for the newest copy again and again, the button goes back and forth between two states and the number of copies stays what it was; the copy of what the phone held before the first wrong import is never pushed out by it. A copy whose points could not be stored when it was put back is kept.
12. **An import that MilO's process is ended in the middle of is finished at the next start** (`platform/transfer/ImportResume`, called last in the start-up work of `MiloApplication`). The trips and the raw points are in two database files and are replaced one after the other, the points taking some ten seconds for a year of driving. A process that Android ends in between (MilO swiped away, an update, a reboot) has stored the file's trips and still holds the raw points of the trips that are gone, under the same trip ids, and can say nothing. The note of step 6 says it for it:
    - **Were the trips replaced?** The note is written before they are, so it cannot say. The line the event log gets in the transaction that replaces them can: it is in the same database file and is there exactly if they were. The note holds that line's time and text.
    - **Not replaced:** everything is as it was. The note, MilO's copy of the file and the safety copy of that import are removed; the card says "MilO was closed just as the last import began, before anything was replaced. Everything on this phone is as it was. Import the file again.", and the log has a `REPORT` line. Nothing is done to the raw points: a trip that started since may have an id the file uses.
    - **Replaced:** the steps after the trips are taken again from MilO's copy of the file, which is still there: the settings, the raw points, the lines in the log, and the rest of the app told. Each can be taken twice. The card shows "Replacing the data on this phone. This can take a minute…" while it does, then "MilO was closed in the middle of the last import. It finished the import when it started again." above the usual "Imported: …"; the log has "MilO was ended in the middle of an import, and has finished it at this start from its copy of the file …". Seen on the emulator: 54 trips and 5,400 points, an import of 47 trips and 405,187 points, `kill -9` three seconds after "Replace everything"; after the next start every trip and every point compared equal to the file, eleven seconds after the process started.
    - **Replaced, and the copy cannot be used** (it is gone or no longer reads as the export it was, or a start has tried it before): the raw points of the trips that are gone are removed and the card says "… The trips and the sent reports are the file's. Its GPS points are not stored, and its settings may not be either. Import the same file again to make it whole. …". Seen on the emulator with the copy removed by hand.
    - **It touches no trip,** and only raw points up to the highest trip id the import knew: a trip that started since has a higher id. So it runs also while a trip is being recorded, as the second half of an import always could.
    - **A start that is itself ended while it finishes is counted** in the note. The start after it only removes the points; after two, the import is left as it is and an `ERROR` line says so ("… two later starts could not finish it. It is left as it is …"). Whatever ended two processes in the middle of this must not go on ending every process that follows: the trip service runs in each of them.
    - **Every safety copy is kept by it,** also beyond three; the next import of a picked file makes room.
11. On the emulator an import of 250 trips and 415,860 points took about ten seconds, and the trips, the sent reports and every point compared equal to what was exported.

**Edge cases & gotchas**
- **Never run on the phone, and a restore cannot be tried there:** it needs an uninstall, which deletes the trips. A restore is first met on the day the phone is replaced. Until then the export file is the copy that is known to work; make one before that day (device checks 235 and 250).
- **Android applies the rules again when it restores, and for a backup made on the same phone it applies the cloud rules.** On the emulator a backup made in transfer mode held `points.db`, and the restore at install on the same emulator left it out; an explicit restore through the same transport in transfer mode (`bmgr restore`) brought back all 83,172 points. So the points arrive only by a restore that Android itself treats as a move from another phone. Google's own phone-to-phone transfer has not been run.
- **The emulator's test transport refuses a transfer over 25 MB as well,** which a real transfer (limit 2 GB) does not. That is how the refused-for-size line was first seen: a 28.7 MB points database in transfer mode.
- **Xiaomi's own phone-clone tool is not Android's transfer.** If it copies MilO's data, MilO's backup agent is not called and MilO does not know that anything happened. The truck's row on Setup still reports the missing association (it asks Android at every start), but the checklist's confirmations would come along as if they were true. After any move to another phone: go through Setup again.
- **A cloud backup needs the same signing key.** A build signed with another key is refused the restore (ARCHITECTURE, section 10). The export file is not tied to the key.
- **A backup may never happen.** Android backs up only with backup switched on, an account set, the phone idle, charging and on Wi-Fi, and whether HyperOS lets it is not known. The `PROCESS` line of step 8 is how to tell.
- **Android ends MilO's process after it has collected a backup,** also when MilO was alive in the background. The next trigger starts it again.
- **The safety copies are inside MilO and go with it.** An uninstall removes them, and no backup or transfer carries them. They undo a wrong import on this phone; they are not a backup.
- **A safety copy is as large as an export with every point,** and three are kept: on a phone with a year of driving that is on the order of 100 MB under `no_backup/`. There can be more than three for a while: putting a copy back and finishing an import that was cut off remove none, and the next import of a picked file keeps the newest three again.
- **Two safety copies kept within the same minute read alike on the card:** it writes the day and the time to the minute. The question that follows the button says how many trips, sent reports and GPS points the copy holds.
- **Finishing an import that was cut off happens at a process start, and takes as long as the points did** (about ten seconds on the emulator for 405,187). For that time it holds the points database for writing, so the fixes of a trip that begins in the same seconds are stored when it is done, later than they arrived and unchanged. An import that is under way when a trip starts always did the same.
- **Not covered: a power cut.** The two databases keep a change safe from the end of a process, not from the phone losing power within seconds of it (SQLite's write-ahead log, as Room sets it up, like every other write MilO makes). A phone that dies at the very moment an import ends could still come back with trips and points that do not belong together. An export would then say "Left out of the file: …"; importing the file again puts it right.
- **Raw points that belong to no stored trip stay where they are.** An export and a safety copy pass over them and count them; nothing removes them but the next import, which replaces every point up to the highest trip id.
- **An import leaves a large log file beside the points database** (`points.db-wal`, as large as the imported points) until Android next collects a backup, which empties it. It holds no data that is not also in the database.
- **An import replaces; it never merges.** A trip recorded on this phone since the file was exported is gone from the lists after the import, and is in the safety copy.
- **The event log is neither exported nor replaced.** It is the record of this phone. It is in Android's backup, so after a restore it is the other installation's log, followed by the lines of this one.
- **A restore over an installation that is still there** (Android Studio's "restore app state", `bmgr restore`) replaces `milo.db` and leaves the `points.db` that was there: the points are then those of the trips before the restore. A restore at install has no such leftovers.
- **Ids in a file are capped at two thousand million** (`MAX_IMPORTED_ID`): the tables number upwards from the highest id they ever held, and one absurd id would leave none for the next trip.
- **A file with an accuracy or a speed that is not a number has it written as "none"**; a position that is not a number stops the export, with the points switched off as the way round. Neither has been seen.
- **`"formatVersion"` must be raised with any change to the document,** and every earlier version kept readable. A new column of `trips`, `sent_reports` or `raw_points` is such a change.
- **No new category in the event log:** the lines of an export and an import are `REPORT` lines, a restore's and a collected backup's are `PROCESS` and `PAIRING`, a refused backup's is `ERROR`. A build of phase 4, part A, installed over this one reads everything (seen on the emulator).

## Settings

**Status:** In progress (grows each phase): the phase 1 settings, the work schedule, the report's four settings and the monthly reminder's two are stored and have a screen. The screen ran on an emulator, never on the phone · **Platforms:** Android · **Last updated:** 2026-10-06

**Required behaviour**
- Truck: the paired device, with a way to change it.
- Trip: disconnect grace period (default 2 minutes), minimum trip distance (default 0.3 km), trip-start sound (bundled chirp or a chosen audio file).
- Schedule: tracked days and hours, and whether out-of-schedule trips are saved as Personal or ignored.
- Report: name, company, vehicle description, accounts email. There is no rate-per-km setting.
- Reminder: on or off, and the day of the month.

**How it works today**
*The screen* (`feature/settings/`), opened with the cog beside the title of the home screen. It is not in the bottom bar; while it is open the bar keeps Home marked, and its Back arrow (or Back) leads to Home. (Opened with "Open Settings" on the Report screen, it stands on top of that screen: the bar keeps Trips marked, and Back leads back to the report.) Nine cards: Truck, Report for the accountant, Monthly reminder, Trips, Work schedule, Trips outside the work hours, Driving alert, Trip-start sound, and Backup, export and import.

Until then the field is outlined in red and says under it "Not saved: this is not a complete email address. It has to look like name@example.com, with nothing else in the field." It is never drawn around the stored address.

Each press is stored at once and has the reminder look again straight away, so a reminder that is showing goes the moment it is switched off or its day is moved past today.

   A button is greyed out at its end of the range. Each press is stored at once; there is no Save. The two buttons do not move while the value between them changes (see `StepperRow` under [Design system](#design-system)), so a thumb can stay where it is and press again.

   - Each day has a switch with its name. A day that is switched off shows nothing else; its hours are kept, and come back with the switch.
   - A day that is switched on shows two buttons side by side, "Start: 08:00" and "End: 16:30" on a phone set to 24 hours, "Start: 8:00 a.m." and "End: 4:30 p.m." on one that is not (the phone's own "Use 24-hour format" switch decides, as for a trip's times). The dial has AM and PM exactly when the times on the screen do: both go by the phone's switch.

One switch, **"Tell me when I drive without a trip"**, on out of the box, and two quiet sentences under it. The first says what the alert does and that MilO never starts a trip from it by itself, "the phone cannot tell the truck from any other vehicle". The second says that it needs the Physical activity permission, which the Setup screen asks for, and that the phone can take a minute or more to notice. The card does not show whether the permission is granted; the Setup screen's row does.

9. **Backup, export and import** (since 2026-10-06, phase 4, part B; what each does is under [Backup, export and import](#backup-export-and-import)). Last on the screen: it is used a few times a year, and it is the one card that can replace everything. From the top: what Android's own backup takes (the trips, the sent reports, the settings and the event log, with what the event log names: the truck and the other paired devices with their Bluetooth addresses, and addresses he typed) and that the truck has to be paired again after a restore; what an export is; **"Last export: Oct 6, 2026 at 1:29 PM"** (or "…, without the GPS points", or "No export has been made on this phone yet."); the switch **"Include the GPS points"**, on each time the screen is opened, with a line saying what they are, that the trips are complete without them, and that with the switch off the file still says where each trip started and ended, as an address and as a position; the button **"Export all data"**; a line on what an import does; the button **"Import data"**; and, once an import has been made on this phone, for each safety copy MilO holds, newest first, "Before an import MilO kept a copy of what this phone held then, on (date) at (time)." with a button **"Put that data back"** under it. While a trip is being recorded the buttons are greyed out and the card says "A trip is being recorded. Export and import wait until it has ended." While something is under way it says what ("Writing the file…", "Reading and checking the file…", "Replacing the data on this phone. This can take a minute…"), and the buttons wait. What the last export or import came to stands at the end of the card until the next one, one sentence each: in green what was done ("Exported: 250 trips, 4 sent reports and 415,860 GPS points."), in amber what is left to do ("The truck has to be paired again on this phone …"), in red what was not done. Opened again with an outcome still standing, Settings starts at its top like any screen: the card draws nothing until its first real state is known (`DataViewModel.state` is null until then), so that what stands there from before is not taken for news. 10. **When a change takes effect.** Nothing tells the trip engine. The trip controller reads the grace period and the minimum distance at every trigger, and the trip service reads the sound settings at every trip start, so the stored value is the one in force from the next event on, with no restart. For a trip that is being recorded this means: a grace period that starts after the change uses the new length, one that is already running keeps its deadline, and the minimum distance that counts is the one stored when the trip ends. The schedule and the choice for trips outside it are read the same way and used at one moment only, the end of a trip: a trip is sorted by what is stored when it ends. No trip that is already saved changes when the schedule does. The driving alert reads the schedule as well, each time the phone reports driving, to keep quiet outside the work hours; it starts nothing. **The driving alert's switch and the reminder's card are the settings that do tell something.** the alert has no trigger of its own to read it at, so a press has it ask the phone to report driving, or to stop, at once. The reminder is told for a like reason: one that is showing has to go when it is switched off, not at tomorrow's look. The report's four settings are read when a report is made: the next PDF has the new name, and a report already sent is not changed.
11. **If the settings file cannot be read,** the screen says so in place of the first eight cards ("MilO cannot read its settings, so they cannot be shown or changed here. Trips are still recorded, with the usual values."), and an `ERROR` line goes to the event log. If a change cannot be stored, the screen says "That change could not be saved. The Log screen says why."
12. What the first eight cards show is decided by one pure function, `settingsUiState`, from the stored settings; the ninth has its own (`dataCardState`), and a ViewModel of its own beside the screen's (`DataViewModel`), because the screen's is at its size limit and the card shares nothing with the others. The ranges and the stepping are `SteppedChoice` in `data/settings/SettingChoices.kt` (the two trip numbers, and the reminder's day); what hours a day may have, and the copy, are `WorkSchedule` in `core/schedule/`.

*The store.* `SettingsStore` in `data/settings/` keeps the settings in a DataStore Preferences file and is the only way to read or write them. `settings` is a flow of `MiloSettings` that delivers the current values and every change; `current()` reads them once. A value never written reads as its default. Each setter refuses a value that cannot be right: a negative duration, distance or time, a blank address, name or URI, a report text that is blank, untrimmed or over 80 characters, and an accountant's address that is not one email address. The schedule's setter needs no check of its own: a `WorkSchedule` cannot hold a day that ends before it starts. "No truck name" and "no sound name" are passed as null. If the file cannot be read, every read throws: the store never replaces it with empty settings.

Stored now: the truck's Bluetooth address, name and companion association id; the grace period (default 120 s); the minimum trip distance (default 300 m); the trip-start sound on or off (default on); an optional custom sound, as the place of MilO's copy of the file and the name of the file that was picked; and values that are not Shawn's to choose but must outlive the process: the hold-off after a manual end, stored as the time End was pressed; how far the process-exit records have been imported; and the time at which he confirmed each step of the setup checklist that MilO cannot read (`ConfirmedStep`: HyperOS Autostart, Battery saver, Other permissions, the recents lock). A day whose stored times make no sense, which no setter can write, reads with the default hours. The Settings screen writes the switch, and the setup checklist reads it for the Physical activity row. The report's four settings are written by the Settings screen and read by the Report screen each time it shows or makes a report; the waiting report is written and removed by the Report screen (`ReportRecords`). The monthly reminder reads its switch, its day and the record of when it was last shown at every look, and writes that record when it shows one; the Settings screen writes the switch and the day. The truck's three values are written by `TruckPairing` (from the pairing screen, or when it adopts an association), and the confirmations by the setup checklist. Three more writes come from backup, export and import, all in `TransferStorage.kt`: the date of an export (`setLastExport`); the settings an import brought, in one write, with the truck stored without an association unless this phone's own truck is kept (`replaceTransferred`); and, after a restore, the removal of the association's number, the four confirmations and a sound whose file is not here (`forgetOtherInstallation`). The truck's values are read by the Bluetooth receiver and the companion service at every event (to tell whether it is about the truck) and by every reading of the truck's connection.

**Edge cases & gotchas**
- **Never run on the phone.** Device checks 111 to 124 and 126, for the schedule 130 to 135, 151 and 152, for the driving alert's card DA-2, for the report's four settings 179 to 181, for the reminder's card 213 and 221, and for the ninth card 234 to 245. On an emulator on 2026-10-06 the screen was drawn, the two numbers were stepped, a trip recorded straight afterwards was discarded by the new minimum without a restart, and the screen came back after Android had killed MilO in the background (FINDINGS_LOG).
- The ranges (0.5 to 10 minutes, 0.1 to 2 km) were chosen while building and are Shawn's to change: two constants in `SettingChoices.kt`.
- The store accepts values the screen does not offer (a grace period of 0, for one). Such a value is shown as it is, and the first press of minus or plus brings it to the nearest value the screen does offer.
- There is no minimum distance of zero. With none, a start that never left the yard would count as a trip.
- A longer grace period keeps a trip open, and the phone recording, for longer after Shawn leaves the truck. The trip is still cut where the truck was found gone, so the walk is not counted.
- Changing a number is not written to the event log: each press would be a line. A trip's own lines show the values it ran with ("… is under the minimum of 300 m").
- **The switch "Include the GPS points" is not stored.** It is on whenever the screen is opened, so that an export is whole unless Shawn says otherwise that time.
- **An export or an import is not stopped by leaving the screen,** and what it came to is still on the card when the screen is opened again: both run outside the screen, and where they stand is kept by `DataTransfer`, not by the card. Ending MilO's process loses what the card said. An import that was under way then is finished at the next start, and the card says so ([Backup, export and import](#backup-export-and-import)); a file whose question was never answered is removed at the next start.
- The screen has no "reset to defaults". The defaults are 2 minutes and 0.3 km, Monday to Friday 08:00 to 16:30, "Save as Personal", the driving alert switched on, and the monthly reminder switched on for the 1st.
- **The reminder's day is stepped a day at a time,** so from the 1st to the 15th is fourteen presses. It is set once; a list of thirty-one days to pick from would have been a new kind of control for it.
- **The reminder is on out of the box,** as the brief has it ("on the 1st of each month"). It shows nothing until last month has a Business trip, and needs the notification permission the Setup screen asks for.
- A change of the reminder's card is written to the event log only as what the reminder then decided (a `REPORT` line, when the decision is not the one last written).
- **The driving alert is on out of the box,** a judgement made while building: the brief asks for the alert, and it does nothing until the Physical activity permission is granted on the Setup screen and a truck is paired. A press of its switch is written to the event log (a `DRIVING` line), unlike the two numbers and the schedule: it changes what MilO asks of the phone.
- **A half-typed address is not stored, and is gone when the screen is left.** The field says so in red while it stands there. Turning the phone keeps it, in red. Leaving Settings with Back does not: coming back, the field shows the address that is stored, which can be the one from before, without red.
- **The check of the address catches slips, not wrong addresses.** A missing "@", a space, a comma for the dot, two addresses in one field are refused; `acounts@example.ca` is taken. Only the email bouncing shows that one.
- **With the keyboard open, the red line under the address field can be hidden by the keyboard** while the field itself, outlined in red, stays in view (seen on an emulator, 2026-10-06). Putting the keyboard away shows the line.
- A name is stored at every keystroke, so a name that is half typed when MilO is ended is stored half typed.
- **To move a day's hours past its present end, the end is changed first** (and to move them before its present start, the start first): each time is checked against the other as it stands. The red line says so.
- **The copy asks no question** and replaces the hours of every other tracked day in one press. It is undone by setting the days again, which is why the button is only shown while it would change something.
- A change of the schedule is not written to the event log, like the two numbers. Each trip's closing line says which hours it was judged by.
- The time picker cannot choose seconds, and the schedule stores whole minutes. The latest end a day can have is 23:59; a trip that starts in the last minute of such a day is Personal.
- The day names follow the phone's language. **Whether a time has 24 hours or AM and PM follows the phone's own "Use 24-hour format" switch:** the dial, the two times beside it, and every trip's times on Home and Trips, so that they agree with each other and with the phone's clock. Until the review of 2026-10-06 the language alone decided, and on a phone set to 24 hours in English the dial had AM and PM, where one missed tap stores a time that is valid and wrong. Where the phone's switch and the language's own habit disagree, the time is written plainly, "16:30" or "4:30 PM", and not in the language's own short form. Seen on an emulator, never on the phone (device check 152).
- **"That change could not be saved" still stands at the top of the screen,** above the first card, whichever card the press was in. For a press far down the screen it is out of view, the fault the schedule's own red line had until the review of 2026-10-06. It needs a settings file that cannot be written, and was left for a change of its own.
- **The time picker is Material 3's, which that library still marks as experimental** in the release the build pins (1.4.0). It is used through one component (`TimeDialog`), so a change to it in a later release is one file's repair; the weekly update PR would fail to compile, not misbehave.

## Design system

**Status:** Live · **Surfaces:** Phone · **Last updated:** 2026-10-06

**What it does**
The one place colours, spacing, type and shapes are defined, and the shared components every phone screen is composed from. Check here before building any UI: reuse what exists, and add to it deliberately when something is missing (STANDARDS §8).

**What exists**
- Tokens in `core/designsystem/theme/`: `Color.kt` (light and dark schemes plus the four status colours), `MiloSpacing.kt` (4, 8, 16, 24, 32 dp steps), `Type.kt`, `Shape.kt`. `MiloTheme` applies them and exposes `MiloTheme.spacing` and `MiloTheme.statusColors`.
- **Status colours** (`MiloTheme.statusColors`): `ok` (green), `problem` (red, the scheme's error colour), `attention` (amber: Shawn has to do or confirm something MilO cannot check) and `unknown` (grey, the colour of secondary text: MilO could not find out). `attention` and `unknown` were added on 2026-10-05 for the setup checklist.
- Components in `core/designsystem/component/`:
  - `PrimaryButton`: the main action on a screen.
  - `SectionCard`: a titled group of content.
  - `StatusRow`: one requirement and its state. An indicator, a label, an optional second line, and up to two text buttons on a line of their own under the text. **Four states** (`RowStatus`), each with its own shape as well as its own colour: `OK` (green tick), `PROBLEM` (red warning), `UNKNOWN` (grey question mark), `NEEDS_CONFIRMATION` (amber empty ring). The icons are in `StatusIcons.kt`. A setting that Shawn has confirmed by hand is drawn as `OK`, and its second line says that it is his word.
  - `MiloNavigationBar`: the bottom bar, Material 3's navigation bar with one entry per top-level screen, each with an icon and its name. Added on 2026-10-05, with navigation.
  - `ScreenTitle`: the heading every screen starts with. Given an `onBack`, it shows a back arrow before the title; only a screen opened from another one (pairing, Settings) has it. Given an `action` (`ScreenTitleAction`: an icon, what a screen reader says for it, and what it does), it shows one icon button at the end of the line. **Added on 2026-10-06** for the way from Home to Settings, which has no place in the bottom bar. It is for the way to another screen, not for an action on the screen itself.
  - `SwitchRow`: a labelled on/off switch. The whole row is the target.
  - `StepperRow`: a setting that is a number chosen in steps. Its name, an optional second line saying what it is for, and the value between a minus and a plus button (`StepperButton`: what a screen reader says for it, what it does, and whether it is greyed out). **Added on 2026-10-06** for the grace period and the minimum trip distance. Buttons and not a slider, because the values are few and exact, and a slider is hard to set to one of them with a thumb. The value stands in the middle of a place of fixed width (five times its text size, so it grows with the phone's font size), which keeps both buttons where they are while the value changes: with a place as wide as its text, the minus button moved 14 dp between "2 min" and "1.5 min", and every other press of a thumb held still missed it. A value wider than the place is still shown whole, and only then do the buttons move. A screen reader says the new value after each press.
  - `FigureRow`: a line or two of text with one figure at its end, such as a trip and its km. The figure can be greyed, for one that is in no total, or left out. **Added on 2026-10-06**, taken out of the Trips screen's rows when the home screen's Today card needed the same row: every list of trips is made of it.
  - `ChoiceRow`: one of a few choices of which exactly one is in force: a radio button, its name, and an optional quieter line saying what choosing it means. The whole row is the target, 48 dp at least, like `SwitchRow`. **Added on 2026-10-06** for "Save as Personal" and "Ignore them": a switch says on or off, and this setting has two sides that both need a name. The rows of one choice stand in a column with `Modifier.selectableGroup()`, so that a screen reader announces them as one group.
  - `ChipChoice`: one choice out of many short ones, each a chip (Material 3's `FilterChip`), wrapped onto as many lines as they need. `ChipOption` is one of them: its label, whether it is the one in force, and what choosing it does. **Added on 2026-10-06 (phase 4)** for the Log screen's filter by category, which has fourteen choices of one word each: as a column of `ChoiceRow`s they would fill the screen before the list they narrow, and a row that scrolls sideways hides the choices that are off its edge. A screen reader announces the chips as one group.
  - `TimeDialog`: asks for a time of day with Android's usual clock dial, under a title that says what the time is for, with a button that takes the time and one that does not. Nothing is chosen until the first is pressed; pressing beside it, or Back, changes nothing. It is told whether to show AM and PM. **Added on 2026-10-06** for the start and the end of a work day. It wraps Material 3's time picker, which is still marked experimental in the pinned Material release; the opt-in is in this one file and nowhere else.
  - `DateDialog`: asks for a day with Android's usual calendar, a month at a time, with a button that takes the day and one that does not; the companion of `TimeDialog`. It is told the last day that may be chosen, and greys out every later one. **Added on 2026-10-06** for the date of a trip on the edit screen. It wraps Material 3's date picker, which is marked experimental in the pinned Material release like the time picker; the opt-in is in this one file. The picker counts days in milliseconds at midnight UTC, and two small functions with a unit test turn a calendar day into that and back, so that no time zone can move the chosen day to its neighbour. **The calendar scrolls inside the dialog** (the review of 2026-10-06): Material's calendar has one height, and with the phone on its side the dialog is lower than that, so the weekday letters were drawn over the first weeks and the last week lay under the buttons. Upright nothing changes. (`TimeDialog` needs none of this: Material's clock dial lies on its side by itself.)
  - `TextEntry`: one line of text to type in, under a label that stays visible while it is filled: an address, or a distance (then with the keyboard for decimal numbers). It is told how long the text may be. **Added on 2026-10-06** for the edit screen; the first field of MilO that is typed into. **The field keeps what is typed itself** and reports each change; the text it is given is only where it starts. A text field has to show a keystroke in the frame it is typed in, and text that travels to a ViewModel and back through a flow arrives a frame later, so that the cursor jumps or a letter is lost. It can be given **an error line** (the review of 2026-10-06): what is wrong with what the field holds is then written in red directly under it, the field is outlined in red, and a screen reader is told that the field is in error. Told that it holds **an email address** (2026-10-06, for the accountant's address), it brings up the keyboard for one: "@" on it, no capitals and no corrections.
  - `ConfirmDialog`: the question before something is taken away. A title, a text that says what will happen and how it is undone, a button that names the action ("Delete", never "OK") and a button that does not do it. Pressing beside it, or Back, does nothing. Its text scrolls when the dialog cannot show all of it (the long question before an import, the phone on its side, large type): Material's dialog cuts off what does not fit, and the end of a question is where it says what is kept and how the action is undone. **Added on 2026-10-06** for deleting a trip; the first dialog of MilO's own. Also the question before "Restore recorded values", the one before a report is sent a second time and, asked by the app itself, "Leave without saving?". **Since 2026-10-06 it can be told that a press beside it, or Back, is something else than its second button** (`onPutOff`): "Did you send it?" has two answers, "I sent it" and "Not sent", and a stray tap must be neither, so there it only puts the question off.
  - `MiloIcons`: the icons a screen may use: Home, Trips, Setup, Log, Back, Settings (a cog), Add and Remove (the plus and the minus of a `StepperRow`).
  - `rememberTwentyFourHourClock`: draws nothing. It answers whether the phone is set to write times with 24 hours ("Use 24-hour format" in Android's date and time settings; until that switch has been touched, Android answers by the phone's language), and reads it again each time the screen resumes, because the switch is changed outside MilO and Android tells a screen nothing about it. A screen calls it once and hands the answer to `formatTimeOfDay` or `formatClockTime` (`core/util/TimeFormat.kt`) and to `TimeDialog`. **Added on 2026-10-06** by the review of the work schedule. It is the only place a phone screen reads that setting, and the only setting a Composable reads for itself.
  - `CameToFrontEffect`: draws nothing. It calls the screen's ViewModel when the screen is first shown and every time Shawn is looking at it again, on either of two signs: the screen resumes (it is entered, or MilO returns from a settings screen, another app or one of Android's dialogs), or MilO's window gets the focus back (the quick settings panel or the notification shade closing, which resumes nothing). Returning from a settings screen gives both signs, so what it calls must be safe to call twice. The rule for the second sign is a plain function with a unit test (`focusRegained`); the effect itself cannot be tested off the phone. Added on 2026-10-05, after the review of the screens.
- **Words two features must say alike** are in `core/designsystem/text/`. Today one file, `TripCategoryWords.kt` (`categoryWordsRes`): which words say what a closed trip is saved as, used by Home and by Trips. It is here and not beside the rule in `core/schedule/`, because it chooses a string resource and the rules packages import nothing of Android (**moved here on 2026-10-06** by the review). It is the only file of the design system that knows a type from outside it (`TripCategory`). A second file since 2026-10-06, `SubmissionWords.kt` (`submissionWordsRes` and `submissionWords`): which sentence says whether a month's report has been sent, used by the Trips screen's month card and by the Report screen. It is handed plain times and a number, and knows no type from outside.
- **Patterns the screens share,** to be followed by the next ones:
  - A screen starts with `ScreenTitle` and pads its content with `MiloTheme.spacing.medium`. A screen of fixed content is a scrolling `Column` of `SectionCard`s; a screen of stored rows (Log, Trips) is a `LazyColumn`.
  - While a screen's first read is under way it shows one line of text ("Reading…"), and an empty list says so in a sentence. Neither is a component.
  - A problem and the way to fix it is a `StatusRow` with a button, on Home, Setup and the pairing screen alike. A press that did not work (a refused sound file, a change that could not be stored, a trip that could not be changed) is a `StatusRow` in the red state without a button, shown until the next press. It stands beside what was pressed: in the sound's card, under the day whose time was refused.
  - A setting is stored at the moment it is changed, and no settings screen has a Save button. A setting that is picked in a dialog (a time of day) is stored when the dialog's own button is pressed. **A setting that is typed is stored as it is typed** (2026-10-06, the report's four). Where what is typed cannot be stored as it stands (an email address that is not one yet), nothing is stored, and the field says so with its own error line for as long as that is so.
  - **A form whose fields only make sense together is stored as a whole,** with one `PrimaryButton` at its end that names the action ("Save", "Add this trip"). The edit screen is the one such form (2026-10-06). **What stops a save is said in red where it is put right** (reworded by the review of 2026-10-06; until then every refusal was listed above the button, a screen away from what it named): a field that is typed into shows it as the `TextEntry`'s own error line, and anything else as a red `StatusRow` in the card it is about, under what is to be changed. The screen moves to the first of them, once for each press that stored nothing; a line goes away when what it names is put right; and the screen leaves when the form has been stored. Only a failure of storage, which is about the press and no field, is a red `StatusRow` at the button. What is typed is held by the screen's ViewModel, so it survives the phone being turned.
  - **A screen that holds something typed and not saved is not left without a question.** The screen reports whether it does (a plain function it is handed, like the ones for navigation), and the app asks "Leave without saving?" with a `ConfirmDialog` before it carries out any way out: the phone's Back, the screen's own Back arrow, a button of the bottom bar. The screen never asks itself: it sees only its own arrow.
  - **The keyboard takes room from a screen and never slides it away.** The activity is `adjustResize`, and `MiloApp` ends every screen where the keyboard begins. A screen with a text field is a scrolling one, so that the field and whatever is under it can still be reached.
  - A day is a text button that shows the date and opens a `DateDialog`, as a time of day opens a `TimeDialog`.
  - Text is typed into a `TextEntry`. The screen is told what was typed and never tells the field what to show.
  - A time of day is written by `formatTimeOfDay` or `formatClockTime`, with the answer of `rememberTwentyFourHourClock`: 24 hours or AM and PM as the phone is set, never as a screen decides for itself.
  - A setting that is on or off is a `SwitchRow` (and so is a yes or no in a form: "Ended the next day"); one that is a number in steps is a `StepperRow`; one that is a choice between named things is a column of `ChoiceRow`s; a time of day is a text button that shows the time and opens a `TimeDialog`. One choice out of many one-word ones, above a list that it narrows, is a `ChipChoice` (the Log screen's categories); it is not for a setting.
  - A total that must not be read as part of another stands in a line of its own, quieter than the figure above it (Personal under Business, on Home and on Trips). **Every total is the sum of the figures that are shown for its parts** (`sumOfTenths`), never the parts' metres added up and rounded once: what can be added up by hand must add up.
  - **A question that Android cannot answer is asked of Shawn, and nothing is recorded that he did not say** (2026-10-06: "Did you send it?"). Both answers are buttons that name what he did; a press beside the question only puts it off, and it is asked again the next time he is back on the screen. What the question is about is stored, so it outlives the screen.
  - **What a press needs and does not have is said before the press,** beside the button, with the way to where it is set (the Report screen's missing name and address, and "Open Settings"). The press then makes nothing and the screen moves to those lines.
  - Something that can be undone is done at once (Restore, Count this trip, a setting); only what takes something away asks first, with a `ConfirmDialog` (deleting a trip; "Restore recorded values", which drops what was typed in place of the recorded figures; leaving the edit screen with something typed and not saved). Sending a report for a period a second time asks first as well: it takes nothing away, but it puts a second email in the accountant's inbox. Removing a sent report asks first as well, and the question says what it does for that report in particular. **A question whose answer depends on what is stored says the answer that applies,** chosen by a pure function (`sentEffect`, `removalEffect`), and never one sentence for every case. **Where a press has more than one thing to ask about, the questions come one after the other,** each with its own two buttons, and "no" to any of them does nothing (sending a month that has not ended and was sent before).
  - A trip whose figures are typed and not recorded carries an asterisk after its times and a line that starts with the same asterisk and says what it means. The report for the accountant uses the same mark, after the trip's kilometres, with a legend.
  - A secondary action is a text button at the end of its line, on a line of its own under the text it belongs to. That holds for an action on a list as a whole too ("Add a missed trip", "Share the whole log") and for one on a single line of it ("Remove" under a sent report): the icon beside a screen's title is for the way to another screen only.
  - A row that is pressed as a whole is at least 48 dp high, Material's smallest target for a finger. `SwitchRow` sees to that itself; a row that a screen makes pressable (a finished trip on Trips) uses Material's own `minimumInteractiveComponentSize()`, so no dp value is written in the screen. A row that cannot be pressed is not given a click handler that is switched off: a screen reader would call it "disabled".
  - Something that appears because of a tap appears where it can be seen: if it would be below the edge of a list, the list moves (`BringIntoViewRequester`; the Delete button on Trips, the refusal of a day's hours on Settings, and the first thing the edit form refuses). Only a tap moves the screen: what is drawn again because the phone was turned stays where it is.
  - A screen that shows something Android reports no changes of (a permission, a setting, the paired devices, which month is the current one) reads it again through `CameToFrontEffect`, never through a lifecycle effect of its own.
  - **A press whose work takes seconds says what it is doing, makes its buttons wait, and says what it came to in the card it was pressed in** (2026-10-06: export and import). One quiet line while it works; then one `StatusRow` for each thing there is to say, green for done, red for not done, amber (the ring) for something left for Shawn to do; they stay until the next press. A button that has to wait is greyed out, with the reason said beside it, and never hidden.
  - A ViewModel is handed plain functions for navigation; a feature never imports another feature.

**Edge cases & gotchas**
- No colour, dp or sp value may be written outside `core/designsystem/`. The only exceptions are the two launcher-icon drawables, which the launcher reads before Compose exists, and `drawable/ic_stat_trip.xml`, the notification icon, which Android draws itself outside Compose.
- Notifications are not Compose and cannot use these components. Their text is in `strings.xml`. There are four channels: the three of trip recording (`platform/trip/TripNotifications.kt`) and the monthly reminder's (`platform/reminder/ReminderNotification.kt`).
- **The PDF is not a screen and uses none of this.** It is paper: its sizes are points, its one colour is black, and its fonts and rules are set in `core/report/ReportPage.kt` and `ReportBlocks.kt`. Its words are in `strings.xml` like every other text.
- The icons are built from path data in `MiloIcons.kt` and `StatusIcons.kt`, not taken from the material-icons library, which is frozen. A unit test proves that each one parses. **How the icons added on 2026-10-05 look on the phone has not been reported:** Shawn has used the bottom bar there (2026-10-05), and device check 52, which asks about each icon, has not been run. The cog, the plus and the minus were seen on an emulator on 2026-10-06.
- A card is only as wide inside as its widest line. A button that is to sit at the end of a card must be in a row that fills the width, or it lands in the middle of a card with short lines (seen on the emulator with the Settings screen's truck card).
- The bottom bar is always there, on the pairing screen and the Settings screen too. Only the keyboard covers it, while something is typed on the edit screen.
- The Android Auto screen cannot use these components. It is drawn by the car from Car App Library templates.
