# Research: Starting a location foreground service from the background

> Gathered 2026-10-03 by a research agent reading live primary sources. Every finding was then re-checked by an independent verifier; its verdict is shown on each finding.
> This file is evidence for the ADRs. It records what was true on that date and is not kept current.

## Summary

Research only; no files were created or modified (the docs-pointer fix in the relayed user request was not part of this subtask and was not touched).

Bottom line: a background-started location FGS is fully legal and workable for MilO on Android 12 through 17, provided two independent conditions are both met at the moment of start: (1) a background-start exemption and (2) ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION ("Allow all the time"). Background-start exemptions do NOT confer while-in-use location; on Android 14+ a location FGS created from the background without background-location throws SecurityException at startForeground, and on 12/13 it starts but gets no location. Background location is both required and sufficient (with FINE location + FOREGROUND_SERVICE_LOCATION).

For condition (1) there are three stackable exemptions. Documented and permanent: a CompanionDeviceManager association plus REQUEST_COMPANION_START_FOREGROUND_SERVICES_FROM_BACKGROUND (AOSP grants it whenever an association exists, not only while the truck is present), and battery-optimisation exemption. Undocumented but real: AOSP sends ACL_CONNECTED/ACL_DISCONNECTED with a 20-second FGS-start temp allowlist (REASON_BLUETOOTH_BROADCAST) since Android 12, but the "Bluetooth broadcast" bullet is absent from the current official exemption list (page dated 2026-10-01), so it must not be the only leg. BOOT_COMPLETED and MY_PACKAGE_REPLACED are also exempt, and location is explicitly on the Android 15+ boot-completed type allowlist.

ACL_CONNECTED/DISCONNECTED are still exempt from the manifest implicit-broadcast ban, require the BLUETOOTH_CONNECT runtime permission to be received, are delivered to cached/frozen processes (manifest receivers are not queued), but are NOT delivered to a force-stopped or never-launched app, nor before first unlock after reboot. No Android 15, 16 or 17 behaviour change adds a timeout or new start restriction for the location type. The biggest silent killers are not FGS rules but: app hibernation (permissions reset after months of not opening the app), the user-set "Restricted" battery state, force-stop, and OEM battery managers. Recommended design is redundant triggers (CDM presence + manifest ACL receiver + boot/package-replaced reconcile) converging on one idempotent service start.

## Findings

### 1. Current official exemption list for starting an FGS from the background: the entries that apply to MilO are (a) Companion Device Manager association plus REQUEST_COMPANION_START_FOREGROUND_SERVICES_FROM_BACKGROUND or REQUEST_COMPANION_RUN_IN_BACKGROUND, (b) user turned off battery optimisation, (c) BOOT_COMPLETED / LOCKED_BOOT_COMPLETED / MY_PACKAGE_REPLACED receivers, (d) user taps a notification/widget. A Bluetooth-broadcast exemption is NOT on the list.

Page (last updated 2026-10-01) lists 14 exemptions: transition from visible activity; app allowed to start activities from background; high-priority FCM; user action on notification/widget/bubble; exact alarm for user-requested action; current IME; geofencing / activity-recognition transition; BOOT_COMPLETED, LOCKED_BOOT_COMPLETED, MY_PACKAGE_REPLACED; TIMEZONE/TIME/LOCALE changed; NFC transaction; device/profile owner; CDM with the two companion permissions ('Whenever possible, use REQUEST_COMPANION_START_FOREGROUND_SERVICES_FROM_BACKGROUND'); battery optimisations off; SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW (Android 15+ targets also need a visible overlay). I grepped the raw page text for 'bluetooth': no match in the exemption section. Violations throw ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException.

- Confidence: high · Applies to: Android 12+ (targetSdk 31+)
- Source: https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start

### 2. AOSP still sends ACTION_ACL_CONNECTED and ACTION_ACL_DISCONNECTED with a 20-second FGS-start temp allowlist (REASON_BLUETOOTH_BROADCAST), so a manifest receiver can call startForegroundService inside onReceive. This is undocumented on developer.android.com today and should be treated as a bonus, not the sole guarantee.

RemoteDevices.aclStateChangeCallback builds the intent with EXTRA_DEVICE and EXTRA_TRANSPORT, adds FLAG_RECEIVER_REGISTERED_ONLY_BEFORE_BOOT | FLAG_RECEIVER_INCLUDE_BACKGROUND, and calls sendBroadcast(intent, BLUETOOTH_CONNECT, Utils.getTempBroadcastOptions().toBundle()). Utils.getTempBroadcastOptions sets setTemporaryAppAllowlist(durationMs, TEMPORARY_ALLOW_LIST_TYPE_FOREGROUND_SERVICE_ALLOWED, REASON_BLUETOOTH_BROADCAST) with DEFAULT_TEMP_ALLOW_LIST_DURATION_MS = 20_000 (DeviceConfig-overridable). The same code exists in android12-release (packages/apps/Bluetooth, getTempAllowlistBroadcastOptions, 20_000). Consequence: start the service synchronously in onReceive; do not hop through WorkManager or a delayed coroutine. OEM builds could differ; not verified per OEM.

- Confidence: medium · Applies to: AOSP Android 12 through main (2026)
- Source: https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/modules/Bluetooth/+/refs/heads/main/android/app/src/com/android/bluetooth/btservice/RemoteDevices.java

### 3. In AOSP the companion-device exemption applies whenever the app has ANY CDM association and holds one of the two companion permissions, not only while the device is present; battery-optimisation exemption and temp allowlists are separate, later checks.

ActiveServices.shouldAllowFgsStartForegroundNoBindingCheckLocked: after proc-state, START_FOREGROUND_SERVICES_FROM_BACKGROUND and SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW checks it tests mAm.mInternal.isAssociatedCompanionApp(userId, callingUid) and isPermissionGranted(REQUEST_COMPANION_START_FOREGROUND_SERVICES_FROM_BACKGROUND) || REQUEST_COMPANION_RUN_IN_BACKGROUND, yielding REASON_COMPANION_DEVICE_MANAGER. Then isAllowlistedForFgsStartLOSP(callingUid): FAKE_TEMP_ALLOW_LIST_ITEM (device-idle allowlist, i.e. battery optimisation off) yields REASON_SYSTEM_ALLOW_LISTED, otherwise the temp-allowlist item's reason code (e.g. REASON_BLUETOOTH_BROADCAST, REASON_BOOT_COMPLETED). A source comment also notes that a CDM app holding REQUEST_COMPANION_RUN_IN_BACKGROUND is put on the power user-allowlist. Practical meaning: with a CDM association the ACL receiver, boot receiver and any sticky restart are all covered by a documented exemption with no 20-second window. None of these paths sets while-in-use (shouldAllowFgsWhileInUsePermissionLocked only allows TOP/visible/system/BAL-privileged/device-owner callers).

- Confidence: high · Applies to: AOSP main; same structure since Android 12
- Source: https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/main/services/core/java/com/android/server/am/ActiveServices.java

### 4. Location FGS type requirements since Android 14: manifest foregroundServiceType="location", FOREGROUND_SERVICE_LOCATION (normal permission, API 34) plus FOREGROUND_SERVICE, pass FOREGROUND_SERVICE_TYPE_LOCATION to startForeground, and at that moment hold ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION or ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION with location services enabled.

Missing type declaration gives MissingForegroundServiceTypeException; missing type permission or unmet runtime prerequisites gives SecurityException from startForeground, which 'prevents the foreground service from starting ... and might cause your app to crash'. Permissions must be granted before startForeground. The service-types page states verbatim: 'you cannot create a location foreground service while your app is in the background, unless you've been granted the ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION runtime permission.'

- Confidence: high · Applies to: targetSdk 34+ on Android 14+
- Source: https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types

### 5. A background-start exemption does not grant while-in-use location. Without ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION a location FGS started from the background throws SecurityException on Android 14+ and silently gets no location on Android 12/13. ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION is required and (with fine/coarse location and the type permission) sufficient.

Docs: 'it cannot create the service while the app is in the background, even if the app falls into one of the exemptions from background start restrictions'; on Android 14+ 'your app gets the exception as soon as it tries to create the foreground service'; below 14 'the system would let you create the service, but the service wouldn't have access to the needed resources'. checkSelfPermission returns GRANTED even when only while-in-use is held, so it cannot detect the problem; check ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION explicitly. The while-in-use exemption list (system component starts service, widget/notification interaction, PendingIntent from a visible app, device owner, VoiceInteractionService, START_ACTIVITIES_FROM_BACKGROUND) does not include Bluetooth broadcasts or CDM callbacks. AOSP ForegroundServiceTypePolicy confirms the mechanism: for the location permissions it reads the app-op mode; MODE_ALLOWED (all-the-time) returns GRANTED regardless of allowWhileInUse, MODE_FOREGROUND (while-in-use only) returns GRANTED only if allowWhileInUse. The AOSP policy contains no LocationManager.isLocationEnabled check, although the docs list 'location services enabled' as a prerequisite; preflight it anyway.

- Confidence: high · Applies to: Android 12 to 17
- Source: https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/main/core/java/android/app/ForegroundServiceTypePolicy.java

### 6. Background location must be requested as a separate step after foreground location; on Android 11+ the dialog has no 'Allow all the time' option and the user is sent to a settings page. Approximate-only grants carry over to the background.

Request ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION + ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION first, then ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION on its own; show rationale using PackageManager.getBackgroundPermissionOptionLabel(). 'If the user grants ... ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION but grants only approximate location access in the foreground, your app has only approximate location access in the background as well' so the owner must choose Precise. The Manifest.permission reference marks ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION as dangerous and 'hard restricted' (installer must allowlist it); I did not find a primary source confirming adb/Android Studio installs allowlist it by default, so verify on the device that 'Allow all the time' is offered.

- Confidence: high · Applies to: Android 11+
- Source: https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/permissions/background

### 7. ACTION_ACL_CONNECTED and ACTION_ACL_DISCONNECTED are still on the implicit-broadcast exception list, so a manifest-registered receiver is allowed for targetSdk 26+.

Exceptions page (last updated 2026-02-26) lists 'BluetoothHeadset.ACTION_CONNECTION_STATE_CHANGED, BluetoothA2dp.ACTION_CONNECTION_STATE_CHANGED, ACTION_ACL_CONNECTED, ACTION_ACL_DISCONNECTED' with the reason 'User experience is not likely to suffer if apps receive broadcasts for these Bluetooth events.' AOSP additionally sets FLAG_RECEIVER_INCLUDE_BACKGROUND on the ACL intents. The HFP/A2DP connection-state actions are also exempt and can be added to the same receiver as a second signal for a car head unit.

- Confidence: high · Applies to: Android 8+ through current docs
- Source: https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/broadcasts/broadcast-exceptions
- Verifier: **confirmed**. Exceptions page (2026-02-26) entry matches verbatim. AOSP mechanism confirmed: BroadcastSkipPolicy only blocks manifest receivers when the intent lacks FLAG_RECEIVER_INCLUDE_BACKGROUND, which the ACL intents set. Missing build detail: the receiver must be android:exported="true" because the sender is the Bluetooth app (android.uid.bluetooth), not system_server (see missed #4). (https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/broadcasts/broadcast-exceptions)

### 8. The BLUETOOTH_CONNECT runtime permission is required to receive the ACL broadcasts on API 31+; without the 'Nearby devices' grant the receiver is silently skipped.

BluetoothDevice reference for both actions: 'For apps targeting Build.VERSION_CODES.S or higher, this requires the Manifest.permission.BLUETOOTH_CONNECT permission which can be gained with Activity.requestPermissions'. AOSP sends the broadcast with BLUETOOTH_CONNECT as the receiver permission. Both actions 'Always contain the extra fields EXTRA_DEVICE and EXTRA_TRANSPORT'; EXTRA_TRANSPORT was added in API 33 (TRANSPORT_BREDR / TRANSPORT_LE), so on API 31/32 filter by MAC only. The broadcast fires for every Bluetooth device (earbuds, watch), so the receiver must compare EXTRA_DEVICE's address with the stored truck MAC and return fast otherwise. BLUETOOTH_CONNECT is also needed for getBondedDevices, getProfileConnectionState and profile proxies.

- Confidence: high · Applies to: targetSdk 31+
- Source: https://developer.android.com/reference/android/bluetooth/BluetoothDevice

### 9. The ACL broadcasts are not delivered to an app in the stopped state (force-stopped, or installed but never launched). Android 15 tightened this: only direct or indirect user action un-stops the app.

The system adds FLAG_EXCLUDE_STOPPED_PACKAGES to all broadcast intents; apps are stopped 'when they are first installed but are not yet launched and when they are manually stopped by the user'. The AOSP ACL intent does not set FLAG_INCLUDE_STOPPED_PACKAGES. Android 15: 'Apps should only be removed from the stopped state through direct or indirect user action'; all PendingIntents are cancelled on entering stopped state, and ACTION_BOOT_COMPLETED is delivered when the user un-stops the app (ApplicationStartInfo.wasForceStopped() tells you). Consequence: after a Force stop in Settings, MilO will not auto-start trips until the owner opens it once; the in-app status screen should detect and say so. An Android Studio Run launches the activity, which clears stopped state.

- Confidence: high · Applies to: All versions; stricter on Android 15+
- Source: https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-all

### 10. Manifest-declared receivers are not deferred for cached/frozen apps: the Android 14 queuing change affects only context-registered receivers. I found no Android 15, 16 or 17 behaviour change that defers manifest-declared ACL broadcasts.

Android 14: 'the system can place context-registered broadcasts in a queue while the app is in the cached state ... Manifest-declared broadcasts aren't queued, and apps are removed from the cached state for broadcast delivery.' A dead process is cold-started for the manifest receiver ('the system launches the app when the broadcast is sent'). Android 16 only changes cross-process ordered-broadcast priority. Implication: do not rely on a context-registered ACL receiver for trip start; a context-registered receiver inside the running FGS for disconnect is fine because an FGS process is not cached. The ACL intent is not sent with FLAG_RECEIVER_FOREGROUND, so delivery can lag under heavy broadcast load (e.g. right after boot); no primary source quantifies this.

- Confidence: high · Applies to: Android 14+
- Source: https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/behavior-changes-all

### 11. Two reboot gaps exist for the ACL receiver: nothing is delivered to a non-directBootAware receiver before the first unlock after reboot, and the AOSP ACL intent is flagged registered-receivers-only until the system has booted. An already-connected truck therefore produces no broadcast for MilO.

Direct Boot doc: only components marked android:directBootAware="true" run before unlock; LOCKED_BOOT_COMPLETED is sent before unlock, BOOT_COMPLETED after unlock when credential-encrypted storage (where Room lives by default) is available. AOSP adds FLAG_RECEIVER_REGISTERED_ONLY_BEFORE_BOOT to the ACL intents. Bluetooth can reconnect to the truck before the owner unlocks the phone, so a BOOT_COMPLETED reconcile step is mandatory. Making the trip pipeline direct-boot aware would require moving the database to device-protected storage and is not recommended for v1.

- Confidence: high · Applies to: Android 7+
- Source: https://developer.android.com/privacy-and-security/direct-boot

### 12. To detect an existing classic connection use BluetoothAdapter.getProfileConnectionState(HEADSET / A2DP) as a cheap synchronous pre-check and BluetoothAdapter.getProfileProxy(...).getConnectedDevices() to identify the device; BluetoothManager.getConnectedDevices is GATT-only and useless for a car head unit.

BluetoothManager.getConnectedDevices(profile) parameter is documented as 'GATT or GATT_SERVER'. BluetoothAdapter.getProfileConnectionState(profile): 'can be used to check whether the local Bluetooth adapter is connected to any remote device for a specific profile. Profile can be one of BluetoothProfile.HEADSET, BluetoothProfile.A2DP', requires BLUETOOTH_CONNECT. getProfileProxy(context, listener, profile) delivers the proxy asynchronously on the main looper; then BluetoothHeadset/BluetoothA2dp.getConnectedDevices() can be matched against the truck MAC. Because the proxy is async, do the lookup inside the already-started FGS (or after the synchronous pre-check) rather than holding a receiver open with goAsync (receivers must finish in under 10 seconds). BluetoothDevice.isConnected() is not public SDK; do not use it.

- Confidence: high · Applies to: API 31+
- Source: https://developer.android.com/reference/android/bluetooth/BluetoothAdapter

### 13. BOOT_COMPLETED and MY_PACKAGE_REPLACED receivers may start a location FGS on Android 15+: location is not among the blocked types and is explicitly on the AOSP boot-completed allowlist. Background location is still required.

Android 15 (targetSdk 35+) blocks dataSync, camera, mediaPlayback, phoneCall, mediaProjection and microphone from BOOT_COMPLETED receivers (ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException). AOSP ActivityManagerConstants.DEFAULT_FGS_BOOT_COMPLETED_ALLOWLIST = CONNECTED_DEVICE | HEALTH | REMOTE_MESSAGING | SYSTEM_EXEMPTED | SPECIAL_USE | LOCATION. The boot broadcast carries a temp allowlist of DEFAULT_BOOT_TIME_TEMP_ALLOWLIST_DURATION = 20 s (REASON_BOOT_COMPLETED); MY_PACKAGE_REPLACED is sent with REASON_PACKAGE_REPLACED via the same mechanism. Needs RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED. Caveat: for apps targeting 13+, BOOT_COMPLETED is not delivered while the app is in the user-set 'Restricted' battery state. Since Android 15, BOOT_COMPLETED is also delivered when the app leaves the stopped state, so the same reconcile code runs after a force-stop is cleared.

- Confidence: high · Applies to: targetSdk 35+ on Android 15+
- Source: https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-15

### 14. CompanionDeviceManager presence observation works for classic Bluetooth: the system binds the app's CompanionDeviceService on connect and unbinds on disconnect, even if the app process is dead. The callback API changed twice, so three code paths are needed for minSdk 31.

startObservingDevicePresence(String) (API 31, deprecated in 36): 'The app doesn't need to remain running in order to receive its callbacks ... For Bluetooth classic devices this is triggered when the device connects/disconnects.' API 36 adds startObservingDevicePresence(ObservingDevicePresenceRequest) and CompanionDeviceService.onDevicePresenceEvent(DevicePresenceEvent) with EVENT_BT_CONNECTED / EVENT_BT_DISCONNECTED / EVENT_ASSOCIATION_REMOVED. onDeviceAppeared(String)/onDeviceDisappeared(String): added 31, deprecated 33. onDeviceAppeared(AssociationInfo)/onDeviceDisappeared(AssociationInfo): added 33, deprecated 36. Requires REQUEST_OBSERVE_COMPANION_DEVICE_PRESENCE, uses-feature android.software.companion_device_setup, and a service declared with permission BIND_COMPANION_DEVICE_SERVICE and action android.companion.CompanionDeviceService. 'The system binding CompanionDeviceService elevates the priority of the process'. Minor API levels 37.1/37.2 add trust-pairing callbacks that MilO does not need.

- Confidence: high · Applies to: API 31 to 37
- Source: https://developer.android.com/reference/android/companion/CompanionDeviceManager

### 15. AOSP CDM details that help the reboot case: a Bluetooth connection seen before the user unlocks is queued and delivered on unlock, and calling startObservingDevicePresence while the device is already connected fires the callback immediately. Field reports say presence callbacks can still be unreliable after reboot on some phones, so CDM should be one of several triggers.

DevicePresenceProcessor.onBluetoothCompanionDeviceConnected: if !isUserUnlockingOrUnlocked it calls onDeviceLocked, which stores a pending EVENT_BT_CONNECTED, and sendDevicePresenceEventOnUnlocked replays it. startObservingDevicePresence: 'Send callback immediately if the device is present' (but it is a no-op if already observing). CDM binds non-self-managed apps with BIND_ALMOST_PERCEPTIBLE (no while-in-use, no background-activity privilege). android12-release already has a BluetoothDeviceConnectedListener and android14-release has pending-connected-device handling for locked users. CDM discovery (CompanionDeviceDiscoveryService.checkBoundDevicesIfNeeded) matches already-bonded devices for single-device requests, so the truck need not be discoverable during association if the request uses BluetoothDeviceFilter with the MAC and setSingleDevice(true). Counter-evidence: android/platform-samples issue 159 (closed stale, 2024) reports onDeviceAppeared not firing after reboot on Pixel 6a, Nothing Phone 2 and OnePlus 8 Pro on Android 13 while Samsung devices worked.

- Confidence: medium · Applies to: AOSP 12 to main; OEM behaviour varies
- Source: https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/main/services/companion/java/com/android/server/companion/devicepresence/DevicePresenceProcessor.java

### 16. The location FGS type has no runtime timeout on Android 15, 16 or 17; only dataSync and mediaProcessing (6 hours per 24 hours) and shortService (about 3 minutes) time out.

Timeout page (2026-10-01): 'The system permits dataSync and mediaProcessing foreground services to run for a total of 6 hours in a 24-hour period, after which the system calls Service.onTimeout(int, int)'. AOSP constants confirm DEFAULT_DATA_SYNC_FGS_TIMEOUT_DURATION and DEFAULT_MEDIA_PROCESSING_FGS_TIMEOUT_DURATION of 6 h and DEFAULT_SHORT_FGS_TIMEOUT_DURATION of 3 min; nothing for location. Do not use dataSync or shortService for trip recording. Also from AOSP: DEFAULT_FGS_START_FOREGROUND_TIMEOUT_MS is 10 s, so call startForeground at the top of onStartCommand.

- Confidence: high · Applies to: targetSdk 35+
- Source: https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/timeout

### 17. Android 16 (API 36) and Android 17 (API 37) add no new restriction on starting or running a location FGS from Bluetooth or CDM triggers. Android 17 appears to be the latest stable release (API 37), but that stable status is confirmed only by secondary sources.

Android 16 all-apps: jobs running concurrently with an FGS now obey job runtime quotas (do not hang trip work on WorkManager); ordered-broadcast priority is no longer global; improved Bluetooth bond-loss handling; CDM discovery timeouts no longer reported to apps. Android 16 targeting: ACTION_KEY_MISSING / ACTION_ENCRYPTION_CHANGE intents, CDM removeBond. Android 17 targeting: ACCESS_LOCAL_NETWORK runtime permission, background-audio hardening, BAL hardening, RFCOMM read() returns -1; Android 17 all-apps: autonomous Bluetooth re-pairing, app memory limits. None touch FGS location starts, ACL broadcasts or background location. developer.android.com shows Android 17 with API 37.1/37.2 minor levels and QPR1/QPR2 betas (which follow a stable release); web search results (Wikipedia and others) give a stable release date of 2026-06-16. The SDK platform release-notes page still tops out at Android 16, so confirm API 37 in the SDK Manager before setting targetSdk.

- Confidence: medium · Applies to: Android 16 and 17
- Source: https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-17

### 18. Denying POST_NOTIFICATIONS does not block the FGS; the notice just moves to the Task Manager. Since Android 14 the user can swipe away an ongoing FGS notification, which does not stop the service.

Android 13: 'If the user denies the notification permission, they don't see notices related to foreground services in the notification drawer. However, users still see notices related to foreground services in the Task Manager, regardless of whether the notification permission is granted.' Android 14: FLAG_ONGOING_EVENT notifications become user-dismissible except while the phone is locked or via Clear all (exceptions: CallStyle, DPC, media, default Search Selector). The change is described purely as a notification-dismissal change. Android 12+: display of an FGS notification may be delayed by up to 10 seconds with a few exceptions. MilO should still request POST_NOTIFICATIONS because the fallback 'could not auto-start, tap to start' notification is useless without it.

- Confidence: high · Applies to: Android 13+ / 14+
- Source: https://developer.android.com/about/versions/13/behavior-changes-13

### 19. The Task Manager Stop button kills the whole app with no callback but does not put it in the stopped state, so the next ACL_CONNECTED or CDM event still starts a trip. The in-progress trip is cut off and must be closed out on next start.

On Stop: 'The system removes your app from memory. Therefore, your entire app stops'; back stack removed; FGS notification removed; 'Your app remains in history. Scheduled jobs execute at their scheduled time. Alarms go off at their scheduled time or time window.' 'The system doesn't send your app any callbacks'; check ApplicationExitInfo REASON_USER_REQUESTED on next start. Test with: adb shell cmd activity stop-app PACKAGE_NAME. Persist trip points to Room continuously so a stopped trip can be finalised (or resumed if the truck is still connected) at next process start.

- Confidence: high · Applies to: Android 13+
- Source: https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/handle-user-stopping

### 20. ACTION_REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS is allowed for a sideloaded app and is useful: it is a documented FGS background-start exemption, exempts the app from App Standby Bucket restrictions, and allows network and partial wake locks in Doze. The only prohibition is a Google Play policy, which does not apply.

REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS is a normal-level permission. Doze page: 'Google Play policies prohibit apps from requesting direct exemption ... unless the core function of the app is adversely affected'; apps on the list can 'use the network and hold partial wake locks during Doze and App Standby' but regular alarms still do not fire in Doze. App-standby page: 'Apps that are on the Doze exemption list are exempted from the App Standby Bucket-based restrictions.' Doze itself (network suspended, wake locks ignored, jobs and standard alarms deferred) matters little while driving because deep Doze needs a stationary device, but it means the disconnect grace timer should run inside the live FGS rather than on WorkManager or a standard alarm. Check status with PowerManager.isIgnoringBatteryOptimizations().

- Confidence: high · Applies to: Android 6+
- Source: https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby

### 21. MilO is exempt from the automatic 'restricted' standby bucket twice over (companion device app, and holder of ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION), but the user-chosen 'Restricted' battery setting breaks the design completely.

Restricted-bucket exemptions include 'Companion device apps' and 'Apps that are granted ... ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION', plus apps the user set to unrestricted. Otherwise Android 13+ moves an app to restricted after 8 days without interaction (45 days on Android 12). In the user-set 'Restricted' battery state: 'Can't launch foreground services; Existing foreground services are removed from the foreground; Alarms aren't triggered; Jobs aren't executed', and for targetSdk 33+ BOOT_COMPLETED is not delivered. The status screen should check ActivityManager.isBackgroundRestricted() and warn.

- Confidence: high · Applies to: Android 12+
- Source: https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/appstandby

### 22. App hibernation is a real long-term threat to a set-and-forget app: after a few months without user interaction Android resets runtime permissions (including location and Nearby devices) and blocks background work. The owner should disable it for MilO.

On Android 12+ hibernation resets runtime permissions, stops background jobs/alerts and clears cache. Counts as usage: activity resumed, widget interaction, notification interaction (dismissal does not count), and 'Service or content provider bound by another app or OS' (so CDM binding likely counts, though not stated for CDM specifically). Does NOT count: implicit broadcasts received, jobs, alarms; so the ACL receiver path alone will not keep the app alive. There is no companion-device exemption in the documented list. Check with PackageManagerCompat.getUnusedAppRestrictionsStatus and send the owner to IntentCompat.createManageUnusedAppRestrictionsIntent to turn off 'Pause app activity if unused' (Android 13+ label).

- Confidence: high · Applies to: targetSdk 30+ on Android 12+
- Source: https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/app-hibernation

### 23. Android developer verification does not block this app: installs via ADB (which is what Android Studio uses) are exempt.

FAQ: 'As a developer, you are free to install apps without verification with ADB.' and 'Apps installed using ADB won't require verification.' Enforcement began 2026-09-30 for certified devices in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand, with global expansion from 2027. Sharing an APK file by other means would later need a limited-distribution account (up to 20 devices) or the user-side advanced flow.

- Confidence: high · Applies to: Certified Android devices, 2026 onward
- Source: https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq

## What the verifier said the research missed

- CDM and the battery-optimisation allowlist fight each other on Android 12-16 (verified in AOSP release branches). android12/13/14/15-release updateSpecialAccessPermissionAsSystem: if the app requests REQUEST_COMPANION_RUN_IN_BACKGROUND it is ADDED to the permanent power allowlist at association time and on every package update; if it does not, it is REMOVED (undoing a user-granted exemption, including on every Android Studio reinstall). android16-release and android16-qpr2-release CompanionExemptionProcessor: added only while the device is present (and only with that permission), and removeFromPermanentAllowList is called on every disconnect regardless. android-17.0.0_r1 fixes this with CompanionExemptionStore (isUserOverridden / isCdmAllowlisted). Source: https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/android16-release/services/companion/java/com/android/server/companion/CompanionExemptionProcessor.java
- AOSP refs/heads/main is stale and must not be cited as current. The android-17.0.0_r1 tag differs from main in files this design depends on (ActiveServices is ~900 lines longer; Bluetooth Utils.java became Util.kt; CDM exemption code moved to powerexemption/). All AOSP evidence should be pinned to release tags/branches (android-17.0.0_r1, android16-release, etc.).
- Android 17 ACL broadcast semantics changed (undocumented): RemoteDevices.aclStateChangeCallback now sets BroadcastOptions.setDeliveryGroupPolicy(DELIVERY_GROUP_POLICY_MOST_RECENT) with matching key 'bluetooth.ACL_CONNECTION' + transport/address, so CONNECTED and DISCONNECTED for the same device share one delivery group and an older undelivered one is dropped. On a quick drop-and-reconnect MilO may see only CONNECTED; on a quick connect-then-drop only DISCONNECTED. Trip logic must be level-triggered: CONNECTED while recording cancels the grace timer; DISCONNECTED with no trip is a no-op; never assume paired edges.
- Receiver export rules for Bluetooth broadcasts: the sender is com.android.bluetooth (sharedUserId android.uid.bluetooth), not system_server, and ActivityManager.canAccessUnexportedComponents only allows root and system UIDs. ## Recommendations

- ONE-TIME SETUP (in-app, foreground, in this order): (1) request BLUETOOTH_CONNECT; (2) request ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION + ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION and insist on Precise; (3) separately request ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION and guide the owner to 'Allow all the time'; (4) request POST_NOTIFICATIONS; (5) let the owner pick the truck from BluetoothAdapter.getBondedDevices() and store its MAC; (6) CDM associate with AssociationRequest + BluetoothDeviceFilter.setAddress(mac) + setSingleDevice(true), then start presence observation (API 36+: ObservingDevicePresenceRequest with the association id; API 31-35: startObservingDevicePresence(mac)); (7) fire ACTION_REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS; (8) send the owner to the unused-app-restrictions screen to turn off 'Pause app activity if unused'.
- EXACT START SEQUENCE: use three redundant triggers that all call one idempotent function TripStarter.start(context, reason). Trigger A: CompanionDeviceService (onDevicePresenceEvent EVENT_BT_CONNECTED on API 36+, onDeviceAppeared(AssociationInfo) on 33-35, onDeviceAppeared(String) on 31-32). Trigger B: manifest receiver (exported=true, the sender is the Bluetooth UID and the actions are protected broadcasts) for ACL_CONNECTED / ACL_DISCONNECTED, optionally also BluetoothHeadset and BluetoothA2dp ACTION_CONNECTION_STATE_CHANGED; compare EXTRA_DEVICE address to the truck MAC and return immediately if different. Trigger C: BOOT_COMPLETED and MY_PACKAGE_REPLACED receiver plus MainActivity.onStart, which run a reconcile step.
- Inside TripStarter.start, synchronously and before returning from onReceive / the CDM callback: preflight that ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION, ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION and BLUETOOTH_CONNECT are granted, location is enabled, and the app is not background-restricted. If preflight passes call ContextCompat.startForegroundService(TripService). If it fails do NOT start the service; post a high-importance 'MilO could not auto-start this trip, tap to start' notification (a notification tap gives both the start exemption and while-in-use) and write a diagnostics row.
- In TripService.onStartCommand, as the first statement, call ServiceCompat.startForeground(this, id, notification, FOREGROUND_SERVICE_TYPE_LOCATION) inside try/catch for ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException and SecurityException; on failure log, post the tap-to-start notification and stopSelf. Only after startForeground succeeds request location updates, open the trip row in Room, and register a context receiver for ACL_DISCONNECTED. Never route the start through WorkManager, a delayed coroutine or an alarm: the Bluetooth and boot temp allowlists are 20 seconds and jobs are quota-limited on Android 16+.
- RECONCILE (boot, package replaced, app opened, and service restart): call BluetoothAdapter.getProfileConnectionState(HEADSET) and (A2DP); if either is connected, start the FGS and inside it obtain the HEADSET/A2DP profile proxies and check getConnectedDevices() for the truck MAC; stop the service within a few seconds if the truck is not there. Also close out or resume any trip row left open by a process kill.
- TRIP END: on ACL_DISCONNECTED (context receiver in the service, backed by the manifest receiver and the CDM disappeared/EVENT_BT_DISCONNECTED callback) start the grace timer inside the running FGS (Handler or coroutine, not WorkManager or a standard alarm, which Doze defers); cancel it if the truck reconnects; otherwise finalise the trip, stopForeground(STOP_FOREGROUND_REMOVE) and stopSelf.
- Do not rely on the Bluetooth-broadcast FGS exemption alone: it is present in AOSP but no longer on the documented list. The CDM association with REQUEST_COMPANION_START_FOREGROUND_SERVICES_FROM_BACKGROUND and the battery-optimisation exemption are the documented guarantees, and both are permanent rather than time-windowed, which also covers sticky service restarts.

## Questions raised for the owner

- What phone is this (make, model, Android version)? Samsung, Xiaomi, OnePlus and similar add their own battery managers and 'sleeping apps' lists on top of stock Android, and the Companion Device Manager presence callbacks have field reports of being unreliable after reboot on some non-Samsung phones.
- Are you willing to grant location 'Allow all the time' with Precise on? Automatic trip start from a dead app is impossible without it on every Android version from 12 up; the only alternative is tapping a notification at the start of each trip.
- Are you willing to set MilO to unrestricted battery use and switch off 'Pause app activity if unused'? Without the second one Android will silently strip the app's permissions after a few months of you not opening it.
- After a phone reboot, auto-start cannot work until you have unlocked the phone once. Is it acceptable that a trip begun before the first unlock is recorded only from the moment you unlock, or do you need full coverage in that case (significantly more work)?
- Does the phone ever connect to the truck's Bluetooth when you are not driving for business (truck idling in the yard, someone else driving with your phone nearby, personal trips)? This decides whether a Bluetooth connection alone should open a trip or whether a movement check is needed first.
- How long should the grace period be between a Bluetooth disconnect and the trip being closed (for example fuel stops or brief ignition-off stops)?
