# ADR-002: Trip detection and background start

Date: 2026-10-03
Status: Accepted (design). Amended 2026-10-03, twice (after the review of the phase 1 foundation, and when the recording core was built), 2026-10-05, four times (when the triggers were built, after the review of the recording core and the triggers, when the screens were built, and for the addresses), and 2026-10-06, four times (the driving alert, a new way for the system to start MilO that starts no trip, whose conditions were completed after a review the same day; that evening, the parked rule, which ends a trip when the truck has stood still although Bluetooth is still connected; the review of the parked rule; and, that night, the verification of the finished work before the second day in the truck). The changes are listed under "Amendments" at the end. Automatic start and stop were seen on the phone in the truck on 2026-10-06 (FINDINGS_LOG, 2026-10-06 (evening)); the parked rule of amendments 28 to 30 has not been.

## Context

MilO must start recording when the phone connects to the truck over classic Bluetooth, even when the app's process is dead, and end the trip after the truck disconnects. Shawn's current mileage app often misses trips, so reliable start is the top requirement. The target is one phone: a Xiaomi POCO X5 on Android 14 (HyperOS). The app targets API 37, but the behaviour that runs is Android 14's.

The research in `docs/research/` settled these facts. Each is one line here; the file named holds the evidence.

1. **Two separate permissions are needed to start from the background.** A background-start exemption lets the service start; "Allow all the time" location lets it read location. On Android 14, starting a location foreground service from the background without background location throws `SecurityException`. (`fgs-background-start`)
2. **Three exemptions can allow the start.** A companion device association with `REQUEST_COMPANION_START_FOREGROUND_SERVICES_FROM_BACKGROUND` (documented, lasts as long as the association). The battery-optimisation exemption (documented). A 20-second allowance the Bluetooth stack attaches to its connect broadcast (real in AOSP 12 to 14, not on the documented list). (`fgs-background-start`, `miui-dev-bluetooth-audio`)
3. **CompanionDeviceManager and the ACL broadcast are one detector with two delivery paths.** Both come from the same Bluetooth-stack event. What the companion path adds is that the system binds MilO's service directly, which starts a dead process on stock Android. (`cdm-presence`)
4. **A second, independent detector exists.** The hands-free and audio profile broadcasts (`BluetoothHeadset` and `BluetoothA2dp` `ACTION_CONNECTION_STATE_CHANGED`) may also be manifest-registered and come from a different part of the Bluetooth stack. (`cdm-presence`)
5. **Events are hints, not truth.** Companion callbacks can arrive in reverse order on a cold start, a quick drop-and-reconnect can deliver only one of the two broadcasts, and no event fires at all when the truck is already connected at boot, after an app update, or after the process is restarted. (`cdm-presence`, `fgs-background-start`)
6. **Android 14 has no public "is this device connected" call.** The available check is the hands-free and audio profile state (`getProfileConnectionState`, then a profile proxy's `getConnectedDevices()`). (`cdm-presence`)
7. **Receivers for Bluetooth broadcasts must be exported.** The sender is the Bluetooth app, not the system. A non-exported receiver never hears the disconnect and the trip never ends. (`fgs-background-start`)
8. **HyperOS Autostart gates every start of a dead app.** Decompiled system code shows the connect broadcast dropped and service starts and binds rejected while Autostart is off, and it is off by default for a sideloaded app. Unconfirmed on this phone. (`miui-background-limits`)
9. **Xiaomi's cleaners kill idle apps, and force stop leaves the app dead until it is opened.** The exit reason the system records names the cleaner. (`miui-background-limits`)
10. **`CarConnection` only works while MilO's process is alive.** It cannot wake the app, so it can hold a trip open but never start one. (`location-and-car`, `android-auto-screen`)
11. **After a reboot nothing is delivered until the first unlock.** (`cdm-presence`)

## Decision

### One entry point, many triggers

Every trigger calls the same function on one app-wide `TripController`. The function is idempotent: calling it twice, or in the wrong order, leaves the same result. Triggers call it synchronously. Nothing goes through WorkManager, an alarm or a delayed job, because the start allowance is measured in seconds. A manifest receiver keeps its broadcast open until the controller has dealt with the trigger, so the process cannot be frozen between the two.

| Trigger | What it is | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Companion service | `CompanionDeviceService` callbacks for the associated truck, plus a check in its `onCreate` | Dead process on stock Android; the standing start exemption |
| Bluetooth receiver | Exported manifest receiver for ACL connect and disconnect and for the hands-free and audio profile state changes, filtered to the truck's address | The backup path, and the independent detector |
| Reconcile | `BOOT_COMPLETED`, `MY_PACKAGE_REPLACED`, process start, app launch (every time MilO comes to the front), service restart, a truck just paired | Every case where no event fires |
| Manual | Start and End on the phone and on the Android Auto screen | Bluetooth never connected at all |

The driving alert only notifies (built on 2026-10-06, amendment 27). `CarConnection` only holds a trip open, or a wait beside the parked truck (amendment 29). Neither starts a trip.

### What counts as "the truck is connected"

- A connect event that carries the truck's address is trusted as it stands and starts the trip at once. The profiles connect a few seconds after the link, so checking them first would miss the start window.
- A companion "appeared" callback also starts the service at once. The service then confirms within 15 seconds that the truck really is connected (profile state, or an ACL connect seen for the address). If not, it stops, and the trip it had just opened is marked discarded as a false start, with the reason in the event log. It does not go through the grace period. A reading of "not connected" taken before the 15 seconds are up proves nothing, because the profiles connect after the link.
- The companion callback is believed only when no trip is open. It never marks the truck as seen in a trip that is already running: believed wrongly there, it would end a manual trip on a disconnect that never happened. With a trip open it does one thing only: it releases a hold-off that is more than 60 seconds old, and what is believed about the truck stands.
- Reconcile has no event to trust, so it reads the truck's connection: the hands-free and audio profile state up to Android 16, the link itself (`BluetoothDevice.isConnected`) from Android 16 QPR2.
- Only the link (ACL) broadcast of the classic transport is a connect or disconnect event. A hands-free or audio profile changing state, and a low-energy link to the truck's address, are prompts to read the connection: a profile can reconnect on a link that never dropped, and a low-energy link says nothing certain about the hands-free connection.
- A reading can come back "unknown": MilO is not allowed to use Bluetooth, or Bluetooth did not answer. Unknown is never "connected". A reconcile or a once-a-minute reading that comes back unknown changes nothing. A timer or a button has to act, so it goes by what is already believed about the truck. At a process start nothing is believed yet, and unknown counts as "not connected".
- A disconnect event starts the grace timer. When the timer ends, the profile state is read again before the trip is closed.
- While a trip is open, the service reads the truck's connection again about once a minute, to catch a disconnect that was never reported. Two readings of "not connected" in a row are treated as a disconnect and start the grace period. One reading alone is not enough, because the profile state can lag behind the link. A reading of "connected" is always believed.
- A reading of "not connected" counts against a link-level connect event only once a reading has shown the truck connected on that link. Until then it proves nothing, whether it was prompted by the minute timer, by a reconcile or by a profile broadcast: the profiles connect seconds after the link, and a truck can be connected without either of the two profiles a reading looks at. Such a reading is written to the event log and changes nothing. A disconnect event is still trusted as it stands.
- Only something that brings a fresh reading of the truck closes a trip whose time has run out: that reading, a connect or disconnect event, or a press of Start or End (each press reads the connection first). A GPS fix or an Android Auto change that arrives after the deadline knows nothing new about the truck, so it closes nothing. The trip waits for the reading.

### Trip rules

The rules are a pure Kotlin state machine in `core/trip/`, with unit tests and no Android types, so they can be tested without a phone.

- **Idle, truck connects:** a trip starts.
- **Recording, truck disconnects:** if Android Auto is still connected the trip carries on; otherwise the grace period starts (default 2 minutes).
- **Recording, and the truck has not really moved for the parked limit (default 10 minutes):** the trip is closed where and when it last moved, whatever started it and whatever is still connected, Android Auto included. If the truck is still connected, by Bluetooth or by Android Auto, MilO then waits beside it, and the truck moving starts the next trip (amendments 28 to 30).
- **Grace, truck reconnects:** the same trip carries on.
- **Grace ends:** the trip is closed at the time of the last recorded point, not at the end of the grace period.
- **A reconnect seen after the grace period has ended does not revive the trip.** The trip is closed as above and the truck starts a new one. The reading taken when the timer fires always arrives a little after the deadline, so a "connected" reading up to 30 seconds late still carries the same trip on. Later than that, the timer was lost (the process was frozen or killed) and the trip ended when its grace ran out.
- **Connect while recording, or disconnect while idle:** nothing happens. Events are never assumed to come in pairs.
- **Manual start:** starts a trip when idle. If the truck connects during it, it then behaves like an automatic trip.
- **Manual start during the grace period, truck still gone:** the waiting trip ends where the truck was found gone, and a manual trip starts. Otherwise the press would be swallowed and nothing would be recording once the grace period ran out.
- **Manual end while the truck is still connected:** the trip ends and automatic start is held off. Without this, the next reconcile would start a new trip straight away. The hold-off is released by whichever comes first: (a) any reading that shows the truck disconnected; (b) a link-level connect event (the ACL connect broadcast, or the companion "appeared" callback) that arrives more than 60 seconds after End was pressed; (c) 12 hours passing; (d) a trip that Shawn started by hand after that press being closed by the parked rule with the truck still connected, where the wait takes the hold-off's place (amendment 29). A missed trip is worse than an unwanted restart.
- **A manual trip with no truck connected** ends on End Trip, or by the parked rule like every other trip, so a forgotten one cannot run all night. Until 2026-10-06 it had a guard of its own, 30 minutes without movement, and Bluetooth trips were never split on stops; Shawn replaced both with the one parked rule (amendment 28).
- **A finished trip under the minimum distance** (default 0.3 km) is discarded, and the discard is written to the event log.

### State survives the process

The open trip is a row in the database with its status. On any restart the controller reads it, together with a fresh reading of the connection. A trip is carried on only if it can still be the same drive:

- **The trip was in its grace period.** It is judged by its stored deadline, exactly as above: over means closed at the last point, whatever is connected now; otherwise truck connected carries the same trip on, and truck gone resumes the grace timer.
- **The trip was recording.** If its newest stored point is more than 30 minutes old, nobody watched the truck in between, and the trip is closed at that point. Otherwise truck connected means carry on recording the same trip, and truck gone starts the grace period now. A trip that carries on is judged by the parked rule in the same step: if its last real movement is older than the limit it is closed there. So with the truck connected, a gap of more than the parked limit in the middle of a drive makes two trips of it (amendment 30).
- **MilO was waiting beside the parked truck** (amendments 28 and 29). The wait is stored. Truck still connected and the wait inside its three days: the wait carries on, and no trip starts. Truck read as gone: the wait ends, also one that Android Auto alone was holding, because Android Auto cannot be read before the service is up. Truck not readable: the wait carries on, and the next reading that can be had decides. Past the three days: a trip starts if the truck reads as connected, as at any restart.

In every case where the old trip is closed and the truck is connected, a new trip starts in the same step. Without these two limits, a process killed in the afternoon and woken by the truck the next morning would join both days into one trip.

### The service

`TripService` is a foreground service of type `location`.

1. The trigger runs a preflight: fine and background location granted, Bluetooth permission granted, location switched on, app not background-restricted.
2. If preflight fails, the service is not started. MilO posts a "could not start this trip, tap to start" notification and writes the reason to the event log. A tap on a notification is itself allowed to start the service.
3. Otherwise it calls `startForegroundService`. The first statement in `onStartCommand` is `startForeground` with the location type, inside a catch for `ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException` and `SecurityException`. A failure is logged and falls back to the same notification.
4. Only then does it request location, open or resume the trip row, register its own exported receiver for disconnect, observe `CarConnection`, and play the trip-start sound.
5. The grace timer, the timer of the parked limit and the once-a-minute reading of the truck's connection run inside the service. A GPS fix stands in for either when its timer is late: for a deadline once it is 10 seconds overdue, and for the minute reading once 70 seconds have passed since the last one. An overdue deadline is dealt with before the fix that revealed it is counted.

Step 4 is kept by the controller, not by the service: an event that would leave a trip open is not acted on while the service is not in the foreground. The service is asked to start with the trigger in its start intent, and it hands the trigger back to the controller once `startForeground` has succeeded. If Android refuses, no trip row is opened.

### Permissions declared

`BLUETOOTH_CONNECT`, fine, coarse and background location, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_LOCATION`, `POST_NOTIFICATIONS`, `RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED`, `REQUEST_OBSERVE_COMPANION_DEVICE_PRESENCE`, `REQUEST_COMPANION_START_FOREGROUND_SERVICES_FROM_BACKGROUND`, `REQUEST_COMPANION_RUN_IN_BACKGROUND`, and `REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS`. Not needed: `BLUETOOTH_SCAN`, exact alarms, internet. Two more permissions are declared that trip detection does not use: `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE`, for the address lookup (amendment 26), and `ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION`, for the driving alert (amendment 27).

All are in the manifest. `REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS` was the last to be added, on 2026-10-05 with the permission checklist (the Setup screen): it only allows MilO to ask for the exemption, and the checklist's battery row is the one place that asks (STANDARDS §12: declare only what built code uses).

Both companion permissions are declared on purpose. Up to Android 15, an app with an association but without `REQUEST_COMPANION_RUN_IN_BACKGROUND` is removed from the battery allowlist on every reinstall.

### Pairing

Onboarding lists the phone's paired devices, Shawn picks the truck, and MilO associates it using an address filter with single-device mode and no device profile. That combination is the only one that matches an already-paired device without a scan. MilO then starts observing presence. At every process start and every time it is opened, it checks the association still exists and re-arms observation.

The request to associate goes through the pairing screen's Activity: Android shows its consent dialog on top of it. (Android 12 refused the request from any other context. MilO has not run on Android 12 since 2026-10-07, and the call was left as it is.) On a phone without companion device support the truck is stored anyway, so the Bluetooth receiver knows which device to listen for. Only the pairing screen can do that: without an association there is nothing to adopt (amendment 19).

### Evidence from day one

Because none of this is proven on the phone, phase 1 writes to the event log:
- every trigger, with its source, the state before and after, and whether the service started. Two things are left out, because they arrive all day and usually change nothing: a GPS fix, and a once-a-minute reading that only confirms what is already believed. Either is logged when it does change something;
- every service start, stop and failure, with the exception;
- every process start, with the reason the last process died (which names Xiaomi's cleaner if one killed it);
- every grace timer start, cancel and expiry, and every Android Auto connection change.

### Where the code lives

- `core/trip/`: the state machine, the distance calculation and the point filter. Pure Kotlin.
- `platform/trip/`: `TripController`, `TripService`, location recording, notifications, the sound.
- `platform/bluetooth/`: the receiver, the companion service, association, the connection check.
- `platform/car/`: the Android Auto classes. The screen reads the controller's state directly and has no ViewModel. The `CarConnection` watcher is here too.
- `platform/system/`: permission and Xiaomi settings checks.
- `data/`: the databases, repositories and settings.

Phone screens reach the controller through their ViewModels.

## Consequences

**Better**
- No single trigger is a point of failure, and a missed trip leaves a record of which trigger did or did not fire.
- The trip rules can be tested exhaustively without a phone.
- A killed process picks its trip back up.

**Worse**
- Autostart on HyperOS is a hard prerequisite that MilO can only partly verify. If it is off, every automatic path fails together. The permission checklist is the only defence.
- More code than a single receiver: three trigger classes, a reconcile step and a state machine.
- `minSdk` is 31, so the companion and notification code needs branches for Android 12 and 13 that this phone will never run. Raising `minSdk` to 34 would delete them. That is Shawn's call; the brief says 31. *(Decided and done: Shawn chose "Android 14 and newer" on 2026-10-06. Since 2026-10-07 `minSdk` is 34 and those branches are gone. See the findings log, 2026-10-07.)*

**Deferred, decided by the phase 1 test drives**
- **An always-on service.** If starting from a dead process proves unreliable even with Autostart on, the fallback is a small permanent foreground service that keeps the process alive and listens in code. It costs a permanent notification and some battery, so it needs Shawn's agreement and is not built now.
- **Activity recognition as an extra wake path.** Its delivery uses an explicit target, which may get past the Autostart gate. The path exists since 2026-10-06, for the driving alert only (amendment 27), and is still untested on the phone: device checks DA-8 and DA-9 are the test. Whatever they show, a report of driving starts no trip; using it for more than a notification would be a new decision.

**Open, and assumed for now**
- A trip that begins before the first unlock after a reboot starts recording at unlock.
- Reverse geocoding of start and finish addresses was to arrive in phase 2 with the day view. It was built on 2026-10-05, on its own (amendment 26); the day view is still phase 2.

## Amendments

**2026-10-03, review of the phase 1 foundation.** Four points, each written into the text above. The reasons and the numbers are in FINDINGS_LOG under the same date. The two limits are judgements made without Shawn, and his to change.

1. **Only a fresh reading of the truck closes a trip on a timeout.** The first code closed an overdue grace period on any event, including a GPS fix. A fix handled a moment before the timer's reading then cut one drive in two. This is what "read again before the trip is closed" always meant; it is now a rule of the state machine.
2. **A late reconnect does not revive a trip.** "Grace ends: the trip is closed" and "truck connected means carry on" contradicted each other when the timer was lost. The first now wins, with a 30-second tolerance for a timer that fires late.
3. **A restart long after the last recorded point closes the trip.** "Truck connected means carry on" had no time limit. The limit is 30 minutes.
4. **Start during the grace period is not swallowed.**

**2026-10-03, the recording core (work package 2, part A).** Points 5 to 7 were decided by the orchestrating session before the code was written and are written into the text above. Points 8 to 10 were decided while building, because the text above did not settle them. All are Shawn's to change; the reasons and the numbers are in FINDINGS_LOG under the same date.

5. **What releases the hold-off.** "Until the truck next disconnects" swallowed the next trip whenever that disconnect was never seen (the app was dead). Now three things release it, whichever comes first: a reading that shows the truck disconnected; a link-level connect event more than 60 seconds after End was pressed; 12 hours. A new link can only form after the old one dropped, so the connect event proves the disconnect nobody saw. The 60 seconds ignore late duplicates of the connect event from the connection that was already up. The 12 hours are applied at the next reading of the truck, so that the trip they allow starts on a fresh reading. Pressing End again with the truck connected restarts the hold-off from that press.
6. **Catching a lost disconnect.** While a trip is open the service reads the truck's connection about once a minute. Two readings of "not connected" in a row start the grace period.
7. **A false start from the companion callback.** A trip opened by the "appeared" callback alone is discarded if the truck's connection is not confirmed within 15 seconds, by a link-level connect event for the truck or by the profile state. The service stops at once: no grace period.
8. **Android Auto's "connected" is not believed for ever.** The value comes from the Android Auto app and nothing guarantees it ever goes back. Two guards: while Android Auto is believed connected the service makes the library ask again once a minute; and once Android Auto alone (the truck gone) has held a trip open for 12 hours it is taken for stuck and no longer believed, until it has been seen to report "not connected". 12 hours, so that a real working day on Android Auto with Bluetooth down is never cut.
9. **The preflight is the same for every start.** Background location is required even for a press of Start with the app open, which Android itself would allow without it. A phone on which only manual trips work should say so at once. The Bluetooth permission joins the preflight with the Bluetooth code.
10. **A tap on the "could not start" notification starts the service directly,** with no preflight: the tap is one of the moments Android allows the start. If it still fails, the same notification is posted again.

**2026-10-05, the triggers (work package 2, part B).** Decided while building the Bluetooth receiver, the companion service, pairing and the connection check, because the text above did not settle them. Points 11 to 13 are written into the text above. All are Shawn's to change; the reasons are in FINDINGS_LOG under the same date.

11. **A reading of the truck can be "unknown", and unknown is never "connected".** Without the Bluetooth permission, or when a Bluetooth profile does not answer, the phone cannot say. What a trigger does with it: a reconcile or a once-a-minute reading changes nothing (a trip does not start on it, and a trip that is recording is not put into its grace period by it); a timer or a button goes by what is already believed, so a grace period still runs out and a companion start that nothing confirmed is still a false start; a process start, where nothing is believed yet, counts it as "not connected".
12. **Only the classic link broadcast is a connect or disconnect event.** The hands-free and audio profile broadcasts are the independent detector, and they prompt a reading. Trusted as link events, a profile that reconnects on a link that never dropped would release the hold-off 60 seconds after End was pressed.
13. **A manifest broadcast is kept open until the controller has dealt with it,** for 8 seconds at most. A trigger that needs a reading first is handled a moment after `onReceive` returns, and a process with nothing else running may be frozen in between.
14. **The companion "disappeared" callback is trusted as a disconnect,** like the link broadcast. If it is wrong, the next reading inside the grace period cancels it. Android 16's "Bluetooth connected" presence event is still treated as "appeared" and has to be confirmed: Android does not say which transport connected.
15. **The reconcile at app launch runs every time MilO comes to the front,** not once per Activity. Android keeps an Activity for days, and opening MilO is what Shawn does when a trip did not start.
16. **A lone association with no truck stored is adopted as the truck.** In ordinary use the two are made together. It is what lets automatic start be tried on the phone before the pairing screen exists, with the association made over adb.
17. **`REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS` waits for the permission checklist,** the only code that will ask for the exemption. (Done: see 25.)

**2026-10-05, the review of the recording core and the triggers (work package 2).** Four reviewers read the code. Points 18 to 23 change the text above; point 24 only makes it exact. Point 18 changes a rule the orchestrating session had decided (amendment 6), so it is Shawn's to confirm first. The reasons are in FINDINGS_LOG under the same date.

18. **A reading of "not connected" proves nothing until a reading has shown the truck connected on its present link.** Amendment 6 counted every such reading of the minute timer. On Android 14 a reading sees only the hands-free and the audio profile. With a truck whose link is up but which is on neither profile, the first two readings ended every trip four minutes after it started, and nothing started the next one, because the link never dropped. The same reading taken by a reconcile seconds after the link was made (the profiles follow the link) put a trip into its grace period at once. Now neither counts until a reading has seen the truck. The price: on such a truck, and in the first moments of any trip, a disconnect that is never reported is not caught by a reading. A disconnect event still ends the trip. Device check 32 shows whether this truck is affected.
19. **Only a device paired with the phone is adopted, and an adopted truck can be replaced the same way.** Amendment 16 adopted any lone association. One mistyped digit in the adb command was then stored as the truck for good. Now the association's address must be in the phone's list of paired devices. And when the stored truck's own association is gone, a lone association for a paired device is adopted in its place: the way back from a wrong truck, and the way to pair another one, while there is no pairing screen.
20. **A GPS fix stands in for the minute timer too,** as it already did for a deadline, and the readings stay about a minute apart whichever of the two prompts them: two readings seconds apart would be worth no more than one.
21. **An overdue deadline is judged before the fix that reveals it is counted.** Counted first, the first fix after a long silence moved the no-movement limit of a forgotten manual trip on, and the trip stayed open.
22. **The companion callback with a trip open leaves what is believed about the truck alone.** It released an old hold-off by way of "the truck is no longer known to be connected", which started the open trip's grace period.
23. **A trip nothing is recording is not kept in memory.** When the trip service is lost and Android refuses to start it again, the controller drops what it holds, and the next trigger picks the stored trip up through the restart rules, which close it if it has gone stale. After a failure in storage the controller reads storage again at once, for the same reason.
24. **A profile that drops can release the hold-off.** Amendment 12 keeps a profile that reconnects from counting as a new link. It does not stop the release by a reading: when the only connected profile drops, the reading it prompts says "not connected", which releases the hold-off as decided ("any reading that shows the truck disconnected"), and the profile's return then starts a trip. Unchanged, because a missed trip is worse than an unwanted restart; device check 47 shows whether it happens with this truck.

25. **`REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS` is declared.** The permission checklist exists as the Setup screen, and its battery row asks for the exemption with Android's own dialog. This closes point 17.

**2026-10-05, the addresses (work package 4).** One point, written into the text above.

26. **Addresses are looked up beside the recording, never inside it, and `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE` is declared for them.** Reverse geocoding was pulled forward from phase 2. `platform/address/TripAddresses` reads the controller's published state and writes only the address columns of a finished trip, so nothing in this record changes: no trigger, rule or effect knows about addresses. The one addition to the controller's side is that `CurrentTrip` carries the position of the trip's first usable fix. **2026-10-06, the driving alert (phase 2).** One point. The text above is changed in three places (the line under the table of triggers, "Permissions declared", "Deferred"); nothing about how a trip starts, is recorded or ends is changed.

27. **Driving detection is a new way for the system to start MilO's process, and it starts no trip.** MilO asks Google Play services' Activity Recognition to report when the phone enters or leaves a vehicle, and the reports are delivered through a `PendingIntent` to a receiver in the manifest (`platform/driving/DrivingReceiver`). So there is now one more thing Android can start a dead MilO for, beside the Bluetooth broadcast, the companion binding, boot and update. It differs from those in what it may do: **it never calls `TripController.onTrigger`.** A report of driving leads to a notification at most, when the work schedule says it is work time, no trip is open, a truck is paired and not known to be connected, no trip ended in the last 5 minutes and no alert was posted in the last 30 (APP_ENCYCLOPEDIA, "Safety net"). The trip starts only when Shawn taps the notification, through the path point 10 already set out for the "could not start this trip" notification: the tap starts the trip service directly with a manual start. The reason is the one this record gave from the first day: the phone cannot tell the truck from any other vehicle.
    - **Kept apart by construction, not by care.** The class that judges a report is handed the question "is a trip open?", what the controller publishes, and the controller's `whenCaughtUp`; it is given neither the controller nor the trip storage.
    - **The process start it causes still runs the reconcile.** However MilO's process is started, `MiloApplication` has the controller read the truck and pick up the stored trip. So a report of driving that wakes a dead MilO while the truck is in fact connected starts the trip after all, by the reconcile, exactly as opening MilO by hand would. That is the existing rule ("process start" in the table of triggers), not the alert starting a trip, and it is the one way in which this wake path can help automatic start: on a phone where HyperOS drops the Bluetooth broadcast and the companion binding for a dead app but lets this delivery through.
    - **Whether HyperOS lets it through is not known.** The research has the Autostart gate drop manifest broadcasts to a dead app, and expects a dead app's receiver to need Autostart for this delivery too (`docs/research/2026-10-03-location-and-car.md`, finding 32). This record's "Deferred" list noted that a delivery which names its target class may be treated differently. Neither has been tried. Device checks DA-8 and DA-9 run it with Autostart off and on.
    - **An unknown reading of a paired truck shows the alert.** Point 11 keeps "unknown" from ever starting or ending a trip. The alert decides the other way, because a notification is all it can cause, and a phone that cannot read Bluetooth is a phone on which no trip starts by itself. With no truck paired at all there is no alert: there is nothing a trip could be for.
    - **Two spans of time keep one drive from alerting more than once,** both read from storage so that a restart of the process forgets neither: no alert within 5 minutes of the end of the newest closed trip, and no second alert within 30 minutes of the last. The phone can report the same drive late or again, and "left" then "entered" at a stop (the research's finding 34). The cost is in APP_ENCYCLOPEDIA's gotchas: within the half hour a later drive without Bluetooth brings no alert.
    - **The tap skips the preflight, as point 10 already lets the "could not start this trip" tap do.** There a preflight has already run and what it found is in the event log; before the alert's tap none has run. With location switched off for the whole phone the tap starts a trip that records nothing, where Home's Start button would refuse. A reviewer saw it on an emulator; it is left as it is and logged as debt (FINDINGS_LOG, 2026-10-06), because the two ways out each change something this record decides: which path a tap takes, or whether an unreadable Bluetooth still shows the alert.
    - **A failure inside the alert is kept from the recording.** It shares the process with the trip service, so every piece of its work catches all exceptions and logs them (`platform/driving/DrivingFailures`).
    - **The request is renewed at every process start,** which covers the boot and the update the reconcile receiver already wakes MilO for; Google does not say that a request outlives either. `ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION` is declared, a runtime permission asked for on the Setup screen as a recommended row.
    - Three judgements made while building, Shawn's to change: a report of entering a vehicle more than 5 minutes old is not acted on; the alert is switched on out of the box; and the Physical activity row is recommended, so its absence does not raise the home screen's warning. The reasons are in FINDINGS_LOG under the same date.
    - **Changed by the review of the same day** (FINDINGS_LOG, 2026-10-06, the `[FIX]` entry): as first built, the alert was shown with no truck paired, had neither span of time, and let an exception out into the application scope. The four points above that say otherwise are what the code does now.

**2026-10-06 (evening), the parked rule.** One point, with nine parts. It replaces two things this record decided: "a trip runs from Bluetooth connect to disconnect, and nothing else splits it" (Shawn's choice at kickoff), and the 30-minute no-movement guard for manual trips. Shawn decided it himself, with the first day's data in front of him: the truck stayed connected for an hour after it was parked at home, so the last trip of the day never ended (FINDINGS_LOG, 2026-10-06 (evening)). The text above is changed in "Trip rules", "State survives the process" and "The service". How a trip starts from a connect, the companion callback, a reconcile or a button, and how a disconnect and the grace period end one, are not changed.

28. **A trip ends when the truck has not really moved for the parked limit, and while the truck stays connected MilO waits for it to move.**
    - **The rule.** While a trip is being recorded (no grace period running, no companion start waiting to be confirmed), and its last real movement is older than the parked limit, it is closed at that movement: its end time and end position are the last movement's, and what was recorded afterwards counts for nothing. The limit is a setting, 10 minutes out of the box, 5 to 30 on the Settings screen. It applies to every trip, however it started. Android Auto being connected does not hold such a trip open: a parked truck is parked. A trip in its grace period keeps the grace rules, so a disconnect during the stop ends the trip where the truck was found gone, as before. Like every timeout, the close waits for a fresh reading of the truck (the timer's own, the minute check, a press), because what follows depends on whether the truck is still connected.
    - **"Really moved" is what the distance calculation counts,** and nothing else: further than the fixes' own stated error can explain, and not one bad fix that the next fix takes back. The trip rules are told "moved" for each fix that adds distance, and "that was one bad fix" when the calculation takes a step back, so a stray fix does not keep a parked trip open and a slow crawl does not end one. The price is the calculation's own: a phone carried about near the truck is movement, as it always was.
    - **Waiting.** If the truck is still connected when the trip is closed, and automatic start is not held off, MilO waits: no trip is open, the wait is stored in the settings file with the place the truck is parked at (the closed trip's end position), and the trip service stays in the foreground with a quiet notification that says so. It reads the position once every 30 seconds, a sixth of the recording rate, with the same high-accuracy fixes: the rule that tells movement from jitter rests on their stated accuracy, and a cheaper kind of position (Wi-Fi, cell towers) would start trips on its own errors. What that costs the battery over a night has not been measured (device check 271). The once-a-minute reading of the truck goes on, and amendment 18 holds for it as during a trip. As first built Android Auto was not watched during the wait; amendment 29 changed that.
    - **The truck moves.** It has moved once two fixes in a row say so, which is up to a minute after it drove off; "in a row" is at most two minutes apart (amendment 29). A trip then starts, dated at the first of those fixes. A second fix that comes later than the two minutes still starts the trip, dated at that second fix (amendment 30). It is given the parked place as its first point, and the fixes that showed the movement after it, so it starts where the truck was parked and the first stretch is counted. It is a truck trip like any other from there on. No trip-start sound: the sound means "connected to the truck", and nothing connected.
    - **The wait ends without a trip** when the truck is seen gone (a disconnect event, a reconcile's reading, the second minute reading in a row) and Android Auto is not connected either (amendment 29): the service stops and nothing is recorded. A new link to the truck (the ACL connect broadcast, the companion callback) ends the wait and starts a trip exactly as it does when idle, with the sound. Start pressed starts a manual trip at once. End pressed sets the hold-off as it always does and ends the wait, since movement must start nothing while the hold-off stands. That is a rule for a press that crosses with a trip being closed: while MilO waits, the one button on Home and on the Android Auto screen reads "Start trip", so there is no End to press (amendment 30). As first built, no wait began either when a trip was parked while the hold-off was still set (Shawn pressed End, then Start, with the truck connected throughout); amendment 29 changed that, because the drive after such a stop was recorded by nothing.
    - **Three days.** After 72 hours of waiting (12 as first built; amendment 29) MilO stops watching, so that a truck which never disconnects cannot drain the battery, and says so in the log. The truck is then connected, parked and not watched. What starts the next trip: a new link to the truck; any reconcile that still reads it connected (MilO opened, its process restarted, a boot, an update, a profile changing state), which starts a trip as a reconcile always has, and if the truck is in fact still standing the parked rule closes that trip and the wait begins again; and Start. The driving alert covers the rest: in this one state it is shown although the truck reads as connected, because nothing else will start a trip (amendment 27 is changed in that single condition). Outside the work hours nothing does, and a drive then goes unrecorded.
    - **A restart.** No stored wait and the truck connected: a trip starts, the proven rule, unchanged. It is the choice that cannot lose a real drive: the trip records at once at the full rate, and if the truck is standing the parked rule closes it after the limit, discards it as too short (one line in the log) and begins the wait. Going straight to waiting would save ten minutes of GPS and would cost the first minute of a drive that was already under way. A stored wait is found again as a wait.
    - **The timers of the parked rule read the truck while it is believed connected,** which no timer did before. So a timer's reading of "not connected" that proves nothing by amendment 18 (no reading has yet shown the truck on its present link) is set aside, and the timer goes by what is believed: the trip is closed and MilO waits. Passed on, it would have put every trip on such a truck into its grace period ten minutes after it stopped.
    - **Judgements made while building, Shawn's to change:** the 30 seconds between fixes while waiting; the limit on waiting; 5 to 30 minutes as the range of the setting; and that after that limit the driving alert may show for a connected truck.

29. **The wait must not lose the drive that follows it.** Every part below is a case in which, as amendment 28 was first built, a truck that was still connected drove off with nothing recording, or a trip came out cut or misdated.
    - **The wait lasts three days, not 12 hours.** What the limit has to outlast is the truck standing at home with the phone in range: 14 hours 10 minutes for a night, going by the one day there is data for (2026-10-06: driven off at 07:18, parked at 17:08), and 62 hours over a weekend. At 12 hours MilO stopped watching in the small hours of every night, and a truck still connected in the morning started nothing. 72 hours is a judgement, Shawn's to change; its price is up to three days of the watch, whose cost has still not been measured.
    - **A trip started by hand while the hold-off stood, once parked, ends the hold-off, and MilO waits.** End with the truck connected, then Start, then a stop of the limit: the trip was closed, nothing waited, and the hold-off went on keeping every reading from starting a trip. Now the parked rule releases the hold-off in that step (the log says `TRIP_PARKED`) and the wait begins. The wait does what the hold-off was for: a reading of "connected" starts nothing. A press of End still sets the hold-off, on that trip as on any other.
    - **Android Auto holds a wait, as it holds a trip.** A trip that Android Auto alone was holding open (Bluetooth down, the phone on the cable) was closed by the parked rule with nothing waiting, and the drive after the stop was lost; before the parked rule that trip stayed open and recorded it. Now MilO waits whenever the truck's Bluetooth or Android Auto is connected, the trip service keeps its watch on Android Auto during the wait, and the wait ends when both are gone. The trip that movement starts counts as a trip in which the truck was seen, so Android Auto goes on holding it open and its disconnect starts the grace period. The 12-hour guard against a stuck Android Auto (amendment 8) applies to a wait as to a trip. Two limits: once MilO has stopped watching, Android Auto holds nothing, because nothing reports it then; and a restart of the process ends a wait that Android Auto alone was holding, because Android Auto cannot be read until the service is up.
    - **A new link that finds a trip parked starts the next trip in the same step.** A trip that stood nine minutes, lost the truck and got it back ninety seconds later was closed by the link broadcast, and MilO waited; the same broadcast delivered a second time (both receivers deliver it during a trip) ended that wait and started a trip with the sound. The outcome depended on how often the event arrived. Now the link broadcast closes the parked trip and starts the new one at once, with the sound, as every connect does, and the second delivery finds nothing to do. A reading that finds the truck back, which is no new link, still parks the trip and waits.
    - **An unreadable truck at a process start does not end a wait.** Amendment 11 counts "unknown" as "not connected" there, which is safe for a trip (it waits out a grace period) and was fatal for a wait: it ended for good with the truck still connected. Now the stored wait carries on and the next reading that can be had decides, counted as it would have counted at the start: a reconcile's "not connected" at once, the minute check's at the second in a row. Not past the wait's limit: an unreadable truck must never start a trip.
    - **Two fixes "in a row" are at most two minutes apart.** A first fix that showed the truck somewhere else waited without limit for a second. One stray fix followed by a night of fixes too poor to use would have been borne out by the first good fix of the morning, and the trip dated the evening before. Now a first fix that nothing bears out within two minutes is dropped, and the watch starts again from the parked place. (Amendment 30 keeps the dropped fix in mind as a sighting, so that the drive is not lost with it.)
    - **The trip that movement starts is told when it last moved.** Its first points are the parked place and the fixes that showed the truck leave, and it last moved at the newest of them, not at the first. Told only of the first, the rules closed a truck that stopped again at once half a minute early and a fix short.
    - **A trip that never moved is waited for at its newest usable fix.** Such a trip is cut at its start and has no end position, so the wait had no place, and the first fix of the wait stood in for it: a truck that drove off within half a minute of the close lost the stretch up to that fix.

30. **A drive must not be lost because the fixes that show it come far apart.**
    - **A second sighting that comes late still starts the trip.** Amendment 29 dropped a first fix that nothing bore out within two minutes, so that a stray fix could not date a trip hours back. As built, the fix after it was then a first sighting again, to be dropped in its turn. A truck driven off while usable fixes came more than two minutes apart was never seen to move: no trip for the whole drive, Home still saying "Truck connected and parked", and the driving alert quiet because the truck reads as connected. A reviewer showed it with the real fixes of 2026-10-06 thinned to one every 125 seconds: the 38 km drive home was not recorded. With the change it is, from four minutes after the truck drove off, and from the parked place. Now the dropped fix still cannot date the trip, but it is kept in mind as a sighting, and the next usable fix that also shows the truck away from the parked place starts the trip at once, dated at that later fix, with the parked place as its first point. A usable fix that shows the truck back at the parked place takes the sighting back. Whether this phone ever delivers the watch's fixes that far apart is not known; on 2026-10-06 its fixes during trips came 5 to 16 seconds apart. **The price:** after a gap in usable fixes, one stray fix can be enough to start a trip while the truck stands. Such a trip is closed by the parked rule ten minutes later and discarded as too short, and the wait begins again. A judgement, Shawn's to change: a missed drive is worse than a short trip that is thrown away.
    - **A wait has no End button.** Amendment 28 says what End does during a wait. The rule holds, but while MilO waits the one button on both screens reads "Start trip". End reaches a wait only when a press crosses with the trip being closed, as it did on the emulator. Whether Shawn wants a way to tell a waiting MilO not to record the next drive is a new feature, and his to ask for.
    - **With the truck connected, the 30 minutes of a restart matter only beyond the parked limit.** A stored trip whose process was dead for longer than the parked limit, and no longer than 30 minutes, is not carried on as one drive any more: time nobody watched counts as standing (decided with amendment 28), so the trip is closed where it last moved, MilO waits, and the first fixes, far from that place, start a new trip from it. Two trips, the gap counted as one straight line. The same happens when the fixes are too poor to use for longer than the limit. Each of the two trips is sorted into Business or Personal by its own start.
    - **End pressed with the truck connected, and the truck then never disconnecting, is the one state in which nothing watches a connected truck from the first minute.** No wait begins (a wait begins only when the parked rule closes a trip), no service runs, a reading of "connected" starts nothing for 12 hours, and the driving alert is quiet because the truck reads as connected. This is the hold-off as amendment 5 decided it and as it was built before the parked rule; what changed on 2026-10-06 is the knowledge that this truck can stay connected for hours. It was the state of the phone that night (End pressed at 18:03). The ways out are unchanged: a disconnect that is seen, a new link, 12 hours and then any reading (MilO opened), or Start. Whether the hold-off should become a wait, or the driving alert be shown while it stands, is Shawn's to decide.
    - **Looked at and left as decided,** each with what it looks like on the phone in APP_ENCYCLOPEDIA's gotchas: a wait found again after MilO's process was dead while the truck was driven somewhere else starts a trip that holds the straight line between the two places; the watch measures from the closed trip's end, which can be a few metres short of where the truck came to rest; a reading that still says "connected" just after a disconnect event has ended a wait starts a trip that is discarded two minutes later; and the wait's cost to the battery is still unmeasured.

31. **A parked truck drives off by speed, and a drive in another vehicle is removed.**
    - **Driving off is a usable fix away from the parked place at 15 km/h or more** (Shawn chose "Speed over 15 km/h" over "speed or distance" and over leaving it by distance). "Away" is still the distance calculation's: further than the fixes' stated error can explain. The speed is the phone's own reading, from the satellites' Doppler shift, which reads nought for a standing truck however its position scatters, so one fix with both is enough; until now it took two fixes in a row, at any speed. Walking about a site with the truck connected no longer starts a trip. A fix without a speed reading is judged as before (two in a row within two minutes, amendments 29 and 30), and the step from the fix before has to be as fast. The trip still starts at the parked place, its first stored point (his words: "we should track from where we started, not from when we started the trip"), and is dated at the first fix of the drive: of the fixes in the last two minutes before the fast one that showed the truck away, the first that did not read it as standing (under 3.6 km/h). A truck that rolled on a few metres after its trip ended and then stood is not given the time it stood.
    - **A trip that the watch started and that loses the truck for good within its first kilometre is deleted, row and points** (he chose 1 km, and "Delete for good" over keeping it as discarded). His case: "in rare occasions I am connected to my truck at work and I may use another vehicle". The phone still connected to the parked truck rides off in the other vehicle, the watch starts a trip, and the truck's Bluetooth is lost as the phone leaves its range, within a few hundred metres. "Loses the truck for good" is the grace period running out (`GRACE_EXPIRED`); the distance is the one up to the moment the truck was found gone. A trip of 1 km or more, one the parked rule closes, one ended with End, and every trip a new link, a reading or a button started are untouched. It is the first time MilO deletes a trip row: until now a row was never removed, only marked. The event log keeps one line with the times and the distance. Which open trip the watch started is stored in the settings file, so that a restart in the middle of it still knows.
    - **The price.** A real truck trip whose Bluetooth drops within its first kilometre and stays down for the whole grace period, with Android Auto not connected, is deleted. In the truck the connection stays up, and a drop is mended in seconds; if it ever happens, the log line has what is needed to add the trip again by hand. A truck that creeps for minutes under 15 km/h starts its trip when it first passes that speed, late but not short: the stretch from the parked place is counted.
